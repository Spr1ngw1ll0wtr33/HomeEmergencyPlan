# Sweden 2026 preparedness booklet: evidence extract

Evidence for a UK family home emergency plan, drawn from the current Swedish household brochure. It replaces the 2018 MSB edition used for the SE entries in `nordic-sources.md`. Text was extracted with `pdftotext -layout`; every page was also viewed as a rendered image (checklists, siren diagrams, shelter-ranking graphic and illustrations). Quotations in "double quotes" are verbatim from the English edition, including its own spellings (e.g. "roiling boil", "resucitation").

## Source

| ID | Title | Publisher | Edition / file | Pages |
|----|-------|-----------|----------------|-------|
| SE26 | *In case of crisis or war* ("Important information to all residents of Sweden") | Swedish Civil Defence and Resilience Agency (MCF, formerly MSB), distributed "to every household in Sweden on behalf of the Swedish government" | Publ. nr MCF0180, **June 2026** (ISBN 978-91-7927-776-5); PDF created 2 October 2026; `sources/europe/sweden/in-case-of-crisis-or-war-2026.pdf` | 32 |

**Page references.** Single pages (not spreads, unlike the 2018 file); PDF page numbers match the printed numbers. Pages 1–2 are the cover and inside cover, p.4 is the contents list, and pp.13–14 are an illustrated spread (shelter-ranking graphic and an illustration of a family stocking a larder) with no further text.

**Note.** The booklet states its own available formats: Swedish, Easy Swedish and English in print; other languages, Swedish sign language, audio and braille digitally (p.2). "Keep this brochure for future reference." (p.2).

## 1. Alerts, warnings and staying informed

- **SE26 p.10**: Outdoor warning: "The outdoor warning system, which uses loud sirens, is operational in most municipalities and around Sweden's nuclear power plants." Test: "at 3:00 pm (15:00) on the first non-public-holiday Monday in March, June, September and December."
- **SE26 p.10** (Public Service Announcement, PSA): "This siren sounds for 7 seconds, followed by 14 seconds of silence. The pattern repeats for 2 minutes. Go indoors, close all windows and doors and, if possible, switch off the ventilation. Listen to Swedish public broadcaster Sveriges Radio, channel P4 for more information." PSA used "for example, in case of hazardous air-quality events or fires that may emit toxic fumes or cause explosions." Broadcast via "Sveriges Radio, SVT (Swedish public service television), SVT teletext, as well as commercial radio and TV channels"; "Apps that include krisinformation.se, SOS Alarm emergency services, Sveriges Radio, and SVT"; "Emergency alerts sent with SE-Alert to mobile phones in the affected area."
- **SE26 p.11**: Emergency alarm: "The siren sounds for 30 seconds, followed by 15 seconds of silence. The pattern is repeated for 5 minutes. Go indoors. Listen to channel P4". Air raid warning: "short bursts, which sound for 1 minute. Take cover immediately". All clear: "A long, uninterrupted siren lasting 30 seconds." All four signals repeated as a tear-off-style summary on p.32.
- **SE26 p.16** (Communication checklist): "Radio powered by batteries, solar panels or a winding mechanism." "Extra batteries." Callout: "Sveriges Radio, channel P4, is the official channel for emergency information. It will continue to broadcast in the event of crisis or war. Find the frequency for your local P4 channel at krisinformation.se."
- **SE26 p.26**: "download apps that provide weather warnings; for example, krisinformation.se, the Swedish public broadcaster Sveriges Radio, and SMHI Väder. All issued warnings should be taken seriously."
- **SE26 p.28**: hearing impaired: "use the app from krisinformation.se and switch on notifications to receive Public Service Announcements (PSAs)"; visually impaired: "use text-to-speech tools or screen readers".
- **SE26 p.31**: websites mcf.se, forsvarsmakten.se, krisinformation.se, lilla.krisinformation.se.

## 2. Water

- **SE26 p.15**: "You require at least three litres of water a day, mainly for drinking and cooking. In case of a drinking-water shortage, your municipality can provide public water tanks. However, you still need to keep some water at home for emergencies."
- Checklist (p.15): "Get water storage containers or buckets with lids for collecting water." "Buy bottled water or fill up storage containers." "Store water in a dark and cool place. Check if the water tastes or smells unusual once or twice a year. Change the water when necessary." "If you are unsure if water is safe to drink, boil it until it comes to a roiling boil."
- Callout (p.15): "Store water in plastic bottles and put them in your freezer. The bottles can be used as ice packs if the electricity cuts out. When the ice has melted, you can drink the water. Don't fill the bottles all the way to the top – they may crack when frozen."
- **SE26 p.19**: evacuation list "Food and water to last a few days." **p.20**: a shelter "should have water and basic toilet facilities."
- No total stored quantity is stated. (Derived, not stated: 3 litres a day for the booklet's "at least one week" = 21 litres per person.) No container-sterilising method.

## 3. Food

- **SE26 p.17**: "You need food that is filling, energy-rich and that can be stored safely at room temperature. Get food that can be prepared quickly, requires very little water, or that can be eaten immediately. Start building up your emergency storage by simply buying one or two additional items when doing your regular shopping."
- Checklist (p.17), verbatim by category:
  - "Non-perishables: Grains, cereal, pasta, rice, couscous, instant mashed potatoes, milk powder, tortillas, crispbread, crackers, salt, and spices."
  - "Vegetables, roots and beans: Potatoes, carrots, cabbage, squash, onion, beans and lentils."
  - "Tinned goods: Tomatoes, vegetables, fruit, and ready-to-eat meals."
  - "High-protein: Dried or tinned meat and fish, chickpeas, beans, lentils, and cheese in a tube."
  - "High-fat: Cooking oil, pesto, sun-dried tomatoes in oil, tapenade, peanut butter, nuts and seeds."
  - "Energy boosts: Fruit custard, jam, chocolate, honey, protein bars, and dried fruit."
  - "Drinks: Coffee, tea, hot chocolate mix, blueberry and rosehip soup, juice, or milk."
  - "Food for children: Gruel, infant formula, oatmeal, and baby food."
- Callout (p.17): "Make use of available fruits and berries. Grow edible food in your garden, on your balcony, or on a windowsill."
- Cooking/equipment (p.18, "Other"): "Camping stove, gas burner, fuel." "Tin opener." "Matches, fire striker."
- No calorie or weight quantity is given; the duration is "at least one week" (p.15).

## 4. Heating and keeping warm without power

- **SE26 p.16**: "Your home's indoor temperature will drop quickly if the electricity cuts out during winter. Gather in one room, drape blankets over the windows, and cover the floor with rugs."
- Checklist (p.16): "Warm, all-weather outdoor clothing, clothes made of wool, thick socks, beanies, gloves, and scarves." "Blankets, sleeping mats, and sleeping bags." "Alternative heat sources that don't require electricity – for example, a gas or paraffin heater." "Candles, tea lights, and fuel for heat sources." "Matches, fire-starters, and a fire extinguisher."
- Callout (p.16): "If you are using alternative heat sources, please take the necessary precautions. Open a window to let in breathable air, and make sure to switch off heat sources before going to sleep."

## 5. Lighting and power

- **SE26 p.18** ("Other"): "Torch, headlamp." "Fuel in your car tank or a charged battery." "Matches, fire striker."
- **SE26 p.16**: "Candles, tea lights"; "Extra batteries."; "Mobile phone and a charged power bank."; "Phone charger for a car."
- Generators: not covered. Carbon monoxide: not named (only "Open a window to let in breathable air", p.16).

## 6. Communications

- **SE26 p.16**: "You must be able to receive news and important information from government authorities. You must also be able to stay in contact with family and friends." Checklist: "Mobile phone and a charged power bank." "Phone charger for a car." "Important phone numbers written down on paper."
- **SE26 p.24**: "Only call if you need to request help, so as not to overload the network." "Do not call anyone who may be in danger. You might reveal their hiding place."
- **SE26 p.31** (Important phone numbers): "112 For acute emergencies – ambulance, rescue services, and police." "114 14 For non-emergency contact with the police." "113 13 To receive or provide information about serious accidents and crises." "1177 Medical assistance if you are injured or sick."
- **SE26 p.28**: "Use your phone's accessibility features to receive the most important information."
- Family meeting point / out-of-area contact: not covered.

## 7. Cash and payments

- **SE26 p.17** (Currency): "The ability to make payments in various ways increases your emergency preparedness. You should use cash occasionally. Good to have: Around 1,000 SEK in cash at home per adult, preferably in different denominations." "Other payment options – for example, debit or credit cards and digital services."
- **SE26 p.19** (evacuation): "Photo ID, debit or credit card, and cash."

## 8. Documents and records

- **SE26 p.19** (things to bring): "Important information written on paper; for example, phone numbers and insurance information." "Photo ID".
- **SE26 p.29** (pets): "Handwritten phone number to your veterinarian, insurance information and ID number."
- **SE26 p.23**: "Perform regular backups of important information to an external hard drive, USB drive or cloud service."
- **SE26 p.28**: for those needing special assistance, "Make a crisis emergency plan that also includes important information about things like prescription medicines and other medical aids."

## 9. Medication and first aid

- **SE26 p.18** ("Other"): "Home pharmacy, first aid kit." Callout: "If you require prescription medicines or disposable products – for example, if you have diabetes – make sure to keep a month's supply at home."
- **SE26 p.6**: "Complete a course in emergency CPR (cardiopulmonary resucitation)." "Donate blood if you can."
- **SE26 p.25** (How to stop bleeding): "Make sure you and the injured person are safe before assisting them." Steps: "Phone emergency services at 112 or ask someone else to phone them." "Apply direct, firm pressure on the wound with your arms extended in an outstretched position, preferably using a bunched-up t-shirt, scarf or similar item." "If you become fatigued or require assistance, ask someone to apply pressure on top of your hands." "Maintain pressure until an ambulance arrives, and the paramedics say that you can release pressure."
- **SE26 p.19**: "Medicines and aids; for example, hearing aids."

## 10. Hygiene and sanitation

- **SE26 p.18** (Toilet): "If there's no water – for example, as the result of a power cut – you will not be able to flush your toilet. Prepare to dispose of waste in other ways and maintain good hygiene." "You may urinate in the toilet even if you can't flush it. Discard toilet paper in a garbage can or bucket with a lid." "To dispose of excrement, fit a plastic bag or garbage bag in your toilet bowl or use a portable toilet or a bucket with a lid. Cover the excrement with compost litter or sawdust. Your municipality will provide information on where to discard waste."
- Checklist (p.18): "Toilet paper." "Wet wipes, hand sanitiser." "Diapers, menstrual pads." "Plastic bags or garbage bags." "Compost litter or sawdust." "Bucket with a lid."
- Callout (p.18): "To reduce odour buildup, avoid mixing urine and excrement."
- **SE26 p.19**: "Toiletries and hygiene products." **p.27** (Pathogens): "Help reduce the spread of the disease by following the recommendations from government agencies."

## 11. Pets

- **SE26 p.29**: "You are responsible for the care and wellbeing of your pet in the event of crisis or war. Make sure you have supplies at home to last at least a week." "In the event of an air raid, you may bring your pet to protective structures like cellars, garages and metro stations. If you must leave your pet at home – and it can manage free access to food – leave additional food and water."
- Checklist (p.29): "Dry food and water in storage containers." "Medicines for the animal." "A cage or other way to transport the animal." "Handwritten phone number to your veterinarian, insurance information and ID number."
- Horses/livestock: "more information ... is available at jordbruksverket.se" (p.29). Assistance dogs: "Guide dogs or service dogs are allowed in civil defence shelters." (p.28).

## 12. Vulnerable people, neighbours and community

- **SE26 p.6**: "If a serious incident does occur, aid must be primarily focused on those who need it the most. This means that most of us must be able to manage on our own for at least a week." Ways to get involved: "Join a voluntary defence organisation"; "other non-profit organisations and faith communities"; CPR course; "Donate blood if you can."; "Talk to other people in your local community about how to strengthen your collective preparedness – for example, with neighbours in your block of flats or residential area."
- **SE26 p.15**: "You may be able to share some things with others – for example, with your neighbours. In times of crisis or war, we all must help one another."
- **SE26 p.28** (If you require special assistance): "Individuals who currently receive special assistance from their municipality will continue to receive assistance in the event of crisis or war. However, you must still observe emergency preparedness practices in accordance with your specific needs." "Talk to your family, friends, neighbours, assistants or care administrator about what help you may require." "Make a crisis emergency plan that also includes important information about things like prescription medicines and other medical aids." "Prepare for transport to a shelter or other protected areas." Accessibility points as in topic 1.
- **SE26 p.19**: "If you are unable to evacuate on your own, assistance will be provided as well as emergency living quarters and food when you arrive at a secure location."

## 13. Shelter in place (including chemical, nuclear and iodine)

- **SE26 p.10 / p.32** (PSA): "Go indoors, close all windows and doors and, if possible, switch off the ventilation. Listen to ... Sveriges Radio, channel P4". Used for "hazardous air-quality events or fires that may emit toxic fumes or cause explosions."
- **SE26 p.12** (Nuclear weapons box): "During attacks in which nuclear, chemical or biological weapons are used, take cover as you would during an air raid. Civil defence shelters provide the best protection. Radiation levels will lower drastically after a couple of days." Shelters "provide better protection than other spaces when it comes to radioactive fallout, gas from chemical weapons, and biological weapons."
- **SE26 p.11–12**: "You will have better protection indoors than outdoors, preferably in a room without windows."
- **SE26 p.13** (graphic, ranked from "Rudimentary protection" to "Most effective protection"): "Outside in a pit or ditch." "In a tunnel or next to a wall." "Indoors, in a room without windows." "Cellars, garages, or below-ground metro stations." "Civil defence shelter."
- Iodine tablets: not covered. Sealing with tape: not covered.

## 14. Evacuation with notice

- **SE26 p.19**: "You may have to quickly evacuate an area in the event of an impending military attack, natural disaster, or hazardous emissions." "Evacuation instructions will be announced through a variety of channels, including the Public Service Announcement (PSA) system. Switch on ... Sveriges Radio, channel P4, and follow instructions from government authorities." Assistance for those unable to evacuate on their own (see topic 12).
- **SE26 p.26**: P4 will say "if your area needs to be evacuated."
- Shutting off utilities: not covered. Routes / where to go (family, friends): not covered.

## 15. Fire safety and escape

- **SE26 p.16**: checklist includes "a fire extinguisher"; heat-source callout: "Open a window to let in breathable air, and make sure to switch off heat sources before going to sleep."
- **SE26 p.26**: "Find out if there is a fire ban in your area before you ignite a fire or grill."
- Smoke alarms, home escape plans: not covered.

## 16. Flooding

- **SE26 p.26**: "Extreme weather events, including heavy rain, floods and heatwaves, are becoming increasingly common." "Find information through your municipality's website or mcf.se about the risk of, and preparedness for, landslides, erosion and flooding where you live." Illustration (p.26) shows a car in floodwater. No flood-response steps.

## 17. Storms, cold and heat

- **SE26 p.5**: "Extreme weather events" listed among "Other serious threats".
- **SE26 p.26**: heatwaves named as increasingly common; "Visit mcf.se or krisinformation.se to get advice on how to prepare for extreme weather."; "Pay attention to the weather forecast and download apps that provide weather warnings"; callout: in extreme weather "switch on ... Sveriges Radio, channel P4, to get information and further instructions; for example, if you need to boil your tap water or if your area needs to be evacuated." Forest fires and fire bans (p.26).
- Cold: winter power cut (p.16, topic 4). Heat-coping advice: not covered (heatwaves named only).

## 18. Home preparation and insurance

- **SE26 p.15**: "Adapt the advice in the checklists to your specific needs and situation." "Prepare ahead of time, so you don't have to rush out when something serious happens."
- **SE26 p.26**: check local risk of "landslides, erosion and flooding where you live".
- **SE26 p.21**: "Locate shelters or other protective structures close to your home, school and workplace."
- Insurance: only as information to keep on paper (p.19) and pet insurance details (p.29). Insurance cover itself: not covered.

## 19. Car kit

- **SE26 p.16**: "Phone charger for a car." **p.18**: "Fuel in your car tank or a charged battery."
- No car kit list; car radio not mentioned.

## 20. Children

- **SE26 p.30** (Talking to children about crises and war): "Children of various ages express worry in different ways. Be present and pay attention to signs of stress or concern." "Explain the situation to the child." "Listen and invite to a conversation." "Only talk about verified information. Avoid unnecessary details." "If you don't have all the answers, be honest and admit it." "Plan activities with the child to help shift their focus."
- **SE26 p.17**: "Food for children: Gruel, infant formula, oatmeal, and baby food." **p.18**: "Diapers". **p.18**: "Games, puzzles, crosswords and books."
- **SE26 p.21**: locate shelters near "home, school and workplace". **p.31**: "Lilla.krisinformation.se Information about crisis and war suitable for children and younger audiences."
- **SE26 p.6**: municipalities "ensure that childcare and rescue services remain uninterrupted as far as possible." School arrangements: not otherwise covered.

## 21. Psychological wellbeing and coping

- **SE26 p.30** (If you are worried): "Talk to family, friends, neighbours or a mental health organisation about your feelings. It can help you feel less alone." "Get involved – for example, by helping others improve their emergency preparedness. This can give you a sense of purpose." "Take care of your personal health. Good food, sleep and physical exercise relieve stress and improve wellbeing." "Restrict your news intake. Find a level that works for you and spend more time doing things that make you feel good." "Seek professional help if you have severe anxiety."
- **SE26 p.18**: "Games, puzzles, crosswords and books."

## 22. Recommended duration of self-sufficiency

- **SE26 p.6**: "most of us must be able to manage on our own for at least a week."
- **SE26 p.15**: "You contribute to our collective preparedness if you can manage on your own for at least one week."
- **SE26 p.29**: pet supplies "to last at least a week". **p.18**: prescription medicines and disposable products "a month's supply". **p.19**: evacuation "Food and water to last a few days". **p.20**: "Be prepared to stay in the shelter for a few days."

## 23. Grab bag / evacuation bag contents

- **SE26 p.19** (Things to bring): "Make a list of essential belongings if you are required to quickly evacuate and unable to return home for a while." Checklist: "Food and water to last a few days." "Photo ID, debit or credit card, and cash." "Medicines and aids; for example, hearing aids." "Radio powered by batteries, solar panels or winding mechanism." "Warm clothing, water-resistant outerwear and extra sets of clothing." "Toiletries and hygiene products." "Mobile phone and charger." "Map, compass." "Important information written on paper; for example, phone numbers and insurance information." (Illustration shows a rucksack, flask, radio, apple and medicine blister packs.)
- **SE26 p.20** (going to a shelter): "If you have time, bring something to eat, warm clothes and toiletries."
- Framed as a list to prepare, not explicitly a pre-packed bag.

## 24. Scams, cyber and misinformation

- **SE26 p.22** (Psychological defence): "Foreign powers and others outside Sweden use disinformation, misinformation and propaganda to influence us." Methods: "Spreading lies, false narratives, or stories that are partially true, but taken out of context." "Manufacturing fake images, videos or voice recordings." "Attempts to elicit strong emotions related to certain issues or events to heighten a sense of worry or suspicion towards each other." What to do: "Be mindful of content that elicits a strong reaction." "Only share information you know comes from reliable sources." "Try to verify information from several different sources." "Get verification from official government sources when something serious has happened."
- **SE26 p.23** (Digital security): "Create strong passwords that use a combination of letters, numbers and symbols." "Don't click on links in e-mails, or open attachments from unknown senders." "Install security updates immediately." "Perform regular backups of important information to an external hard drive, USB drive or cloud service."
- **SE26 p.24**: "Do not spread rumours or unverified information about what is happening."
- **SE26 p.5**: "If Sweden is attacked, we will never surrender. Any suggestion to the contrary is false." Crisis fraud specifically: not covered (phishing links are, p.23).

## 25. Keeping the plan and kit up to date

- **SE26 p.15**: stored water: "Check if the water tastes or smells unusual once or twice a year. Change the water when necessary."
- **SE26 p.17**: build up gradually, "buying one or two additional items when doing your regular shopping"; **p.17**: "You should use cash occasionally."
- **SE26 p.10**: siren test dates (quarterly). **p.2**: "Keep this brochure for future reference." **p.23**: "Install security updates immediately"; "regular backups".
- No fixed review interval for the whole kit.

## 26. War, military and hybrid threats (context only)

- **SE26 p.3/p.5**: "Armed conflicts are currently being waged in our corner of the world." "Military threat levels are increasing. We must be prepared for the worst-case scenario – an armed attack on Sweden." Hybrid threats: "cyber attacks, disinformation campaigns, terrorism, and sabotage. These types of attacks may occur at any time. Some are happening here and now." Other threats: extreme weather, "Dangerous pathogens", "Outages across important IT systems", "Organised crime".
- **SE26 p.7**: total defence = military plus civil defence; "Sweden is also part of NATO's collective defence."
- **SE26 p.8–9**: heightened state of alert; total defence duty "From the year you turn 16 until the end of the year you turn 70"; "During a heightened state of alert, go to work as usual unless you are assigned a specific wartime posting."
- **SE26 p.12–14, p.20–21**: air raid shelter hierarchy; "You are not designated a specific civil defence shelter – evacuate to the nearest one"; online shelter map at mcf.se; shelters must be made ready "within 48 hours"; sign "a blue triangle inside an orange rectangle" (marked "SKYDDSRUM" in the image, p.20); "If you are outside and don't have time to take cover, you must lie down on the ground. Preferably in a small pit or ditch."
- **SE26 p.24** (Terror attacks): "Run Get far away from the location." "Hide Lock yourself in a room or hide if you can't run away. Put your phone on silent mode." "Tell Call 112 and report what has happened."
- **SE26 p.27**: pathogens: follow authority recommendations.

**Context:** Mostly not transferable to UK household planning, but the Run–Hide–Tell sequence matches UK counter-terrorism advice, and "do not call anyone who may be in danger" is generally applicable.

### Checklists

- **Food list rewritten** (p.17): now grouped by category (non-perishables, vegetables/roots/beans, tinned, high-protein, high-fat, energy boosts, drinks, food for children). New items include couscous, salt and spices, squash, onion, pesto, sun-dried tomatoes in oil, tapenade, peanut butter, fruit custard, protein bars, dried fruit, hot chocolate mix, milk, and a children's food line (gruel, infant formula, oatmeal, baby food). Dropped named items: eggs, rusks, soft whey cheese, oat/soy milk, hard cheese, hummus, tins of bolognese sauce, mackerel ("makerel"), sardines, ravioli, salmon balls, boiled meat, soup, fruit purée, marmalade, almonds. New tips: build up "one or two additional items" per shop; grow food on a garden, balcony or windowsill.
- **Heat** (p.16): adds "thick socks", "fuel for heat sources" and **"a fire extinguisher"**. "Build a den under a table" dropped. "Air the room regularly to let in oxygen" becomes "Open a window to let in breathable air". LPG heaters no longer named (gas or paraffin heater remains).
- **Communication** (p.16): "a car radio" dropped; the P4 frequency lookup at krisinformation.se added.
- **Toilet** (p.18): expanded from one sentence into a full section: urinate in the unflushed toilet, bin the paper, bag or bucket for excrement, **cover with compost litter or sawdust**, keep urine and excrement separate, municipality advises on disposal.
- **Other** (p.18): "spirit stove" becomes "Camping stove, gas burner, fuel"; adds **tin opener**, games/puzzles/crosswords/books, and "or a charged battery" for electric cars.
- **Documents**: the 2018 home item "paper printouts of information such as insurance policies, bank details, registration certificates" is **dropped**; only "phone numbers and insurance information" on paper survives, in the evacuation list (p.19).
- **Evacuation list** (p.19) is new and longer: food and water "to last a few days", photo ID, cards and cash, medicines and aids (hearing aids), radio, warm and waterproof clothing plus spare sets, toiletries, phone and charger, **map and compass**, paper information. 2018 had only one sentence (warm clothes, food and drink, ID).

### New topics

- How to stop severe bleeding (p.25); CPR course and blood donation (p.6).
- Talking to children about crises and war (p.30); children's website (p.31).
- If you are worried: five coping tips, including restricting news intake (p.30).
- Pets (p.29), including taking pets into shelters and assistance dogs in shelters (p.28).
- If you require special assistance: personal crisis plan, accessibility features, transport to shelters (p.28).
- Digital security: passwords, phishing links, updates, backups (p.23).
- Extreme weather (p.26): heatwaves, erosion, fire bans, weather-warning apps; pathogens (p.27).

### Changed

- Warning system renamed IPA to **PSA**; durations now stated (PSA repeats for 2 minutes; emergency alarm for 5 minutes); SE-Alert, apps and commercial broadcasters added; "if possible, switch off the ventilation".
- Emergency alarm action changed from 2018 "Get ready to leave home with that which is most important..." to "Go indoors. Listen to channel P4" and report to any wartime posting (p.11).
- Source-checking: the 2018 six-question checklist is replaced by four shorter actions plus a description of manipulation methods, including fake images, video and voice recordings (p.22).
- Shelter: "Write down ... the closest shelter" becomes "Locate shelters ... close to your home, school and workplace" plus an online map (p.21); "be prepared to stay in the shelter for a few days" and the 48-hour readiness rule are new (p.20).
- "All information to the effect that resistance is to cease is false" becomes "If Sweden is attacked, we will never surrender. Any suggestion to the contrary is false." (p.5).
- Publisher renamed from MSB to MCF; website dinsäkerhet.se replaced by mcf.se. Phone numbers 112, 113 13, 114 14 and 1177 appear in both editions.

### Dropped

## Ideas worth borrowing for a UK household

Points from SE26 that typical UK household guidance does not spell out (check against `uk-sources.md` before relying on them):

1. **One-week baseline** for the household (p.15), with pets also stocked for a week (p.29): a useful upper target alongside the UK's shorter recommendations.
2. **A month's supply of prescription medicines and disposables** (e.g. diabetes supplies) (p.18), where repeat-prescription rules allow.
3. **Twice-yearly water check:** taste and smell stored water "once or twice a year" and change it when necessary (p.15). Tie it to the clock-change weekends.
4. **Freezer water bottles as ice packs**, not filled to the top (p.15).
5. **A complete dry-toilet method:** urinate in the unflushed toilet, bag or bucket for solids, cover with sawdust or compost litter, keep urine and solids separate to cut odour (p.18). Add a bag of sawdust or cat litter to the kit.
6. **A cash figure per adult** (around 1,000 SEK, p.17) and the habit of using cash occasionally; a UK household could set its own per-adult amount in mixed notes and coins.
7. **Fire extinguisher and tin opener on the checklist** (p.16, p.18), easy items to forget.
8. **Food list by nutrient group** (p.17), including high-fat items (oil, pesto, peanut butter, nuts) for energy density, and a separate line for infant and child food. Build it up "one or two additional items" per shop.
9. **Evacuation list with map and compass**, spare clothes and paper copies of phone numbers and insurance details (p.19).
10. **Stop-the-bleed steps** in the plan (p.25): direct firm pressure with arms straight, swap in a helper's hands on top, keep pressure until paramedics say stop.
11. **Talking to children:** explain, listen, stick to verified facts, admit what you do not know, plan distracting activities (p.30).
12. **Worry management:** limit news intake, keep sleep, food and exercise, get involved in helping others (p.30).
13. **Personal crisis plan for anyone needing care**, listing medicines and aids, agreed with carers and neighbours (p.28); turn on phone accessibility and emergency-alert notifications.
14. **Digital hygiene as preparedness:** backups to an external drive or cloud, prompt updates, no unknown links (p.23).
15. **Know where the nearest protective places are** from home, school and work, and that an inner room without windows is better than one with them (p.11–13, p.21). For the UK, read this as the inner-room advice for shelter-in-place.
16. **Run, Hide, Tell, then do not ring people who may be hiding** (p.24), which matches and slightly extends UK advice.
17. **Weather-warning apps and fire-ban checks before lighting a barbecue** (p.26).
