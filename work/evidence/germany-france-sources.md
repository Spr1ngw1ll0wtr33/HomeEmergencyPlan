So the FR kit is both a stay-at-home kit and a grab bag.

## 1. Alerts, warnings and staying informed (radio, sirens, apps)

- **DE p22:** "Information and warnings are important for survival." TV and internet "only work when there is electric power, which could fail. This leaves just the radio as the main warning device. Therefore, always keep a battery-powered radio with VHF and digital reception and spare batteries or a wind-up radio in the house."
- **DE p23:** official warnings online at www.bbk.bund.de or the BBK's NINA app. Radio tips: "You need a battery-powered radio or a wind-up radio with VHF and digital reception. A solar-powered radio or car radio can also be used. There are also wind-up radios with a USB connection for charging mobiles." "Keep sufficient batteries on hand. Remember that batteries do not last indefinitely. Use the batteries up and replace them regularly." "Ensure that you have a supply of the right battery sizes." Caption: mobile phones "will only continue to work for a short time" during a power cut.
- **DE pp24–25:** NINA (Emergency Information and Warning App) with push alerts, integrated into the Modular Warning System (MoWaS); also includes German Meteorological Service (DWD) warnings and water levels; mobile website www.warnung.bund; BBK on Twitter @BBK_Bund.
- **DE p36 (checklist, RADIO):** "Radio with VHF and digital reception, suitable for battery operation or a wind up radio"; "Spare batteries".
- **DE p41:** "Pay attention to the severe weather warnings in the media" (www.dwd.de). **p56:** "Follow the latest weather reports and flood warnings on the radio, Internet or by teletext." **p61, p63:** pay attention to announcements "on the radio, television or from loudspeaker vehicles". **p64:** "Turn the radio (VHF, regional stations) or the television on to get information."
- **DE: sirens** are not mentioned. Loudspeaker vehicles are mentioned (pp61, 63).
- **FR p1:** "Battery radio (with spare batteries) in order to follow the instructions of the authorities" (translated). No apps or sirens.

**Summary:** Both put a battery radio with spare batteries first. DE adds wind-up/solar radios, a warning app and websites. Neither mentions sirens. No disagreement.

## 2. Water (litres/person/day, storage, purification, duration)

- **DE p11:** "If need be, a person can manage for three weeks without food, but only for four days without liquid." "Keep approx. 14 litres of liquid per person in stock for each week." "Suitable drinks are mineral water, fruit juices, drinks which can be kept for a long time." (This works out at 2 litres a day.)
- **DE p34 (10-day checklist table):** Drinks, quantity "20 litres" per person for 10 days. Remarks: "An additional amount was added to the suggested supply of drinks, so that water is also available for the preparation of food such as pasta, rice or potatoes, independent of the public drinking water supply."
- **DE p13 (water for hygiene):** "In the event of a prolonged disruption of the water supply, you should collect water in every available larger container: bathtub, washbasins, buckets, pans, water canisters etc. Also remember water for flushing the toilet." "Use the water sparingly." Use disposable cutlery and crockery so no water is used for washing up. "Make the water last for longer with sterilisation agents. You can obtain these in camping stores."
- **DE:** boiling water is not mentioned. No per-day hygiene water figure is given.
- **FR p1:** "Drinking water in quantity (6 litres per person in bottles)" (translated), for the 72-hour kit (so 2 litres a day).

**Summary:** Both work out at about 2 litres of drinking liquid per person per day (DE: 14 L a week and 20 L for 10 days including cooking water; FR: 6 L for 72 h). They agree. Only DE covers filling the bath and containers and using sterilisation tablets.

## 3. Food (duration, types, quantities, cooking without power)

- **DE p10:** "With a supply of food and drink to last you 10 days, you are ready for this." (see www.ernaehrungsvorsorge.de)
- **DE p11 (tips for stockpiling):** "No experiments. Your supplies should primarily consist of food and drinks that you and your family normally use." Make sure food keeps without refrigeration and "that the majority of your supplies can also be eaten cold." Check best-before dates; mark food that has no label with the purchase date; store food "in a cool, dry and dark place" in airtight packaging; "Newly purchased supplies go to the 'back' of the shelf. Use up the older food first." Frozen food "can easily be consumed in the event of an electric power breakdown. Do not refreeze food that has been defrosted once." Cooking: "camping stoves etc." Remember special food "e.g. for diabetics, allergy sufferers or babies" and pets.
- **DE p34:** "Your goal must be to survive for 10 days without shopping." The table shows "an example of the basic supplies for one person for 10 days. This corresponds to approx. 2,200 kcal per day". It says to allow for tastes, diets and allergies.
- **DE pp34–35, 10-DAY SUPPLY per person (verbatim):**
  - Drinks: **20 litres**
  - Cereals, cereal products, bread, potatoes, pasta, rice: **3.5 kg**
  - Vegetables, pulses: **4.0 kg**. Remark: "Remember that vegetables and pulses in a jar or in cans have already been cooked and that water is also required for dried products."
  - Fruit, nuts: **2.5 kg**. Remark: "Stock up on fruit in cans or jars and only use storable fruit as fresh fruit."
  - Milk, milk products: **2.6 kg**
  - Fish, meat, eggs or whole egg powder: **1.5 kg**. Remark: "fresh eggs can only be stored for a limited time; whole egg powder, on the other hand, keeps for several years"
  - Fats, oils: **0.357 kg**
  - Other as desired, e.g. "Sugar, sweetener, honey, marmalade, chocolate, iodised salt, convenience foods (e.g. ravioli, dried tortellini, instant soups), dry potato products (e.g. mashed potato), flour, instant broth, cocoa powder, hard biscuits, pretzel sticks"
  - p35 also links to the Federal Ministry of Food and Agriculture "provisions calculator" (www.ernaehrungsvorsorge.de/de/private-vorsorge/notvorrat/vorratskalkulator/).
- **DE p17 (no cooking in the kitchen):** "You can prepare smaller meals on a camping stove." "Use an outdoor barbecue or table-top grill, which is operated with charcoal or gas. Caution! Do not grill food in the apartment or house – there is a risk of suffocation!"
- **DE p37 (checklist, POWER FAILURE):** "Camping, spirit stove with fuel".
- **DE p21:** emergency rucksack: "Food for 2 days in dust-proof packaging".
- **FR p1:** "Non-perishable food that needs no cooking (tinned food, baby food jars, pet food…)" (translated). No quantities. No cooking equipment except the multi-tool and tin opener (see 23).

**Summary:** DE gives a detailed 10-day, about 2,200 kcal/day per-person list and allows camping-stove or outdoor cooking. FR gives no quantities and specifies food that needs no cooking for 72 h. They differ on duration (10 days against 72 h) but not on approach: both favour long-life food that can be eaten without cooking.

## 4. Heating and keeping warm without power

- **DE p16:** heating may fail when "electric power, gas, oil or long-distance heating" fail. Power cuts are "generally rectified in a few hours. However, in emergency situations, it may even be days".
- **DE p17 (the heating fails):** "Warm clothing can be used as a substitute for heating for a while. Anyone who has a fireplace or a stove should have a supply of coal, briquettes or wood in the house."
- **DE p37 (checklist, POWER FAILURE):** "Heater"; "Fuel" (the type of heater is not specified).
- **DE p31:** "Also allow for the possibility of operating the entire heating system via emergency power."
- **DE p39 (emergency pack checklist):** "Woollen blanket, sleeping bag"; "Hard-wearing, warm clothing".
- **FR p1:** "Warm clothes and survival blanket" (translated), i.e. a foil emergency blanket.

**Summary:** Both rely on warm clothing and blankets. DE adds stored solid fuel for a fireplace or stove and a heater. No disagreement.

## 5. Lighting and power (torches, candles, batteries, power banks, generators, carbon monoxide)

- **DE p17 (the lights are off):** "Keep a supply of candles and torches (e. g. a twist torch or solar and LED lights), as well as spare lamps, batteries, matches or lighters, candles etc. in the house."
- **DE p17 (more tips):** "Make sure that the batteries in computers, mobile telephones, telephones etc. are charged. Solar-powered battery chargers may help."
- **DE p37 (checklist, POWER FAILURE):** "Candles, tea lights"; "Matches, lighter"; "Torch"; "Spare batteries"; "Camping, spirit stove with fuel"; "Heater"; "Fuel".
- **DE p23:** wind-up radios with USB charging for mobiles.
- **DE: generators.** p30: "If you live in a floodplain, you should consider having a small, powerful emergency generator (submersible pump!)". p31: heating system run via emergency power.
- **DE: carbon monoxide** is not named. The nearest warnings are p17 "Do not grill food in the apartment or house – there is a risk of suffocation!" and p64 (sheltering) "Avoid the unnecessary consumption of oxygen by candles or similar." p47: "Never leave naked flames, such as candles or fire, unattended."
- **DE: power banks** are not mentioned by that name (only solar chargers and USB wind-up radios).
- **FR p1:** "Torch (with spare batteries), candles, lighter or matches" (translated); "Mobile phone charger" (translated). FR also says "Replace the batteries" during the annual check (translated).

**Summary:** Both list a torch, spare batteries, candles and matches or a lighter. DE adds solar chargers, a generator for floodplain homes, and warnings about indoor grills and naked flames, though it never names carbon monoxide. Neither mentions power banks explicitly. No disagreement.

## 6. Communications (phones, contact lists, meeting points)

- **DE p33 (plan together):** "Discuss escape routes, meeting points and their accessibility; it is possible that not all members of the family will be at home in the event of a disaster." It also says to enter local emergency numbers "in the telephone list on the back of this brochure".
- **DE p68 (back cover, "Important telephone numbers"):** Fire brigade, rescue service 112; Police 110; Telephone number for public authorities 115; then blank lines for Medical emergency service, Ambulance service, Poison information centre, Standby pharmacy and Public utility company. "In an emergency say: Where is the site of the emergency? What has happened? Who is calling? Wait for questions!"
- **DE pp26–27:** 112 is free across Europe, including from a mobile phone with a SIM. Steps: secure the scene, life-saving measures, call 112, give the details above, give first aid, and direct rescuers if the house number is hard to find.
- **DE p56:** plan "The possibilities for communicating with neighbours or other people if the telephone and mobile network are down. Discuss distress and danger signals with neighbours." and "The 'distribution of roles' in an emergency. Who will do what?"
- **DE p23:** mobile phones work only for a short time in a power cut. **p64:** "Only make telephone calls in emergencies."
- **DE p61:** note the number of your local poison information centre on the back of the brochure.
- **FR p1:** "Mobile phone charger" (translated). No contact list or meeting point.

**Summary:** DE covers meeting points, a printed phone list and pre-agreed signals with neighbours. FR only includes a phone charger. No disagreement.

## 7. Cash and payments

- **DE p17:** "Remember to have sufficient cash reserves in the house, as cash machines also no longer work during an electric power breakdown."
- **DE p21 (do not forget):** "Identity documents, money, valuables".
- **FR p1:** "Cash (as cash machines may not work)" (translated).

**Summary:** Both say to keep cash because cash machines may fail. No quantity given by either. Agreement.

## 8. Documents and records (the German "document folder")

- **DE p18:** "Put all the important documents together and store them in one place, in a briefcase that you can easily pick up and take with you. In the event of an emergency, all the members of your family should know the location of the briefcase." Tip: "Deposit duplicates of important documents with friends, relatives, notaries, solicitors or banks."
- **DE p19, "The document folder should include":**
  - IN THE ORIGINAL: "Family documents (birth, marriage, death certificates) or family register"
  - IN THE ORIGINAL OR AS A CERTIFIED COPY: "Savings books, account agreements, shares, bonds, insurance policies"; "Pension and income statements, income tax returns"; "Evidence of qualifications: certificates (school certificates, university certificates, certificates of additional qualifications)"; "Contracts and amendment agreements, e.g. including rent agreements, lease agreements etc."; "Will, living will and power of attorney"
  - AS A SIMPLE COPY: "ID, passport, driving licence and vehicle documents"; "Land register excerpts"; "All the amendment notices for benefits received"; "Payment receipts for insurance premiums, especially from the social insurance pension fund"; "Proof of registration with the employment offices, communications from the Employment Agency"; "Invoices which provide evidence of outstanding payment claims"; "Membership or subscription registers of associations, clubs or other organisations"; "International certificate of vaccination"
- **DE p38 (checklist, DOCUMENT SECURITY):** "Have you established which documents are absolutely essential?"; "Are your documents organised appropriately?"; "Are copies available of important documents, and are these certified if necessary?"; "Have you created a document folder?"; "Are the document folder or important documents ready to hand?"
- **DE p33:** keep the brochure with the briefcase. **p41:** document your property with photos. **p47:** keep all important documents in a briefcase (fire section).
- **FR p1:** "Photocopies of essential documents in a waterproof pouch (identity card, prescriptions…)" (translated).

**Summary:** Both say to keep copies of key documents ready to take. DE gives a detailed tiered list (originals, certified copies, simple copies) and suggests leaving duplicates with third parties. FR specifies photocopies in a waterproof pouch, including prescriptions. Minor difference: DE keeps some originals in the folder, while FR mentions only photocopies.

## 9. Medication and first aid (home pharmacy list)

- **DE p14:** "Regularly check the drugs in your medicine cabinet. In the case of drugs without an expiry date, you should make a note of the purchase date. Expired medicines belong in the bin." Expired drugs "may not only lose their effect, but also become harmful."
- **DE p15 (storage):** lockable cupboard or box; "not accessible to children (put it high up or lock it)"; "Choose a sparingly heated, dry room. Note: the bathroom is the wrong place!"; ideally "a freely accessible first aid box and a lockable drug box".
- **DE p15, "It must contain these things":** "Personal drugs prescribed by a doctor"; "Cold remedies"; "Painkillers and antipyretic drugs"; "Remedies against diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting"; "Remedies for insect bites and sunburn"; "Electrolytes to compensate for a loss of fluid"; "Thermometer"; "Tweezers"; "Skin disinfectant"; "Wound disinfectant"; "Dressing materials. Everything that a DIN 13164 first aid kit (car first aid kit) contains: Gauze compress, Bandage scissors, Sticking plasters and bandages, Triangle bandage".
- **DE p36 (checklist, MEDICINE CABINET):** "DIN first aid kit"; "Drugs prescribed by the doctor"; "Painkillers"; "Skin disinfectants"; "Wound disinfectants"; "Cold remedies"; "Thermometer"; "Remedies against diarrhoea"; "Cream for insect bites and sunburn"; "Tweezers".
- **DE pp26–27:** take a first aid course again; providers listed (German Red Cross, Malteser, Johanniter, ASB, DLRG).
- **DE p21 / p39:** emergency pack: "First aid materials, personal medication"; "Personal medication"; "Materials for treating wounds".
- **FR p1:** "Medicines" (translated); "First aid kit (alcohol, plasters, compresses…)" (translated); "Glasses (spare pair)" (translated); prescriptions copied with documents. Annual check "in particular the use-by date of medicines" (translated).

**Summary:** Both include personal medicines and a first aid kit and stress checking expiry dates. DE gives a full home-medicine list and storage advice, and FR uniquely adds spare glasses. No disagreement.

## 10. Hygiene and sanitation (incl. toilets without water)

- **DE p12:** "Even if water is scarce: hand washing is very important for hygiene and health, in spite of everything!"
- **DE p13:** collect water, including "water for flushing the toilet". Tips: "Keep a sufficient supply of soap, detergents, toothpaste, wet wipes and toilet paper." "If there is a shortage of water, a camping toilet with a substitute fluid is a good alternative." "Use household gloves." "Use hand disinfectants." "Use kitchen paper for wiping, instead of wiping things with a wet cloth." "Rubbish bags are good for quick waste disposal. They also help if the waste is not taken away for some time".
- **DE p37 (checklist, HYGIENE PRODUCTS, with quantity prompts):** "Soap (cakes), detergent (kg)"; "Toothbrush (number), toothpaste (tubes)"; "Sets of disposable cutlery and crockery (number)"; "Kitchen paper (rolls)"; "Toilet paper (rolls)"; "Rubbish bags (number)"; "Camping toilet, replacement bags (number)"; "Household gloves (pairs)"; "Disinfectants, soft soap (quantity)".
- **DE p55:** for floods, have "a camping toilet" ready. **p21:** "Clothing and hygiene products for a couple of days" in the rucksack.
- **FR:** Not covered (no hygiene items on the poster).

**Summary:** DE covers hygiene thoroughly, including a camping toilet with bags and saving water by using disposables. FR does not cover hygiene. This is a gap in FR, not a disagreement.

## 11. Pets

- **DE p11:** "Do you have any pets? Cover their needs!"
- **DE p43:** "Pets or livestock are frightened by severe weather. Try to calm your animals down and ensure that they are not able to leave the protected area."
- **DE p56:** plan early for "The evacuation of pets."
- **FR p1:** "pet food" is listed under non-perishable food (translated).

**Summary:** Both include pet food or pet needs. DE also covers calming animals and evacuating pets. Agreement.

## 12. Vulnerable people, neighbours, community

- **DE p7:** "If you can help yourself and your neighbours, you are definitely at an advantage." Rescue workers "cannot be everywhere".
- **DE p11:** special food "for diabetics, allergy sufferers or babies".
- **DE p48:** "You have a disability or a companion who is disabled? Ask about support with evacuation." **p49:** for deaf people, smoke alarms "with a flashing light or a vibration alarm".
- **DE p56:** "The care of sick people or people in need of assistance. Can you evacuate these people from the danger zone on time? Where to?"; agree distress and danger signals with neighbours; distribute roles.
- **DE p63:** sheltering: "Temporarily take in endangered passers-by." "Inform – if necessary – other occupants of the building." **p58:** "Help others, but don't put yourself in danger."
- **DE p66:** mentions volunteering ("honorary posts") among the children's modules; p25 the BBK Twitter covers "honorary posts".
- **FR p1:** Not covered beyond baby food (see 20).

**Summary:** DE covers neighbours, people with disabilities and the sick, and taking in passers-by. FR does not cover this topic.

## 13. Shelter in place (incl. chemical/nuclear/iodine)

- **DE p61:** CBRN substances may be gas, vapour, aerosol, liquid or solid; "A lay person cannot generally recognise the danger." Report to 112 or the poison information centre. "As most gases and vapours are heavier than air, they may accumulate in depressions or cellars. When chemicals are released, such places should, therefore, be avoided."
- **DE p62:** biological: use "at least provisional respiratory protection ... e.g. dust mask". Radioactive: "In cellars, the weakening effect is particularly great due to the adjacent layer of earth and the higher floors."
- **DE p63 (indoors):** "Stay in the building." "Temporarily take in endangered passers-by." "Close windows and doors." "Turn off fans and air conditioning systems; close the ventilation slots in the window frames." "Seek out a protected internal room in your apartment, ideally one which has no outside windows."
- **DE p64 (indoors):** "In the event that radioactive substances are released, seek out a cellar from preference." "Avoid the unnecessary consumption of oxygen by candles or similar." Radio (VHF, regional stations) or TV; follow the authorities; "Only make telephone calls in emergencies."; use respiratory protection, "if necessary an improvised face mask (surgical masks, cloths…)".
- **DE p64 (outside):** "Move across the direction of the wind, if possible"; breathe through respiratory protection "at least through a handkerchief"; "Seek out the nearest closed building." If contaminated, change outer clothing and shoes, bag them and place them outside the living area; "First wash your hands thoroughly, then your face and hair, as well as your nose and ears, with soap and water." Caption: "Every household should have a respiratory protection mask."
- **DE p65:** biological release: also disinfect hands. In the car: "Switch off the ventilation and close the windows", listen to the radio, and go to the nearest closed building.
- **DE p21 / p39:** dust mask or damp cloths; "Protective mask, provisional respiratory protection".
- **DE: iodine (potassium iodide) tablets are Not covered.** The only "iodised" reference is iodised salt in the food list (p35).
- **FR:** Not covered. The kit supports staying at home ("rester chez vous") but gives no shelter-in-place instructions.

**Summary:** DE gives a full "go in, stay in, tune in"-style procedure for chemical and radiological releases, with the nuance of a cellar for radiation but avoiding cellars for chemicals. Neither source covers iodine. FR does not cover this topic.

## 14. Evacuation with notice (what to take, shutting off utilities, routes, where to go)

- **DE p8:** "If you and your family have to be evacuated, you cannot be just starting to pack your emergency pack."
- **DE p20:** causes such as "a leak in the gas pipe, a fire in the house next door, ammunition from the World War II has been discovered"; "it may be a long time before you are allowed back into your home." Keep the pack packed.
- **DE p21:** what to take (see 23).
- **DE p33:** discuss escape routes and meeting points.
- **DE p56:** plan early how to evacuate sick people ("Where to?") and pets, and who does what.
- **DE p55 (flood with notice):** move valuables to higher rooms, secure the oil tank, and keep hazardous chemicals out of reach of water.
- **DE p57 (utilities):** "Turn off electrical appliances and radiators in rooms which may fill up with water... Turn off the electric power completely, if necessary (fuse off)." "Drive your car out of endangered garages". **p52 (fire):** turn off power in the danger zone.
- **DE:** shutting off gas or water mains is not covered. Where to go (rest centres, friends or family) is not covered.
- **FR p1:** the kit "will also be very useful if you have to leave in a hurry" (translated); "Spare keys for the house and the car" (translated). No routes or shut-off advice.

**Summary:** DE covers what to pack, planning routes and meeting points, and switching off electricity in floods. Neither covers gas or water shut-off or destinations. FR covers only the kit. No disagreement.

## 15. Fire safety and escape (incl. extinguishers, smoke alarms)

- **DE p47:** "Install smoke alarms in all the rooms, apart from the kitchen and the bathroom." Have a fire extinguisher, learn to use it and have it serviced; put one in the car (GDV records "16,000 car fires (fire, explosion, heat damage) per year"). "Never leave naked flames, such as candles or fire, unattended." Remove flammable materials from the cellar and attic; "Replace defective appliances and old extension leads."
- **DE p48:** find the nearest staircase ("Do not use lifts!"); know the extinguisher locations; memorise escape routes in hotels and cinemas; keep hallways clear; "Doors in escape routes must be kept closed – but never locked."
- **DE p49:** smoke alarms should be certified VdS and "DIN EN 14604", plus "the Q-label (higher quality)"; fit them "horizontally on the ceiling, as smoke rises". Locations: "Bedrooms; Children's rooms; Living and dining rooms; Halls and staircases; Cellars and attics". "No smoke alarms in the bathroom and the kitchen! Steam triggers a false alarm." Radio-interlinked alarms are an option, as are flashing or vibrating alarms for deaf people.
- **DE p50:** households should have "fire extinguishers and fire extinguishing spray"; extinguishers only help "in the development phase of a fire"; "do not put yourself in danger". Equipment for small fires: "Fire extinguishers; Extinguishing spray; Watering can, bucket; Garden hose".
- **DE pp51–53 (what to do, numbered steps):** "the safety of people always takes precedence over any fire-fighting measures!" 1. Fire just starting: only tackle it if not in danger; "Never extinguish burning fat or other liquid fuels with water"; put a lid on the pan, use Class F extinguishers; turn off power first; extinguish "from the bottom to the top and from the side to the middle"; "Never enter rooms that are filled with smoke!"; crawl along the floor; close the door and call 112. 2. If extinguishing is not possible: close the windows if safe, close the door of the fire room. 3. Call 112. 4. Warn and protect people. 5. Brief the fire brigade. 6. Everyone out: "Close all the doors, but do not lock them"; keep keys ready for the emergency services. 7. Check everyone is safe, because "If someone is missing, the fire brigade must assume that they are still in the building". Take practice alarms seriously.
- **DE p38 (checklist, FIRE PROTECTION):** "Clear out cellar and attic"; "Fire extinguisher, fire protection"; "Extinguishing spray"; "Smoke alarm"; "Garden or car washing hose"; "Container for water for extinguishing fires"; "Water bucket"; "Stirrup pump or bucket pump".
- **FR:** Not covered.

**Summary:** DE covers fire prevention, alarms, extinguishers and a seven-step response in detail. FR does not cover fire. (Note: the DE advice to put smoke alarms in all rooms except kitchens and bathrooms concerns smoke alarms only; UK practice is to fit a heat alarm in the kitchen. DE does not mention heat alarms.)

## 16. Flooding

- **DE p55 (well prepared):** "Obtain sandbags, lining boards, water-resistant plywood boards and silicone for protection." Keep hazardous materials away from the water; move valuables and computers upstairs; anchor or weight the fuel oil tank; obtain "sufficient food and drinking water, a battery-powered radio or a wind-up radio, a torch, a camping stove and a camping toilet"; keep the document briefcase ready; "Remember insect repellent". Tip: find out the critical high-water mark from the local authority (www.hochwasserzentralen.de).
- **DE p56:** follow flood warnings; plan for the sick, for pets, for communication if networks are down, and for roles.
- **DE p57 (indoors):** "Remain calm." Clear out cellars; check backwater valves; "Do not stay in the cellar during the flood, since you will be putting your life at risk."; seal windows, doors and drains; switch off appliances, radiators and power ("fuse off"). **In the car:** move the car out; "Basement garages can become death traps"; "Do not drive through flooded streets"; have the vehicle towed if water is "up to above the wheels".
- **DE p58:** help others safely; get children out of the floodplain first; no boat "rides"; keep away from banks; follow barriers. After the flood: pump out only once the groundwater has fallen.
- **DE p59:** "Take stock and photograph the damages for the insurance company."; clear silt; dry rooms quickly to avoid mould (heaters can be rented); have the electrics, oil tank and structure checked; call the fire brigade for released hazardous substances; "Do not eat any fruit, vegetables and salad from flooded areas."
- **DE pp30–31 (structural):** backwater valves, lifting units, a submersible pump with a sump, tiled basements, separate switchable circuits for at-risk floors, flood-safe meter cabinets, an emergency generator for floodplain homes, and fuel tanks secured against floating.
- **DE p45:** risk of electric shock in flooded cellars; use appliances only if they are dry.
- **FR:** Not covered (the intro mentions "impassable roads" only).

**Summary:** DE gives full before, during and after flood guidance. FR does not cover flooding.

## 17. Storms, cold, heat

- **DE p41 (well prepared):** candles, torches and a VHF radio with batteries; an emergency pack with documents; photograph your property; watch DWD warnings. "In severe weather, you should not stay outside. Avoid danger zones such as forests."
- **DE p42 (outside):** avoid unprotected places; shelter in a building, avoiding large-span halls; secure garden furniture and bicycles; in hail with no shelter, lie face down and protect your head and neck. Thunderstorm: crouch on the balls of your feet in a hollow; avoid open country, peaks, trees, towers and masts; "Keep a minimum distance of 50 metres from power lines"; avoid metal objects such as umbrellas and bicycles.
- **DE p43:** "In your car, you are safe from thunderstorms." At home: close windows and shutters, go to a low or internal room or the cellar; "Cars, caravans and lightweight buildings are not necessarily safe during a storm."; unplug sensitive devices or use surge protection; calm pets.
- **DE pp44–45 (after):** document damage before tidying up; first aid and 112; call 112 for released fuel oil; beware of electric shock in flooded cellars; stay out of badly damaged buildings until experts clear them.
- **DE p29 (structural):** roof wind hooks, snow guards, snow load on flat roofs, wind braces, trees near the house, securing awnings.
- **DE: cold.** Heavy snowfall and avalanches are mentioned as causes of isolation (pp10, 22); heating advice is in topic 4. No specific cold-weather health advice.
- **DE: heat.** Heatwaves are Not covered (only "remedies for insect bites and sunburn" and electrolytes in the medicine cabinet, p15).
- **FR:** Not covered; warm clothes and a survival blanket are in the kit.

**Summary:** DE covers storms and thunderstorms well. Cold is covered only indirectly and heat not at all. FR covers none of these.

- **DE p47:** electrical safety, replacing old extension leads.
- **FR:** Not covered.

**Summary:** DE covers structural resilience and photo records for insurance claims. FR does not cover this topic.

## 22. Recommended duration of self-sufficiency

- **DE p10:** "With a supply of food and drink to last you 10 days, you are ready for this." **p34:** "Your goal must be to survive for 10 days without shopping." **p16:** power cuts usually last hours but "it may even be days". **p21:** the emergency pack is for "the first few days out of your house" and holds "Food for 2 days".
- **FR p1:** "the first 72 hours are often the most trying" (translated); the kit is for 72 hours while "waiting for help" (translated).

**Summary:** This is a clear disagreement. DE recommends a 10-day home stockpile, while FR's kit covers 72 hours (3 days). The two serve slightly different purposes: DE has a home stockpile plus a separate grab bag (2 days of food), while FR has one 72-hour kit for both staying and leaving.

## 23. Grab bag / evacuation bag contents

- **DE p21 (the practical tip):** "The aim of the disaster supplies kit is to help you to cope for the first few days out of your house. Basic rule: do not take more for each family member than fits into a rucksack. A rucksack is more practical than a suitcase, as you have both hands free."
- **DE p21, "The emergency rucksack should contain these items":** "First aid materials, personal medication"; "Battery-powered radio, spare batteries"; "Briefcase (refer to the section 'Have important documents ready to hand')"; "Food for 2 days in dust-proof packaging"; "Water bottle, cutlery and crockery"; "Torch, sleeping bag or blanket"; "Clothing and hygiene products for a couple of days"; "Camera or camera phone".
- **DE p21, "The correct clothing":** "Waterproof clothing, such as a waterproof jacket and waterproof trousers or a long raincoat"; "Waterproof shoes or wellingtons"; "In the event of danger from radioactive or chemical substances, use a dust mask or damp cloths that you hold in front of your mouth."
- **DE p21, "Do not forget!":** "Identity documents, money, valuables"; children's neck pouch or SOS capsule.
- **DE p39 (checklist, EMERGENCY PACK):** "Personal medication"; "Provisional protective clothing"; "Woollen blanket, sleeping bag"; "Underwear, socks"; "Wellingtons, sturdy footwear"; "Cutlery and crockery, thermos flask, cups"; "Materials for treating wounds"; "Can opener and penknife"; "Hard-wearing, warm clothing"; "Torch"; "Head covering, protective helmet"; "Protective mask, provisional respiratory protection"; "Work gloves"; "Camera or camera phone".
- **FR p1 (full kit, all translated):** "Battery radio (with spare batteries) in order to follow the instructions of the authorities"; "Medicines"; "First aid kit (alcohol, plasters, compresses…)"; "Basic tools (multi-purpose knife, tin opener...)"; "Non-perishable food that needs no cooking (tinned food, baby food jars, pet food…)"; "Warm clothes and survival blanket"; "Torch (with spare batteries), candles, lighter or matches"; "Mobile phone charger"; "Glasses (spare pair)"; "Cash (as cash machines may not work)"; "Drinking water in quantity (6 litres per person in bottles)"; "Photocopies of essential documents in a waterproof pouch (identity card, prescriptions...)"; "Spare keys for the house and the car"; "Games to pass the time". Also: "Put the bag somewhere easy to reach!" (translated).

**Summary:** There is a large overlap: radio, medicines and first aid, torch, food, water, documents, money, a can opener or knife and warm clothing. DE adds waterproofs, a helmet, a respiratory mask, work gloves, a sleeping bag and a camera. FR adds a phone charger, spare glasses, spare keys, candles and games. There is a minor difference on water: FR puts 6 L per person in the bag, while DE lists only a "water bottle" in the rucksack.

## 25. Keeping the plan/kit up to date

- **DE p11:** check best-before dates; mark the purchase date; new stock to the back, use older food first; "Regularly check your supplies."
- **DE p14:** "Regularly check the drugs in your medicine cabinet"; note the purchase date for drugs without an expiry date; bin expired medicines.
- **DE p23:** "Use the batteries up and replace them regularly."
- **DE p33:** "Plan together! Sit down with your family"; agree where the emergency pack, briefcase, extinguisher and supplies are kept; keep the brochure with the briefcase. **p47:** have the extinguisher serviced regularly. **p49:** smoke alarm test button and battery indicator. **p48:** check and service the building's safety facilities regularly.

**Summary:** Both require checking expiry dates and batteries. FR sets a clear annual check and a restock-after-use rule, while DE uses stock rotation and "regularly". No disagreement.

## Ideas worth borrowing for a UK household

1. **Use the 10-day per-person food table as a quantity calculator** (DE pp34–35). Per person: 20 L drinks, 3.5 kg cereals/potatoes/pasta/rice, 4.0 kg vegetables/pulses, 2.5 kg fruit/nuts, 2.6 kg milk products, 1.5 kg fish/meat/eggs, 0.357 kg fats/oils (about 2,200 kcal/day). Scale it to the household and to a chosen duration.
2. **Plan around about 2 litres of drinking water per person per day**, which both sources imply (DE p11: 14 L a week; FR p1: 6 L for 72 h). Add extra water for cooking (DE p34) and for flushing the toilet (DE p13).
3. **Stock what you normally eat and rotate it**: "No experiments", new stock to the back, and most of it edible cold (DE p11).
4. **Tiered document folder** with originals, certified copies and simple copies, and duplicates left with relatives or a solicitor (DE pp18–19, 38). Add FR's waterproof pouch and copies of prescriptions (FR p1).
5. **One rucksack per person, hands free** (DE p21), kept packed and somewhere easy to reach (FR p1).
6. **Small additions to the grab bag:** a spare pair of glasses, spare house and car keys, a phone charger and games (FR p1); waterproofs, wellingtons, work gloves and a camera phone for recording damage (DE pp21, 39).
7. **Child ID:** a neck pouch or SOS capsule with name, date of birth and address (DE p21).
8. **Fill the bath and containers early** in a water outage; use disposable plates; have a camping toilet with bags and a stock of bin bags (DE pp13, 37).
9. **Never cook on a barbecue or grill indoors** (DE p17), and avoid using candles while sealed in a room (DE p64). In a UK plan, add an explicit carbon monoxide alarm, which neither source mentions.
10. **Family discussion of meeting points and escape routes**, since not everyone may be home (DE p33). Agree signals and roles with neighbours in case phone networks are down (DE p56).
11. **A printed emergency numbers sheet** with blank lines for the poison centre, out-of-hours pharmacy and utility company (DE p68). UK equivalents would be 999/112, 101, 111, the gas emergency line (0800 111 999), 105 for power cuts and the water company.
12. **Script for calling emergency services:** "Where? What has happened? Who is calling? Wait for questions!" (DE pp27, 68).
13. **Seven-step fire response**, including closing doors but not locking them and checking everyone is out (DE pp51–53). Never put water on a fat fire; use a lid (DE p51).
14. **Smoke alarm placement** in bedrooms, children's rooms, living rooms, halls and staircases, cellars and attics (DE p49). Check UK guidance on a kitchen heat alarm.
15. **Annual kit check plus restock after use** (FR p1), with batteries replaced and medicine expiry dates checked (DE p14).
16. **Flood "before" list:** sandbags or boards, move valuables upstairs, keep chemicals out of reach, know your local flood level, and photograph damage for insurance before clearing up (DE pp55, 59). Never drive through flood water or use basement car parks (DE p57).
17. **Photograph your home's contents in advance** for insurance claims (DE p41).
18. **Shelter-in-place routine for chemical incidents:** go in, close windows, switch off fans, use an internal room and listen to local radio. If contaminated, bag outer clothes and wash hands, then face and hair (DE pp63–64).
19. **Cash at home**, since cash machines fail in power cuts (DE p17; FR p1).
20. **Use children's learning resources** to involve children (DE p66, Max & Flocke). Look for a UK equivalent.
