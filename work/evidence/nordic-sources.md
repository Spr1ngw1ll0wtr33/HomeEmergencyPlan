# Nordic preparedness guides: evidence extract

Evidence for a UK family home emergency plan, drawn from four Nordic government guides (English-language editions). Text was extracted with `pdftotext -layout`; image-heavy and checklist pages were also viewed as rendered images. Quotations in "double quotes" are verbatim from the English editions (including their own spellings, e.g. "makerel", "celler").

## 1. Alerts, warnings and staying informed

- **DK p.4**: Warnings may come via "the physical warning sirens, S!RENEN (Denmark's mobile-based public warning system) and emergency messages." "If you hear a warning siren, go indoors and seek information from the authorities through DR or TV 2. Should other systems be down, DR will broadcast emergency announcements from the authorities. With a battery-operated/wind-up/solar-powered FM radio, you can stay up-to-date if other information channels fail. Many people have an FM radio in their car." Checklist (p.6): "Battery-operated/wind-up/solar-powered FM radio (or your car radio may suffice)." Emergency number 1-1-2; "If there are problems with 1-1-2, the authorities will announce alternative contact details." No frequencies given.
- **NO p.14**: "NRK P1 is the emergency channel that will ensure the broadcasting of public information, even if other news media and public websites are inaccessible." "Have a DAB radio that will function even if the electricity supply fails ... run on batteries, wind-up mechanisms or are rechargeable with a solar cell. Remember you can listen to the radio or charge your mobile phone in your car." "Check with your municipality if there are muster stations or other public places where you can receive information if the electricity supply or telecommunications should fail." Seek information from "public authority channels and editorially-controlled journalistic media".
- **NO p.15** (Emergency Alert System / sirens): mobile emergency alert "In a serious situation with an acute risk to life and health ... To receive alerts, you must have 4G or 5G coverage." Sirens: "three series with one minute in between each series ... «Important notice – seek information». For example, listen to NRK P1." "short intervals for approximately one minute this means «Danger, risk of attack – seek cover»." "sound continuously for 30 seconds, this means that the danger is over." Checklist p.10: "Battery-powered, solar or wind-up DAB radio."
- **SE p.8 (pr. 14–15)**: Important Public Announcement (IPA) signal: "Signal 7 seconds – break 14 seconds"; "Danger over: Unbroken signal 30 seconds". IPAs "broadcast primarily on Sveriges Radio's radio stations, Sveriges Television's TV channels and SVT's teletext system. IPAs can also be sent as text messages to mobile phones within a specific area." Outdoor warning system ("Hesa Fredrik"): "If you hear the signal: go indoors, close windows, doors and ventilation and listen to Sveriges Radio's radio station P4". Test: "at 15:00 on the first non-public holiday monday in March, June, September and December."
- **SE p.9 (pr. 16–17)**: Emergency alarm (imminent threat of war / at war): "Signal 30 seconds – break 15 seconds". Air raid warning: "Signal with short bursts for one minute". "New ways to warn the population may be applicable." Checklist p.6 (pr. 11): "a radio powered by batteries, solar cells or winding", "a car radio". Websites: krisinformation.se, dinsäkerhet.se (p.11, pr. 20).
- **FI p.7**: "An emergency warning is read on the radio and displayed on television if necessary." Alarm signal: "A rising and falling signal that lasts for one minute is used to alert of a danger. When you hear the alarm signal, you should go indoors." Test: "with a short sound on the first Monday of each month at 12 o'clock. You do not have to do anything when you hear the test signal." App: "Download the 112 Suomi app for your phone. You will receive the emergency warnings in your area directly to your phone when you turn on app notifications." FI p.5: "Get a battery-powered radio. YLE provides information about disruptions on its radio channels." "Think in advance about who or which place locally you could ask for more information." Checklist p.3: "Radio operating on batteries or otherwise without mains power".

**Nordic position:** All four rely on a non-mains radio (battery/wind-up/solar, or the car radio) tuned to the national public broadcaster as the fallback channel, plus sirens and mobile alerts; on hearing a siren, go indoors and listen. Differences: NO specifies DAB, DK specifies FM; none gives frequencies; siren patterns differ by country (not transferable to the UK, which has no national siren system).

## 2. Water

- **DK p.2**: "Calculate on 3 litres of water per person per day for drinking and food preparation. So if you need water for three days, you need 9 litres of water per person. Make sure you have water for everyone in your household – including any pets". Storage: bottled water "store it in a cool, dark place and only for as long as the expiry date on the bottle states. Replace it regularly." Tap water "will keep for several months in a sealed bottle/container if stored in a cool, dark place." "Only use drinking water containers that are approved for food use". "Water kept in containers should be changed regularly. Use the old water for watering plants, for example, then wash and refill the container." No purification advice.
- **NO p.5**: "If you store around 20 litres of drinking water per person, this will cover the minimum requirements for one week in the event the domestic water supply fails. Some of our hydration needs can be covered by juice, soft drinks and other alcohol-free drinks." Container method: wash with dish soap; "Fill the container with water and add two corks of bleach per 10 litres of water. Allow this to stand for a minimum of 30 minutes. Empty the container and rinse thoroughly." "Fill containers or bottles completely full with cold, running water from the tap." "Store the water in a dark, cool place, away from direct sunlight." "Clean water in clean containers can be stored for years, without becoming harmful to drink." Space tips: several smaller containers; cupboards / under the kitchen worktop; joint storage via housing association. NO p.13: hot water heater water usable (unplug first; "This water should not be used for drinking"); rain/river water for hygiene only, may be contaminated (nuclear/chemical).
- **SE p.6 (pr. 10–11)**: "Clean drinking water is vital. Allow for at least three litres per adult per day. If you are uncertain about its quality, you need to be able to boil the water." Items: "bottles", "buckets with lids", "Plastic bottles to freeze water in (do not fill to the top as the bottle will crack if you do)", "mineral water", "jerry cans, ideally with a tap, to collect water in. You can also have a couple of clean jerry cans that are filled with water as a reserve. These are to be stored in a cool, dark place."
- **FI p.3**: "At least 2 litres of drinking water for each person for every day"; "Clean containers with lids for water storage". FI p.5: "Keep bottled water at home: at least 2 litres for each resident of your household for three days."

**Nordic position:** 2–3 litres per person per day stored in a cool, dark place and rotated. Disagreement: DK and SE say 3 litres (SE "per adult"); FI says at least 2 litres; NO says "around 20 litres" per person for a week (about 2.9 litres a day). Only NO gives a container-sterilising method (bleach) and claims stored water keeps "for years"; DK says "several months" for tap water. SE alone mentions boiling and freezing water in bottles.

## 3. Food

- **DK p.2**: "enough food for your household for three days ... food that can last for a long period outside the fridge or freezer. Choose foods that do not require cooking in the oven or on a hob (e.g. tinned food, crispbread). Choose foods that you and your household use on a daily basis and like." Build up gradually; "use and replenish your stocks on a regular basis"; "Store food in a cool, dry, dark place." Cooking: "make sure you can boil water without access to electricity or mains gas. You could use a barbecue grill or camping stove ... Never use outdoor grills etc. indoors or in fully or partially enclosed spaces. There is a risk of smoke and/or carbon monoxide poisoning."
- **NO p.8**: "you should always have sufficient food for one week, for everyone in your household." "Use food with a short shelf life first, such as chilled and frozen products." Long-life, room-temperature examples: "crispbreads, oatmeal, canned pulses and beans, canned sandwich spreads, energy bars, dried fruit, chocolate, honey, biscuits and nuts." Needing heating: "pasta, rice, instant soups, flour, canned meals and freeze-dried foods." Plan for special diets/allergies. "If you plan to prepare food on a camping stove, gas cooker or grill, you must check regularly that the equipment is in good condition." Checklist p.10: "Grill, solid fuel stove or camping stove", "Extra gas bottles or fuel", "Food and water for pets."
- **SE p.6 (pr. 10–11)**: "extra food at home that provides sufficient calories. Use non-perishable food that can be prepared quickly, requires little water or can be eaten without preparation." Full list: "potatoes, cabbage, carrots, eggs"; "bread with a long shelf-life, e.g. tortillas, hard bread, crackers, rusks"; "cheese spread, soft whey cheese and other spreads in tubes"; "oat milk, soy milk, milk powder"; "cooking oil, hard cheese"; "quick-cook pasta, rice, grains, instant mashed potatoes"; "precooked lentils, beans, vegetables, hummus in tins"; "chopped tomatoes to, for example, cook pasta in"; "tins of bolognese sauce, makerel, sardines, ravioli, salmon balls, boiled meat, soup"; "fruit purée, jam, marmelade"; "prepared blueberry and rosehip soup, juice or another drink that can be stored at room temperature"; "coffee, tea, chocolate, energy bars, honey, almonds, nuts, nut butter, seeds." Cooking: "spirit stove and fuel" (under "Other").
- **FI p.3/p.5**: "Food you can eat as is"; "Get food you can eat as is as your emergency supply for at least three days. Think about how you can cook if your electricity is out."

**Nordic position:** Store familiar, long-life food that needs little or no cooking or water, rotate it through normal use, and have a non-electric way to boil water (camping/spirit stove, grill) used outdoors only. Disagreement on duration: DK and FI three days, NO one week; SE (2018) unspecified. NO's "eat chilled and frozen food first" is a useful sequencing tip.

## 4. Heating and keeping warm without power

- **DK p.3**: "use your household's blankets, duvets, warm clothes, etc. If it is cold for a prolonged period, keep your household warm by staying mainly in one room. If you use candles or other heat sources, place them on a solid surface away from flammable materials such as curtains and carpets."
- **NO p.6–7**: Keep "Warm clothing and blankets, duvets or sleeping bags." Alternatives: "Ensure that your fireplace and chimney are approved and in good condition. Make sure you have enough firewood." "A gas or paraffin heater designed for use indoors can be an alternative". Generator/battery unit (see topic 5). "Help each other": "In the event of a long-term power failure, and you do not have alternative heating: make arrangements to stay with neighbours, family or friends." Offer to host those without heating. p.7: "Close doors or hang up blankets between rooms." "Hang up blankets or curtains in front of windows." Checklist p.10 includes "An agreement with family or friends for overnight accommodation."
- **SE p.6 (pr. 10–11)**: "Gather together in one room, hang blankets over the windows, cover the floor with rugs and build a den under a table to keep warm. Think about the risk of fire. Extinguish all candles and alternative heating sources before you go to sleep. Air the room regularly to let in oxygen." Items: "woolen clothes", "warm all-weather outdoor clothing", "hats, gloves, scarves", "blankets", "sleeping mats", "sleeping bags", "candles", "tea lights", "matches or fire-lighter", "alternative heat sources, e.g. LPG heaters, paraffin heaters."
- **FI p.6**: "Think about one space in your home that you can keep warm for the longest. Only stay in that one space. Make sure you have warm blankets at home."

**Nordic position:** Retreat to one room, insulate it with blankets over windows and doorways, layer clothing and bedding, and plan to stay with others if needed. NO and SE endorse indoor-rated gas/paraffin/LPG heaters and wood stoves; DK and FI do not mention them. SE alone gives the "extinguish before sleep; air the room" safety rule.

## 5. Lighting and power

- **DK p.3/p.6**: "Use a power bank/battery pack to keep your mobile and other devices powered". "Other useful items to have on hand are a torch, candles, matches and batteries." Candle safety: "solid surface away from flammable materials". Carbon monoxide: grills/outdoor stoves never indoors (p.2).
- **NO p.6**: "A store of candles and matches or a lighter." "Torches or head lamps ... (remember to store extra batteries)." "Emergency power, such as a generator or battery unit with a 230V outlet, can also be an alternative. In such case, you must make yourself thoroughly familiar with regulations and guidelines for safe use." Checklist p.10: "Battery-powered, solar or wind-up torches or head lamps", "Batteries and charged power banks". NO p.14: "Keep a power bank, and charge it regularly." Car can charge phone. No explicit carbon-monoxide warning.
- **SE p.6 (pr. 10–11)**: "torch, head torch", "batteries", "candles", "tea lights", "extra batteries/power bank for devices such as mobile phones", "mobile phone charger that works in the car", "fuel in the tank." Fire/oxygen warning as in topic 4. No generator mention.
- **FI p.6**: "Get a flashlight or a headlamp. Make sure you have suitable batteries for the light/lamp." Checklist p.3: "A flashlight operating on batteries or otherwise without mains power", "Batteries and a charged backup power supply".

**Nordic position:** Torch or head torch with spare batteries, charged power banks, candles/matches as backup, and the car as a charger. Only NO mentions generators/230V battery units (with a safety caveat); only DK explicitly names carbon monoxide.

## 6. Communications

- **DK p.3–4**: "In most crisis situations, a mobile phone is essential." "During major or prolonged power outages, telephone networks and internet may be affected or go down." "Consider writing down important phone numbers and keeping them on the fridge, for example." 112 only for emergencies.
- **NO p.14**: "Keep a paper list of important telephone numbers in case you have to borrow a telephone." Wi-Fi calling: "check whether you can activate Wi-Fi calls on your mobile phone, and whether you have apps on your mobile that communicate via the Internet." "Check whether anyone in your family or a neighbour has an alternative mobile telephone subscription, different to yours". Checklist p.10 list contents: "emergency numbers, emergency clinic, veterinary clinics, family friends and neighbours." NO p.20: emergency numbers 110 fire, 112 police, 113 medical, "14 12" for deaf/hard of hearing; text service via nodsms.no; blank "Your notes" area.
- **SE p.3 (pr. 5)**: "You also need to be able to make contact with relatives." SE p.4 (pr. 7): in a terror attack "Put your mobile on silent"; "Do not call anyone with your mobile unless you have to. If the network is overloaded, it may be difficult for vital calls to get through." SE p.10 (pr. 18): "Even if your pay-as-you-go SIM card has no credit, or your mobile has no SIM card, you can still call 112." SE p.10 (pr. 19) "Important notes": "Write down important telephone numbers, addresses and the closest shelter or other protective space." Checklist: "a list of important telephone numbers on paper".
- **FI p.4**: "If you need urgent help, call 112."
- Meeting points: **not covered** as a household reunion point in any source. NO p.14 refers to municipal "muster stations" (for information); NO p.18 mentions "Evacuation and family contact centres".

**Nordic position:** Keep a paper list of numbers, keep the phone charged, and minimise non-essential calls. NO adds network diversity (a neighbour on another network, Wi-Fi calling). None sets out a family meeting-point or out-of-area contact plan.

## 7. Cash and payments

- **DK p.3/p.6**: "Have one or more physical payment cards that you can use if the internet is down or you can't pay with your phone. Make sure you can remember your PIN codes. In addition, consider having a small amount of cash in coins and small denomination notes."
- **NO p.9**: "Hold several payment cards. These can be different payment cards (debit or credit cards) or bank cards issued by different banks." "Keep some cash at home, preferably in varied and smaller values. The amount of cash will be relative to the number of persons in your household and the type of goods you will need to purchase." "Keep accounts in several banks, in case of technical problems with one particular bank."
- **SE p.6 (pr. 11)**: "cash in small denominations". SE p.3 (pr. 4) lists "Payment cards and cash machines do not work" as a possible consequence.
- **FI p.3**: "A small amount of cash".

**Nordic position:** Keep some cash in small denominations. DK and NO add physical cards (DK: know your PINs; NO: cards and accounts at more than one bank). No source gives a cash amount.

## 8. Documents and records

- **DK**: Not covered (beyond writing down phone numbers).
- **NO p.18**: take "ID documentation" if you have to leave home at short notice.
- **SE p.6 (pr. 11)**: "paper printouts of information such as insurance policies, bank details, registration certificates". SE p.9 (pr. 16): on emergency alarm be ready to leave with "identification documents".
- **FI**: Not covered.

**Nordic position:** Only SE recommends paper copies of key records (insurance, bank details, registration certificates); NO and SE say take ID when leaving.

## 9. Medication and first aid

- **DK p.3**: "Make sure you have at least three days' worth of medications and a first aid kit. A first aid kit can include plasters, dressings (compresses, elastic bandages, gauze, etc.) and products for cleaning and disinfecting wounds." DK p.4: a larger stockpile is good "especially if you ... have special needs such as essential medicines."
- **NO p.12**: "it may be difficult to get through on medical emergency numbers or to emergency clinics." "If you can, learn basic first aid." "If you have a car, you should also have first aid equipment in the car." "Keep a minimum of seven days extra supply of medicines and medical equipment that you use regularly. Establish a routine, and pick up new supplies no later than one week before they run out." Basic first aid box: "Ensure an open airway. Stop any external bleeding and keep the injured person warm. Monitor breathing. Monitor whether the injured person is conscious. Perform cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR). Use a defibrillator."
- **SE p.6 (pr. 11)**: "medicine cabinet and extra medicines". SE p.10 (pr. 18): "Learn to provide first aid. Your knowledge can save lives." SOS Alarm on 112 can advise at the scene.
- **FI p.3**: "Essential medications". FI p.8: "It is good to have first aid skills"; Finnish Red Cross runs courses.

**Nordic position:** Hold extra regular medicines plus a first aid kit and learn first aid. Duration differs: DK at least three days; NO at least seven days extra, refilled a week before running out. NO gives the clearest repeat-prescription routine.

## 10. Hygiene and sanitation

- **DK p.3/p.5**: "toilet paper, hand sanitiser, wet wipes, sanitary pads and tampons"; checklist adds "Nappies". No toilet-without-water advice.
- **NO p.13**: "Keep wet wipes, hand sanitiser and disposable gloves in your home. Keep extra toilet paper and waste bags." Extra nappies/sanitary products. If water is off for longer: hygiene after the toilet and before preparing food; keep home clean, throw away waste to avoid "bacteria and vermin". Emergency toilet: "placing a double carrier bag around the toilet seat or a bucket. Carrier bags can then be double-knotted and placed in regular waste containers." Water heater and rain/river water for hygiene (not drinking).
- **SE p.6 (pr. 10–11)**: "If the toilet is not working, you can take strong plastic bags and place them in the toilet bowl. Good hand hygiene is important for avoiding infection." Items: "buckets with lids", "wet wipes", "hand sanitiser", "nappies and menstrual products".
- **FI p.3**: "Wet wipes and hand sanitiser".

**Nordic position:** Wipes, hand sanitiser, toilet paper and sanitary/nappy supplies; NO and SE describe a bagged emergency toilet (bag in the bowl or a bucket). NO's guidance is fullest (gloves, waste bags, disposal, water-heater water).

## 11. Pets

- **DK p.2/p.3/p.5**: water "including any pets"; "You may also have pets that need food and water"; checklist "Water for pets if needed".
- **NO p.10**: "Food and water for pets"; phone list to include "veterinary clinics".
- **SE**: Not covered.
- **FI**: Not covered.

**Nordic position:** Brief only: include pets' food and water in the store (DK, NO); NO adds the vet's number. No pet evacuation advice.

## 12. Vulnerable people, neighbours and community

- **DK p.3–5**: plan for "infants, elderly or sick people in the household with special needs such as baby formula, nappies or care items". "Be aware of whether people in your neighbourhood or social circle need help." If remote or needing transport help, arrange with neighbours, family, friends. "Not everyone is equally able to prepare themselves – and sometimes it makes more sense to prepare with family, friends or neighbours. You may live in a small space, have a tight budget or have difficulty walking ... Some preparation is better than no preparation."
- **NO p.16**: "Find your «preparedness buddy»". Ways to help each other: "First aid if anyone is injured. Care and support. Accommodation and assistance if anyone needs to be evacuated from their home. Translation and explanation of information provided by public authorities. Practical help in using equipment and tools. Shopping, practical help and transport. Exchange and loaning of equipment and goods. Loan of a mobile telephone connected to a different network than your own. Community cooking/food preparation." Voluntary organisations and faith communities; housing associations can set up "joint self-preparedness stocks" e.g. water in basements. NO p.10: "families, friends and neighbours can cooperate regarding emergency preparedness." NO p.6: host neighbours without heating.
- **SE p.3 (pr. 5)**: "help will be provided first to those who need it most. The majority must be prepared to cope on their own for some time. The better prepared you are, the greater the opportunity you will also have to help others". SE p.6: "It is a good idea to share certain things and borrow from one another." SE p.10 (pr. 18): "Get involved!" in voluntary defence organisations and faith communities; "talk about its contents with people around you" (pr. 19).
- **FI p.2/p.4/p.8**: preparedness includes "a sense of community, which means trust and connection with other people and an ability to help." "Help others to the best of your ability and ask for help as needed." Join associations or volunteer; National Defence Training Association (mpk.fi) training "open to everyone".

**Nordic position:** Self-reliance is framed as freeing authorities to help the most vulnerable; all four urge helping neighbours. NO's "preparedness buddy" list is the most concrete. Consistent across sources; no disagreement.

## 13. Shelter in place (including chemical, nuclear and iodine)

- **DK p.3/p.5**: "In the event of a nuclear accident, the most important advice is to stay indoors. It is recommended that iodine tablets should be available for people under the age of 40 as well as pregnant and breastfeeding women ... follow the recommendations of the authorities." Siren: "go indoors" (p.4).
- **NO p.12**: "Keep iodine tablets in your home (applies to children and adults under 40 years of age, pregnant or breastfeeding). The tablets can protect against radioactive iodine in the event of nuclear accidents and must only be taken on instruction from the authorities." NO p.18: "In many cases, it is safest to remain at home." In war: shelter in a basement or "a room in the centre of the building ... keep away from windows."
- **SE p.8 (pr. 15)**: outdoor warning: "go indoors, close windows, doors and ventilation and listen to Sveriges Radio's radio station P4". Iodine: not covered.
- **FI p.7**: "When there is a threat, you typically should seek shelter indoors ... for example, if harmful smoke is released into the air." Steps: "Go indoors. Stop ventilation. Tightly close and seal any doors, windows and ventilation holes. Follow the authorities' emergency warnings and act according to their instructions. Remain indoors until the authority informs you that the situation is over." Checklist p.3: "Iodine tablets as recommended", "Duct tape".

**Nordic position:** Go in, shut windows/doors, switch off ventilation, listen to the radio, stay in until told. DK, NO and FI include iodine tablets (DK/NO: under-40s and pregnant/breastfeeding; take only on instruction); SE (2018) does not. FI alone adds duct tape for sealing.

## 14. Evacuation with notice

- **DK p.4/p.6**: consider alternative transport and arrangements with others if remote; "Do you have alternative transport options in the event of flooding". No take-list or utility shut-off.
- **NO p.18**: "Make a plan for where you can go and how you will get there. Perhaps you can go to stay with relatives, friends or at a recreational cabin. Evacuation and family contact centres are often established". Take: "warm clothes/blankets, a mobile telephone, bank cards and cash, some food and drink, ID documentation, medicines and other things you may need (e.g. spectacles, contact lenses, walking frames or hearing aids)." "Make sure you have enough fuel or electricity if you are using a vehicle. Be aware that transport during crises can be chaotic and take much longer."
- **SE p.9 (pr. 16)**: on emergency alarm "Get ready to leave home with that which is most important, warm clothes, something to eat and drink and identification documents."
- **FI**: Not covered.
- Shutting off utilities: **not covered** by any source. Routes: only NO ("how you will get there").

**Nordic position:** Pre-arrange somewhere to go (family/friends) and take warm clothes, phone, money/cards, food and drink, ID and medicines. No source covers turning off gas, water or electricity.

## 15. Fire safety and escape

- **DK p.3**: candles/heat sources "on a solid surface away from flammable materials such as curtains and carpets." Grills indoors: smoke/CO risk (p.2).
- **NO p.6/p.8**: chimney and fireplace "approved and in good condition"; check camping stove/gas cooker/grill condition regularly; 110 fire (p.20).
- **SE p.6 (pr. 10)**: "Think about the risk of fire. Extinguish all candles and alternative heating sources before you go to sleep."
- **FI p.4**: 112 for fires.
- Smoke alarms, extinguishers, escape plans: **not covered** by any source.

**Nordic position:** Only fire risk from emergency heating/lighting is addressed; no source covers smoke alarms or home escape plans.

## 16. Flooding

- **DK p.3/p.6**: "if you live close to the coast or in a low-lying area, you could be affected by flooding. It's a good idea to find out where you can get hold of such items as sandbags". Checklist asks about flood-exposed location and alternative transport in a flood.
- **NO**: Not covered as advice (flood photograph on p.3 only).
- **SE p.3 (pr. 4–5)**: flooding may "become more common"; "Do you live in an area that is sensitive to landslides or flooding?"
- **FI p.2**: "natural phenomena, such as storms or floods" listed as situations requiring preparedness.

**Nordic position:** Flooding is acknowledged as a risk; only DK gives an action (know where to get sandbags). No source gives flood-response steps.

## 17. Storms, cold and heat

- **DK p.1**: "Extreme weather events are set to become more frequent and severe". Cold: see topic 4.
- **NO p.2**: extreme weather listed; winter power-cut cold (p.6).
- **SE p.3 (pr. 4)**: climate change may bring "flooding and forest fires"; cold at "a cold time of the year" (p.6).
- **FI p.2/p.7**: storms listed; alarm signal may be used for "a storm".
- Heat: **not covered** by any source.

**Nordic position:** Cold during power cuts is well covered; storms are named only as triggers; heatwaves are absent.

## 18. Home preparation and insurance

- **DK p.3–4**: assess exposure (coast, low-lying); a bigger stockpile "If you want and can – i.e., have the time, space and resources".
- **NO p.5/p.6/p.16**: storage locations for water; fireplace/chimney approval; firewood; housing-association joint stocks.
- **SE p.3 (pr. 5)**: "Think about what risks may affect you and your local area ... landslides or flooding? Is there some sort of hazardous industry or something else in your area that may be good to know about?" Insurance: "paper printouts of information such as insurance policies" (p.6, pr. 11).
- **FI**: Not covered beyond supplies.
- Insurance cover itself: **not covered**.

**Nordic position:** Know your local risks (DK, SE) and keep stores where space allows; insurance appears only as a paper record to keep (SE).

## 19. Car kit

- **DK p.4**: car FM radio as fallback.
- **NO p.12**: "If you have a car, you should also have first aid equipment in the car." p.14: radio and phone charging in the car. p.18: enough fuel/charge.
- **SE p.6 (pr. 11)**: "a car radio", "mobile phone charger that works in the car", "fuel in the tank."
- **FI**: Not covered.

**Nordic position:** No full car kit; the car is treated as a radio, charger and evacuation vehicle, so keep fuel in it (SE, NO) and a first aid kit in it (NO).

## 20. Children

- **DK p.3/p.6**: infants' "baby formula, nappies"; checklist "Are there children or elderly people in the household?"
- **NO p.10/p.12**: nappies; iodine "applies to children and adults under 40".
- **SE**: nappies only (p.6, pr. 11). The brochure's illustration of the radio scene includes a child, but there is no child-specific text.
- **FI p.6**: games, books, paper and pens (for everyone, not specifically children).

**Nordic position:** Children appear only as supply needs (formula, nappies) and in iodine eligibility; no advice on explaining crises to children or school arrangements.

## 21. Psychological wellbeing and coping

- **DK**: Not covered (beyond reassurance tone).
- **NO p.17**: "it is normal to experience concern, negative thoughts, poor sleep, anxiety and other strong emotions." "Spend your time on things you can do something about". "Continue as much as possible with your normal routines; for example, regular mealtimes, personal hygiene etc." "Make contact with other people ... Social contact helps us to share our thoughts".
- **SE p.2 (pr. 3)**: "Many people may feel a sense of anxiety when faced with an uncertain world."
- **FI p.2/p.4/p.6**: preparedness includes "functional capacity, including both physical and mental capacity". "Keep calm." "Do not stay alone if you are feeling uncomfortable. Talk to people close to you or your neighbours." "make sure you have games, books, and paper and pens at home." "Think about the positive actions that have previously made you feel better". "Preparedness does not mean that you think something bad will happen."

**Nordic position:** Normalise anxiety, keep routines, stay connected and have low-tech activities to hand (NO, FI). DK and SE barely address it.

## 22. Recommended duration of self-sufficiency

- **DK p.1/p.2/p.4**: "three days". Rationale (p.4–5) based on "the National Risk Assessment". A longer stockpile is "a good idea" for exposed areas or special needs.
- **NO p.2**: "as many people as possible be prepared to be self-sustained for one week."
- **SE p.3 (pr. 5)**: "The majority must be prepared to cope on their own for some time." No figure in this 2018 edition.
- **FI p.2/p.3**: "emergency supplies for at least 3 days".

**Nordic position:** Three days is the baseline (DK, FI); NO asks for one week. SE 2018 gives no figure (check the current MSB edition). This is the main disagreement among the sources.

## 23. Grab bag / evacuation bag contents

- **DK**: Not covered.
- **NO p.18**: "warm clothes/blankets, a mobile telephone, bank cards and cash, some food and drink, ID documentation, medicines and other things you may need (e.g. spectacles, contact lenses, walking frames or hearing aids)." Not framed as a pre-packed bag.
- **SE p.9 (pr. 16)**: "that which is most important, warm clothes, something to eat and drink and identification documents."
- **FI**: Not covered.

**Nordic position:** No source recommends a pre-packed grab bag; NO and SE give a take-with-you list. NO's mention of glasses, contact lenses, walking frames and hearing aids is distinctive.

## 24. Scams, cyber and misinformation

- **DK p.4**: "Sharing verified information during a crisis and helping to prevent the spread of rumours and disinformation or misinformation is a big help. Always double-check the credibility of the source before sharing". Cyber attacks named as a threat (p.1).
- **NO p.15**: "Hostile parties can knowingly spread false information, to destabilise society." "Be particularly sceptical if any information appears to be surprising, frightening or makes you angry." "Check who is behind the information". "Be aware that text, audio, images and video can be manipulated." "Think about what type of information you share". NO p.9: digital attacks can disrupt payments.
- **SE p.4 (pr. 6)**: questions to appraise a source: "Is this factual information or opinion? What is the aim of this information? Who has put this out? Is the source trustworthy? Is this information available somewhere else? Is this information new or old and why is it out there at this precise moment?" "Do not believe in rumours – use more than one reliable source". "Do not spread rumours". SE p.7 (pr. 13): "All information to the effect that resistance is to cease is false."
- **FI p.4**: reliable information from authorities' websites and YLE.
- Scams/fraud: **not covered** by any source.

**Nordic position:** Check the source, use official/editorial media, be wary of emotive content and do not share unverified claims. SE's six-question checklist and NO's manipulation warning are the most practical. Crisis scams are not covered.

## 25. Keeping the plan and kit up to date

- **DK p.2**: replace bottled water by expiry; change stored tap water "regularly"; "use and replenish your stocks on a regular basis".
- **NO p.8/p.10/p.12/p.14**: "If you stock up before you run out you will always have some in reserve." "You should review your needs and update your stocks at regular intervals." Medicine refill a week before running out; charge the power bank regularly; check cooking equipment regularly.
- **SE p.8 (pr. 15)**: siren test schedule (a natural reminder date); "Keep yourself up to date by visiting dinsäkerhet.se" (p.9). "KEEP THIS BROCHURE!" (p.2).
- **FI p.6**: self-assessment questions ("Did you notice any areas where you could improve your preparedness?").

**Nordic position:** Rotate stock through normal use and review periodically; no source sets a fixed review interval. NO's "stock up before you run out" and medicine-refill rule are the most actionable.

## 26. War, military and hybrid threats (context only)

- **DK p.1**: cyber attacks and sabotage "serious due to the deteriorating security situation in Europe".
- **NO p.2/p.18–19**: war and sabotage named; seek emergency shelters on notification, otherwise basement or central room away from windows; public shelters map at kart.dsb.no.
- **SE p.5–9 (pr. 8–17)**: total defence and the duty for ages "16 and 70" to contribute; heightened state of alert; list of potential attacks (cyber, sabotage, terror, influence operations, severed transport, military attack); "If Sweden is attacked by another country, we will never give up. All information to the effect that resistance is to cease is false." Shelters marked "SKYDDSRUM"; use the nearest; in an emergency a "celler, tunnel or metro station". Terror-attack advice (pr. 7): move to a safe place, avoid crowds, phone on silent, avoid calling people in the danger area.
- **FI**: Not covered beyond the general framing.

**Nordic position (context):** SE and NO cover war and shelters in detail; DK and FI only allude to the security situation. Mostly not transferable to UK household planning, though SE's terror-attack advice (phone on silent, keep lines free, do not share unconfirmed information) is generally applicable.

## Ideas worth borrowing for a UK household

Practical points from these guides that typical UK household guidance does not spell out (check against the UK evidence files before relying on this list):

1. **Calculate water explicitly:** 3 litres per person per day, so 9 litres each for three days (DK p.2); about 20 litres each for a week (NO p.5). Include pets (DK p.2).
2. **Fill your own containers safely:** wash, sterilise with "two corks of bleach per 10 litres of water" for 30 minutes, rinse, fill to the brim with cold tap water, store cool and dark (NO p.5). Rotate stored water onto plants (DK p.2).
3. **Freeze water in plastic bottles, not filled to the top** (SE p.6, pr. 11). They act as ice packs in a power cut and become drinking water.
4. **Hot-water cylinder as a reserve for washing water** (unplug first; not for drinking) (NO p.13).
5. **Bagged emergency toilet:** a double carrier bag in the toilet bowl or a bucket, double-knotted and put in the normal bin; keep disposable gloves and waste bags (NO p.13; SE p.6).
6. **Warm-room plan:** one room, blankets over windows and doorways, rugs on the floor, a "den under a table"; put out candles and heaters before sleeping and air the room (SE p.6; NO p.7; FI p.6; DK p.3).
7. **Reciprocal "warm house" agreement** with family, friends or neighbours for overnight stays if the heating fails (NO p.6, p.10).
8. **Eat chilled and frozen food first** in a power cut, then the cupboard store (NO p.8). SE's food list (p.6, pr. 10) is a ready-made shopping list.
9. **Payment resilience:** a physical card you know the PIN for (DK p.3), cards and accounts at more than one bank (NO p.9), and cash in coins and small notes (DK, NO, SE, FI).
10. **Network diversity:** know a neighbour on a different mobile network, enable Wi-Fi calling (NO p.14, p.16). Keep the phone list on paper, on the fridge (DK p.4; NO p.14).
11. **Medicine routine:** keep at least seven days' extra, and collect repeats "no later than one week before they run out" (NO p.12).
12. **Paper copies of insurance policies, bank details and registration certificates** (SE p.6, pr. 11).
13. **Leave-home list that includes glasses, contact lenses, walking frames and hearing aids** (NO p.18).
14. **Car as a resource:** keep fuel in the tank, a car phone charger and a first aid kit; use the car radio for news (SE p.6; NO p.12, p.14, p.18; DK p.4).
15. **Shelter-in-place drill:** go in, stop ventilation, seal doors, windows and vents (duct tape is on FI's checklist), radio on, stay in until told (FI p.3, p.7; SE p.8).
16. **Source-checking questions** before sharing crisis information (SE p.4, pr. 6), and extra scepticism for content that is "surprising, frightening or makes you angry" (NO p.15).
17. **Morale kit:** games, books, paper and pens; keep normal mealtimes and routines (FI p.6; NO p.17).
18. **"Preparedness buddy"** and the NO p.16 list of mutual help (first aid, lending kit, transport, translation, community cooking).
19. **Know your local exposure** (coast, low-lying land, hazardous industry) and where to get sandbags (DK p.3; SE p.3, pr. 5).
