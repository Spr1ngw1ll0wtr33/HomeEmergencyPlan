## 2. Water

- **UK-GOV** 091126: "Bottled water for 3 days". "3 litres per person per day for drinking. More would be needed for other tasks such as cooking and washing."
- **UK-GOV-CHK** p.2: the same wording ("Bottled water for 3 days. 3 litres per person per day for drinking. More would be needed for other tasks such as cooking and washing.").
- **UK-GOV-PLAN** p.8: "Bottled water (3 litres per person per day for at least 3 days)".
- **BRC**
  - 091744/091752: "Three days' supply of drinking water, in case a power cut, drought or flood disrupts the water supply."
  - 091832: "Drinking water, enough for three days, allowing around 2.5 to 3 litres per person per day."
  - 091843 (heatwave): "extra drinking water".
- **RS-KIT**: Not covered (not in the captured part).
- **RS-PLAN**: no water quantity. 092102 suggests helping vulnerable people by "picking up extra food, water, toiletries or medications". The Scottish Water number is given (see section 26).
- **RS-PLAN-PDF** p.2: "Bottled water." (no quantity).

**UK position:** Store 3 litres per person per day of drinking water for at least 3 days, which is 9 litres per person, bottled. **Disagreement:** BRC says "around 2.5 to 3 litres". UK-GOV says more water would be needed for cooking and washing but gives no figure for it. No source covers how to store or rotate water, beyond the general six-monthly expiry check (section 25), or how to treat water.

## 3. Food

- **UK-GOV** 091126: "Non-perishable food for 3 days". "Food with a long shelf life that doesn't need refrigeration or cooking e.g. tinned meat, fish or vegetables, dried fruit and nuts. It's best to buy food you'd usually eat." There is no tin opener in the web checklist.
- **UK-GOV-CHK** p.2: the same wording as the web page.
- **UK-GOV-PLAN** p.8: "Non-perishable food to last at least 3 days that does not require cooking or refrigerating (e.g. tinned meat, fruit or vegetables) and tin opener". Note that it says "fruit" where the web page says "fish", and it adds a tin opener.
- **BRC**
  - 091752: "Three days' supply of long-life or ready-to-eat food." "Tin opener."
  - 091832: "Non-perishable food to cover meals for everyone in your household for three days."
  - 091737: households already have "long-life food".
- **RS-KIT**: Not covered (not in the captured part). 091937 notes "rural areas are more likely to be cut off from the shops during snow".
- **RS-PLAN**: Not covered, apart from helping others by "picking up extra food" (092102).
- **RS-PLAN-PDF** p.2: "Ready to eat food." "Baby food/pet supplies."

**UK position:** Keep 3 days of non-perishable, ready-to-eat food that you would normally eat, plus a tin opener. The sources choose food that needs no cooking rather than giving any guidance on cooking without power. **None of the sources covers cooking without power** (camping stoves, safe indoor use and so on). There is a minor inconsistency inside UK-GOV: the tin opener appears in the plan PDF but not in the web page or checklist.

## 6. Communications (phones, contact lists, meeting points, ICE)

- **UK-GOV**
  - 091140: "Important phone numbers and information". "Write down contact details of who you'd need to contact in an emergency (including anyone who might need checking on) and a list of any regular medication and doses."
  - 091213 (meeting point): "Decide on a meeting point with family members in case you cannot return home or contact each other for a period of time. Choose places that are easy to find and open to the public in your community, or you might agree to meet at a family member's home if it is safe to do so."
  - 091213: agree a "backup plan" with vulnerable people: "if an outage happens and you cannot call, you or another trusted person they know will visit them in person".
  - 091224: "Write down important phone numbers on paper and store them somewhere easy to find in a power cut."
  - 091224 (ICE): "Add emergency contacts or medical information to your mobile phone (often called 'ICE' or 'Medical ID' on smartphones) so that emergency services can access this information without having to unlock your phone with a passcode – consider the sensitivity of the information you are including (anyone could see it who has access to your mobile phone) and let the person you've selected as your emergency contact know."
  - 091206: "Check if the place where you work or volunteer has a plan for what you should do if you cannot contact them during an emergency, for example in a power cut."
  - 091237: "Swap contact details with neighbours and consider creating an instant messaging group or other closed online social network for your street or building".

- **UK-GOV-PLAN**

- **BRC**

  - 091838 (flooding): "emergency contact information".
- **RS-KIT**: Not covered (not in the captured part).
- **RS-PLAN**
  - 092015: the plan should hold "your agreed meeting places" and "important numbers".
  - 092023/092028: "Agree a meeting place for everyone in your home. This can help you find each other if: you need to leave your home; someone cannot get home; mobile phone networks are disrupted. **UK position:** Keep a paper list of contacts, agree a meeting place (UK-GOV says a public place or a relative's home; Ready Scotland says close to home, sheltered and accessible), set up ICE or Medical ID on phones, and keep in touch with neighbours. **Difference:** UK-GOV allows a meeting place further away, such as a relative's home or a community hub, while Ready Scotland stresses one "close to home". Only UK-GOV mentions ICE. No source suggests a contact outside the area, a fallback meeting place, or texting rather than calling.

**UK position:** Check whether you can join the PSR (thepsr.co.uk; Customer Care Registers in Northern Ireland). RS-PLAN gives the fullest PSR eligibility list (adding "over 60"). The two sources agree on the PSR, and the Herbert Protocol appears only in Ready Scotland.

## Gaps and weaknesses in the UK guidance

1. **Three days only, with no option to go further.** Every source settles on 72 hours. None discusses longer outages (for example a prolonged loss of the power grid or water supply), or how to scale up for households that can afford to. The reason given is to "manage while support reaches those most in need", but the BRC page explaining why 3 days was recommended was not captured.
2. **Water guidance is thin.** The 3 L per person per day figure covers drinking only. UK-GOV says more is needed for cooking and washing but gives no figure, and BRC quotes 2.5–3 L, which is slightly inconsistent. There is nothing on storage containers, rotation intervals, filling baths or containers when a supply cut is forecast, or treating or boiling water, and no water allowance for pets.
3. **Sanitation is missing entirely.** No source explains what to do when toilets cannot be flushed (bin bags or a bucket toilet, flushing with stored water, disposing of waste). This is a significant gap for a 3-day water outage.
4. **Cooking and heating without mains power are not covered.** The guidance avoids the question by recommending food that needs no cooking. There is nothing on camping stoves, safe ventilation, alternative heating, or keeping one room warm.
5. **Carbon monoxide and generators are not covered.** No source mentions CO alarms, the danger of using barbecues, camping heaters or generators indoors, or candle safety beyond "torches are safer than candles". This is a safety gap, given that people improvise during power cuts.
6. **Shelter in place is generic.** "Go in, stay in, tune in" is the only advice. There is no guidance on chemical or radiological hazards: switching off ventilation, extractor fans and air conditioning, choosing an inner room, sealing gaps, or what to do if you were outside when exposed.
7. **No grab bag contents list.** UK-GOV recommends a "grab bag" but does not say what goes in it, and Ready Scotland says only "a waterproof bag".
8. **Evacuation detail is weak.** Rest centres are not mentioned. There is no advice on routes, keeping fuel in the car, where to stay, leaving a note, securing the house, or taking pets with you.
9. **No cash amount.** UK-GOV says the amount "depends on your family's specific needs", and Ready Scotland's captured material does not mention cash.
10. **The medication buffer is vague.** UK-GOV-PLAN says "several days", and there is no advice on keeping a rolling spare supply beyond ordering repeats 5 working days ahead. Keeping medicines cool in a power cut appears only in BRC's heatwave list ("items needed to safely store medication"), and storage at other times is not addressed.
11. **Psychological support is minimal.** There is only a link to "Coping with trauma" (not captured) and comments that preparation "reduces stress". There is nothing for adults on stress, sleep or helplines (for example Samaritans, Breathing Space).
12. **Inconsistencies between UK sources.**
    - Blankets and toiletries are core items for UK-GOV but "not essential" for BRC.
    - The tin opener appears in UK-GOV-PLAN and BRC but not in the UK-GOV web checklist or checklist PDF.
    - The web/checklist food examples say "fish" where the plan PDF says "fruit".
    - The meeting place is "close to home" for Ready Scotland but may be "a family member's home" or "community hub" for UK-GOV.
    - Water is 3 L (UK-GOV) versus 2.5–3 L (BRC).
13. **Missing numbers and services.**
    - UK-GOV gives no gas emergency number and no Floodline number for England and Wales.
    - Neither UK-GOV nor BRC mentions emergencySMS, which appears only in Ready Scotland.
    - No source gives a water supplier number outside Scotland.
    - No source names specific radio frequencies or tells people to find their local BBC station.
14. **Children and school.** The sources say to check the school has your contact details, but not what to agree about pick-up, who is authorised to collect, or what happens if a parent cannot get there.
15. **Digital resilience.** Only Ready Scotland warns about scams and misinformation. No source suggests offline copies of key information on the phone, cloud backups of documents, or ways to save phone battery.
16. **Gaps in the captured evidence (not the guidance itself).** The following were not captured: the rest of the UK-GOV "Alerts and warnings" page, all of UK-GOV's linked pages (hot and cold weather, storms, disabled persons, coping with trauma, local risks), BRC's "What if I can only add a few items" and FAQ sections, and nearly all of the RS-KIT page. Some of the gaps above may be covered on those pages.
