## 1. Go in, stay in, tune in (chemical and radiation)

### First steps if you were outside / may be contaminated
- **Chemical – "Remove, remove, remove"** (UKHSA-Chem; BeReady):
  - "Remove yourself from the immediate area … Fresh air is important." If close to a release, "move away as quickly as possible … ideally you should move upwind … avoiding the source".
  - "Remove your outer clothing if affected by the substance. Try to avoid pulling clothing over your head if possible. Do not smoke, eat or drink. Do not pull off clothing stuck to skin."
  - "Remove the substance from your skin using a dry absorbent material to either soak it up or brush it off. Rinse continually with water if the skin is itchy or painful." If irritating to skin, eyes or lungs, "wash with copious amounts of water"; if water is short, "concentrate on affected areas such as eyes or irritated skin".
  - "Decontaminate yourself first, then follow the same steps to help children or others."
  - "Simply taking off your outer clothing can remove around 90% of the contamination."
- **Radiation – self-decontamination** (UKHSA-Decon), only where you were outside near the release or are told to:
  - Get inside; "limit your contact with other people until you've decontaminated"; put on a clean face covering if one is to hand.
  - "Take off your outer layer of clothes and shoes. Cut off clothing where necessary to avoid removing anything over your head." Bag and seal clothes and "place it away from people".
  - "Mains water (water in the shower or taps) is safe to use. Shower if you can." Soap and shampoo, warm water; "Do not use conditioner as it may fix contaminants to your hair"; keep eyes and mouth closed; "Do not scrub your skin or wounds".
  - No shower: wash hands, face, hair and exposed skin at a sink; no tap: wipe exposed skin with damp cloth, wiping outwards from nose and mouth; blow your nose; bag the waste.
  - "Put on clean clothes that have been stored indoors."
  - Helping others or pets: "Wear waterproof gloves and a face mask if possible"; cover cuts; bag waste; rewash yourself afterwards.

### Go in
- Radiation: "go indoors as soon as you can and stay there. Close the doors and windows and stay as far away from external walls as possible." "Buildings made of brick, stone, concrete or similar materials provide the best protection, but being inside any building is better than being outside." "Vehicles and tents do not provide sufficient shelter." (UKHSA-Rad)
- Chemical: "go into any building, other than the same building as the chemical emergency"; vehicles "do not provide sufficient protection … can leak into the vehicle". (UKHSA-Chem)
- Shelter in the **nearest** building: "Do not travel home to shelter there as this may take too long" (UKHSA-Rad).
- Caravans/temporary buildings "offer some protection" if heating, water, food and toilets are available (both UKHSA pages).

### Stay in – sealing the house
- "Close external doors and windows." "Turn off any equipment which brings air from the outside into the building (such as fans or air conditioning) and close any air vents, including trickle vents in windows, if you can. You should also turn off any fans that are circulating air within the building." (UKHSA-Rad; same wording in UKHSA-Chem)
- "Safety-critical ventilation systems may be left on" (UKHSA-Rad/Chem).
- Sizewell: "Close all outside doors and window[s]"; "Switch off any ventilation or extractor fans" (DEPZ leaflet).
- **Heating:** neither UKHSA page mentions boilers or central heating specifically, only equipment that "brings air from the outside". A UK site leaflet goes further: "Switch off all heating systems, ventilation fans and air conditioning system to avoid drawing in outside air" – **[SITE-SPECIFIC UK]** Urenco Capenhurst safety advice, Cheshire West and Chester Council – https://www.cheshirewestandchester.gov.uk/your-council/emergency-planning/urenco-uk-ltd-capenhurst/safety-advice-for-residents. CDC also says turn off "forced-air heating units that bring air in from the outside" and "Close fireplace dampers" **[NON-UK]** – https://www.cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/response/stay-inside.html. Interpretation: a standard UK wet radiator system does not draw in outside air; a warm-air/forced-air system, heat-recovery ventilation, or an open fire/chimney does. Close chimney dampers or block the fireplace opening if you can.
- **How long:** radiation – "In most cases you will be advised to stay inside for one to two days after a release. This could be longer if there is a prolonged release" (UKHSA-Rad). Chemical – "You may be advised to stay inside for a number of hours. How long will depend on when the release is stopped, how the chemical spreads, and if any immediate clean up is needed" (UKHSA-Chem).
- After a chemical incident, when told it is over: "doors and windows should be opened to restore ventilation" – **[SITE-SPECIFIC UK]** Runcorn Site COMAH leaflet (Halton BC, valid Aug 2023–Aug 2028) – https://www.halton.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-07/COMAH%20PIZ%20LETTER%20%26%20SAFETY%20ADVICE.pdf
- Medicines: if you need regular medication, "seek advice from the NHS by contacting NHS 111 (online or by phone) rather than leaving the building" (both UKHSA pages).
- Shelter ideally where there is "heating, drinking water, food and toilets" (both UKHSA pages).
- Sheltering in a typical house "reduces the exposure from breathing in radioactive material by 40%, and reduces direct exposure by 85% compared to being outside" (UKHSA-Rad, planning principles).

### Children at school / family elsewhere
- "If your family or friends are not with you, for example if they are at school, nursery, hospital … do not go to collect them if you have been informed to stay indoors. Going outside to collect them could expose both you and them." Schools etc. "will be given advice … and will be asked to stay where they are until it is safe to move." (UKHSA-Rad; same in UKHSA-Chem)
- **Sizewell exception (precautionary stage):** "Schools and Childcare providers within the DEPZ will be advised to close immediately in the event of a radiation emergency. Existing school and childcare closure arrangements will be used for you to collect your child." (DEPZ leaflet). SRF plan: for the wider DEPZ beyond 1.35 km, messaging says "no immediate actions are required, but that families should collect children from schools and childcare facilities that will close as a precaution". Rule for the guide: collect only if officially told to; never once told to shelter.
- Evacuation checklist says gather household and pets "(excluding children at school)" (DEPZ leaflet).

### Pets
- Radiation: "If your pet is outside with you when you are advised to go in, bring them indoors with you. If you are indoors and you don't know where your pet is, do not go out to find them." Wash pets brought in or returning "with shampoo or soap and water as soon as possible", with "waterproof gloves and a face mask if possible"; if you cannot, keep them "in an area away from people". "Once you are indoors, do not let your pets out." (UKHSA-Rad)
- Chemical: same, but if neither you nor the pet is known to be contaminated, "you do not need to take any action to decontaminate them unless the emergency services have advised you to". To decontaminate: "wear gloves and ideally eye protection; rinse their eyes with water; wash them with water and shampoo or soap". (UKHSA-Chem)
- Sizewell: "Bring domestic pets indoors if it can be done quickly without spending too long outside"; "Leave all farm animals where they are" (DEPZ leaflet).

### Food and water
- "Food and drink that are already indoors, including tap water and food and drink in shops, will be safe to consume. Covered or sealed food … will be also free of contamination once it has been removed from its wrapping or containers." (UKHSA-Rad; same in UKHSA-Chem)
- "Mains water supplies (tap water) are unlikely to be affected immediately, as there is typically 2 to 3 days' worth of treated water within the mains system … considered safe to drink unless you are informed otherwise." Chemical page adds: "Apart from incidents directly impacting the water treatment infrastructure". (UKHSA-Rad/Chem)
- Private supplies, roof-collected water, shallow wells and water off tarpaulins may be contaminated; wait for advice. Further advice will be given on food stored outside "including food you have grown yourself". (UKHSA-Rad/Chem)
- Sizewell: safe – "Food stored indoors", "Medicines stored in their normal packaging", "Mains tap water, unless told otherwise"; wait for guidance before using private water, eating food stored outdoors, or "Harvesting fresh produce … from a garden or allotment" (DEPZ leaflet). OPZ leaflet: "avoid eating uncovered food, such as fresh vegetables from the garden"; "It should be safe to drink/use fresh tap water and to consume covered food and drink that you already have within your house."
- Practical extra from CDC **[NON-UK]**: "Boiling tap water will not remove radioactive materials" – https://www.cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/response/stay-inside.html

### Tune in / phone use
- "Monitor the news and follow official UK advice on GOV.UK … via the radio, television, internet, social media or the police." "Radiation cannot be seen, heard, smelt, tasted or felt, so it is important that you follow the advice given to you, even if you can't tell that anything has happened." (UKHSA-Rad). Chemical page: "Some chemicals cannot be seen, smelt, tasted or felt".
- Sizewell radio: BBC Radio Suffolk 103.9 and 95.5 FM; also Beach Radio 97.4 FM, BBC TV East, Anglia TV, SCC and Suffolk Police social media (DEPZ leaflet). Helpline 0345 603 2814 (DEPZ and OPZ leaflets).
- **Phone use:** no national UKHSA statement found. UK site leaflets say: "Avoid using the telephone or mobile telephones until the emergency is over to ensure that lines are free for the emergency services" (Runcorn COMAH leaflet, Halton BC, link above) and "Please try not to use telephones to keep lines clear for use by the emergency services" (Urenco Capenhurst, link above) – both **[SITE-SPECIFIC UK]**. Report a real emergency (e.g. symptoms, smell of fumes) on 999 as normal. CDC says take "your phone and charger cables" into the shelter room and use texts/social media to keep in touch **[NON-UK]** – https://www.cdc.gov/chemical-emergencies/response/shelter-in-place.html.

### When to evacuate instead
- "You should not evacuate unless advised to do so, as leaving shelter may increase your exposure." Evacuation "is used in situations where sheltering is not possible or there is the potential for people to receive higher radiation doses over prolonged periods if they are to remain inside", or as a precaution "if there is sufficient time to evacuate safely". Instructions will say what to do and where to go; you may need checking or self-decontamination before a rest centre. (UKHSA-Rad; chemical equivalent in UKHSA-Chem)
- Chemical exception: if *you* are close to a release outdoors, move away (upwind) first – "Remove yourself from the immediate area" (UKHSA-Chem). Do not shelter in the same building as the incident.
- Sizewell: "Evacuation is not an automatic countermeasure"; it is unlikely anyone "will be advised to evacuate immediately"; wider routes would be given "via local TV and radio, or by door to door visits" (DEPZ, OPZ leaflets). If told to leave: gather people and pets, warm clothes, special food, medicines, stable iodine tablets (if issued), documents, valuables; put out naked flames; turn off and unplug cookers, ventilation fans, TVs; lock up; "Leave using your own transport, keeping windows and ventilation closed"; go to the named rest centre or to friends outside the area and tell the police where you are (DEPZ leaflet).
- If you left before you knew: self-decontaminate as soon as you learn of it; do not return until told it is safe (UKHSA-Rad/Chem).

## 2. Stable iodine (potassium iodate) tablets

- **What it does:** "Taking stable iodine tablets at the right time blocks the absorption of radioactive iodine by the thyroid gland and speeds up the body's ability to remove radioactive iodine." Relevant to releases from reactors "that are operational, or have recently (within 90 days) shut down". (UKHSA-Rad)
- **What it does not do:** "Stable iodine does not protect against health effects from other sources of radiation"; "only protects the thyroid" (UKHSA-Rad). It is not a substitute for sheltering.
- **Only on official instruction:** "you will be given official advice from government or emergency services on how to get them, when to take them and how much to take. It is important that you only take the stable iodine tablets provided for this purpose." "Other sources of iodine such as iodised salt should not be taken in the place of stable iodine tablets, as they will not provide the necessary protection and they may be dangerous to your health." Read the patient information leaflet. (UKHSA-Rad). Sizewell: "only to be taken when advised to by automated telephone message, or other communications methods" (DEPZ leaflet).
- **Timing:** "A single dose provides protection for 24 hours, so taking the tablets too early may limit the length of time that you are protected for. Taking the tablets too late reduces the effectiveness … and could lead to more harm than not taking the tablets at all." "Most effective if taken between 24 hours before and 2 hours after exposure … can still be useful up to 8 hours after exposure." "Must not be taken for the first time more than 24 hours after an exposure" (UKHSA-Rad). SRF plan: "ideally within 6 hours of exposure".
- **Distance:** "Beyond this distance [an assessed distance of the reactor], the use of stable iodine is unlikely to benefit people" (UKHSA-Rad).
- **Who:** all ages. "Only one dose should be necessary and the tablets can be taken by any age." Exclusions: allergy to iodine, "Hypocomplementaemic Vasculitis or Dermatitis Herpetiformis" (ask GP if unsure). Babies under 1 month and women more than six months pregnant "should take the tablets as directed in the leaflet". (DEPZ leaflet). UKHSA iodate protocol: "Pregnant and breastfeeding individuals, neonates, infants and children are a priority for treatment"; dosing in pregnancy also protects the foetus; breastfeeding mothers should continue breastfeeding and babies still need their own dose; not to be started "24 hours or more" after exposure.
- **Dose if officially given** (UKHSA iodate protocol – potassium iodate 85 mg tablets, each = 50 mg iodine; a single dose, protects for up to 24 hours):
  - Adults and children over 12: 2 tablets (170 mg)
  - Children 3–12 years: 1 tablet (85 mg)
  - 1 month to under 3 years: ½ tablet (42.5 mg)
  - Birth to under 1 month: ¼ tablet (~21.25 mg; protocol prints "21.5mg")
  - Children: crush and mix with milk, water or juice (juice over 6 months), or jam, honey (over 1 year) or yoghurt; neonates: dissolve in a little milk or water.
  - Always follow the leaflet supplied with the actual tablets: the SRF plan glossary says UK stable iodine "is usually provided in the form of potassium iodate (KIO3) or potassium iodide (KI) tablets", and the Sizewell pack is described as "10 tablets or 5 adult doses" (SRF plan), consistent with 85 mg iodate at 2 tablets per adult, but the product is not named publicly.
- **How issued around Sizewell:**
  - Pre-issued to homes and businesses within 1.35 km (0.8 mile); keep them at the property; return to a pharmacy if you move (DEPZ leaflet; SCC Sizewell B page – https://www.suffolk.gov.uk/community-and-safety/emergency-planning/sizewell-b).
  - Beyond 1.35 km: "Additional stocks of these tablets are available to be issued following an emergency … under the advice of health professionals" (DEPZ leaflet); "arrangements are in place to provide additional tablets up to 15kms within 10-12hours" (SRF plan); "only people who are within 1.35km … would immediately be advised to stay indoors and to take pre-issued stable iodine tablets" (OPZ leaflet).
  - People within 1.35 km without shelter or tablets: Emergency Holding Area at Sizewell Sports & Social Club (SCC Sizewell B page).
- **Buying your own:** no UK official statement found either way. UK guidance is to take "only … the stable iodine tablets provided for this purpose" at the time and dose officially advised (UKHSA-Rad); the UK product is a Pharmacy-only (P) medicine (UKHSA iodate protocol). Interpretation for the guide: households outside 1.35 km do not need to buy tablets; supplies will be distributed if needed; do not take any iodine product on your own initiative. WHO also advises taking KI only on public health authority instruction **[NON-UK]** – https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/use-of-potassium-iodide-for-thyroid-protection-during-nuclear-or-radiological-emergencies (not re-read in full).

## 3. Which room to shelter in

- **UK national (radiation):** "As far as possible, stay in the middle of the building away from external doors, windows and walls, to avoid exposure from any radioactive material that has collected on the outside of the building." "Stay as far away from external walls as possible." "Gamma radiation can penetrate windows and to some extent walls, but … is much reduced by solid walls." (UKHSA-Rad). No UK national advice found on upstairs vs downstairs or basements.
- **UK national (chemical):** UKHSA-Chem gives no room-choice advice beyond going in and sealing the building.
- **UK site leaflets (chemical) [SITE-SPECIFIC UK]:** "Stay in a room away from the industrial area and if possible, go to an upper floor" (Runcorn COMAH leaflet, Halton BC, link above). "If possible go upstairs" (Urenco Capenhurst, hydrogen fluoride risk, link above). Reason (not stated in these leaflets): many toxic gases are heavier than air and collect at low level.
- **International [NON-UK]:**
  - CDC radiation: "Go to the basement or the middle of the building. Stay as far away from the walls and roof of the building as you can." – https://www.cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/response/get-inside.html
  - CDC chemical: a room "you can seal off from outside air" that is "large and has a source of water", "on the highest floor possible", with "no or few windows, be as far away as possible from the outside"; seal vents, windows and door with tape and plastic, or towels and clothing if not available. – https://www.cdc.gov/chemical-emergencies/response/shelter-in-place.html
- **Synthesis for a house where every room has windows (interpretation, not official text):** choose the room with the fewest and smallest windows, on the side of the house away from the incident if known, and stay away from the windows and outside walls within it (e.g. a landing, hallway or interior bathroom if usable). Radiation: the middle of the building; the ground floor of a two-storey house puts more roof and floors overhead, and a cellar is better if there is one (CDC only). Chemical: go upstairs (UK site leaflets and CDC); avoid cellars. For an unknown hazard, follow the instruction given; if none, middle of the house, door shut, away from windows.
