# Gap research: authoritative guidance for the household emergency plan

Date of research: 16/11/2026. All "accessed" dates below are 16/11/2026.

## How this research was done (read first)

- **Method limitation.** Direct page fetching was blocked in the research environment (GOV.UK and fire service hosts could not be reached). Every finding below therefore comes from **web search result summaries/snippets of the named pages**, not from reading the full pages. Quoted text is as returned in those snippets. Treat every figure as "reported by the named source; to be confirmed by opening the page" before printing it in the final plan.
- No personal details were used in any search.
- Layout per gap: **Verified (as reported by source)** = what an official/established source says; **Confidence and caveats**; **Implications for the household plan** = my interpretation, not source text.
- Retailer, campaign-group, news and overseas sources are labelled as such.

## 1. Fire escape from upper floors, escape ladders, pets, alarms, bedside kit

### Findings (as reported by source)

- **Get out, stay out; never go back.** Fire Kills campaign (GOV.UK/Home Office), "Escape" page – https://firekills.campaign.gov.uk/escape – accessed 16/11/2026. Under the UK "get out, stay out" guidance: never go back for possessions, pets or people; tell firefighters immediately if anyone is missing.
- **Tell firefighters about pets left inside rather than going back.** PDSA (veterinary charity), "PDSA warning of hidden household fire risks" – https://www.pdsa.org.uk/press-office/latest-news/pdsa-warning-of-hidden-household-fire-risks – accessed 16/11/2026. Advises telling firefighters if you believe an animal is still inside.
- **If trapped upstairs: close the door, block the gap, go to the window, call 999.**
  - Staffordshire FRS, "Get Out Stay Out" – https://www.staffordshirefire.gov.uk/your-safety/campaigns/get-out-stay-out – accessed 16/11/2026: "close the door, and put bedding or any soft materials around the bottom of the door to block the smoke"; open the window, shout for help.
  - Devon & Somerset FRS, "How to make an escape plan" – https://dsfire.gov.uk/node/541 – accessed 16/11/2026: choose a safe room with a window that opens easily and a phone; "choose a room where you could be visible and heard by a passer-by"; keep a phone and torch in the bedroom at bedtime.
  - Nottinghamshire FRS fire survival guidance – https://www.notts-fire.gov.uk/community/in-an-emergency/fire-survival-guidance – accessed 16/11/2026: when calling 999 give the whole address including town and postcode, say if anyone is trapped and which room.
  - Warwickshire FRS, "If your escape route is blocked" – https://www.warwickshire.gov.uk/fire-safety-home/making-home-escape-plan/4 – accessed 16/11/2026: stay low (crawl) if there is smoke.
- **When to use a window.** Northamptonshire FRS escape advice – https://www.northantsfire.gov.uk/?p=13018 – accessed 16/11/2026 (snippet only): use a window only on the **ground or first floor** when the normal route is blocked; higher than that, wait to be rescued; "lower yourself down before dropping rather than jumping". Surrey FRS – https://surreycc.gov.uk/people-and-community/fire-and-rescue/home-safety/advice – accessed 16/11/2026: makes the same first-floor distinction; on upper floors "don't jump – wait for rescue"; stay by the window so firefighters can see you.
- **Suffolk FRS own leaflet.** Suffolk County Council, "Suffolk fire safety in the home" leaflet – https://www.suffolk.gov.uk/asset-library/imported/suffolk-fire-safety-in-the-home-a5-6pp-v2-lr.pdf – accessed 16/11/2026: keep door and window keys handy and tell household members where they are; practise the escape plan with everyone; unplug appliances at night. The snippet did not show phone/torch-by-bed advice. Suffolk FRS Prevention Team number given in search results: 01473 260588 (not verified on the page).
- **Phone by the bed.** Northumberland FRS night planning – https://www.northumberlandfireandrescue.gov.uk/night-planning – accessed 16/11/2026: keep a mobile within reach in bed, torch easy to grab. South Yorkshire FRS – https://www.syfire.gov.uk/safety-advice/how-to-prevent-house-fires – accessed 16/11/2026: "if you have a mobile, take it to bed with you". Fire Scotland bedtime checks – https://www.firescotland.gov.uk/at-home/bedtime-checks/ – accessed 16/11/2026.
- **Smoke and heat alarms (NFCC).** NFCC Home Detection and Assistive Technology Position Statement, July 2021 – https://nfcc.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/NFCC_Home_Detection_and_Assistive_Technology__Position_Statement_July_2021.pdf – accessed 16/11/2026: minimum of one smoke alarm on each floor in a circulation space (hall/landing); a heat alarm in every kitchen; all alarms ceiling-mounted and **interlinked**. NFCC describes these as recommendations/best practice, not law in England.
- **BS 5839-6** is the code of practice for domestic fire detection (best practice, not law). Secondary sources disagree on which category it recommends for existing homes (LD2 vs LD3). Not verified against the standard itself.
- **Escape ladders – official view.** I found **no UK fire and rescue service or NFCC guidance that recommends or sets standards for domestic escape ladders**. Official UK advice centres on getting out by the normal route, the safe-room approach, and lowering then dropping from first-floor windows.
- **Escape ladders – product standards (non-official sources).**
  - Ladder Association/British Safety Council article "Stepping up to the standard" – https://www.britsafe.org/safety-management/2021/stepping-up-to-the-standard – accessed 16/11/2026: ladders in the UK should now meet BS EN 131 (Professional and Non-Professional classes); BS 2037/BS 1129 withdrawn. **EN 131 is a general ladder standard, not a fire-escape-ladder standard.**
  - Safelincs (retailer) product pages – https://www.safelincs.co.uk/product/saffold-fold-out-fire-escape-ladder – accessed 16/11/2026: fixed fold-out ladders "load tested to BS EN 131-2: 2010 by SGS". **Retailer source.**
  - Smartasaker/Housegard (retailer) – https://www.smartasaker.com/en/fire-escape-ladder – accessed 16/11/2026: one portable ladder's "maximum evacuation height is 4,7 metres", only for windows that open fully, within a set frame width. **Retailer source.**
  - Some European portable escape ladders are sold as meeting EN 14043/EN 14044 or similar – **I could not verify any applicable standard for rope escape ladders.**
- **Escape ladders – practising (overseas sources only).** Seattle Fire Department "Fire Escape Ladders" – https://seattle.gov/Documents/Departments/Fire/Safety/FireEscapeLadders.pdf – accessed 16/11/2026 (**US**): ladder is a backup, not a primary route; rated "for at least 1,000 pounds"; have standoffs holding rungs off the wall; practise setting it up **from a ground/first-floor window**; "don't test ladders in your fire drills" (injury risk). ESFI (US) escape ladder tips – https://www.esfi.org/escape-ladder-safety-tips/ – accessed 16/11/2026.
- **Windows that open only at the top.** Safelincs FAQ (retailer) – https://safelincs.co.uk/evacuation-equipment-faqs/382/my-window-only-opens-at-the-top-can-i-use-a-fire-escape-ladder – accessed 16/11/2026: recommends an emergency escape hammer next to the window if the lower pane must be broken.
- **Throwing items out.** fireservice.co.uk (commercial site, not a fire service) suggests throwing a mattress out of the window to cushion a landing. No official source found on throwing out a "chuck bag".
- **Free home fire safety visit.** Many FRSs, including Suffolk, offer free home fire safety visits and help with escape plans (multiple FRS pages above).

### Confidence and caveats

- **Definitive:** get out/stay out; never go back for pets; close door and block gaps; call 999 with full address and room; phone (and torch) by the bed; NFCC alarm recommendation (each floor, heat alarm in kitchen, interlinked).
- **Fairly consistent across FRSs:** windows are for ground/first floor only; lower yourself then drop; higher up, wait for rescue.
- **Not found / unclear:** any UK official endorsement, standard or load rating for *rope* escape ladders; any official advice on escaping down a ladder with a dog; any official advice on throwing a bag out. The 1,000 lb load rating is US advice and should not be presented as a UK requirement.
- England's legal alarm minimums for owner-occupiers were not verified (Scotland's 2022 interlinked-alarm law is separate and does not apply).

### Implications for the household plan (my interpretation)

- The ladder is a **last-resort** route; the plan should first say: close bedroom door, block gap, call 999 (say which room, that dogs are present, that you intend to use a ladder), open window, be visible.
- Because the bedroom is (presumably) first floor, official "lower and drop" advice is an alternative if the ladder fails; note it.
- Check the ladder's own stated maximum load and height. Assume the manufacturer's rating is for **one adult**; adult-plus-dog loads, and a dog's ability to be carried down a swinging rope ladder, are not covered by any guidance found. Consider a dog harness with handle, and practise only at ground level (dog getting used to the window/ladder), never full descents.
- Ask Suffolk FRS for a free home fire safety visit and raise the ladder and dog question with them directly.
- "Never go back in for the dogs" must be explicit. If a dog cannot be got out, tell firefighters exactly where it is.
- Chuck bag: no official guidance; keep it light and soft and throw it clear of the ladder landing area only once the escape route is decided (my suggestion).
- Bedside: phone (and charger/power bank), torch, keys for windows, glasses, shoes/coat; dog leads/harness near the escape window.
- Alarms: aim for NFCC standard: interlinked smoke alarms on every floor (landing/hall), heat alarm in kitchen; consider smoke alarms in the living room and bedrooms (BS 5839-6 approach), particularly where a downstairs fire is the threat.

## 2. Sizewell: off-site emergency arrangements (REPPIR 2019) and radiation advice

### Findings (as reported by source)

- **Who plans.** Under REPPIR 2019 the local authority must produce the off-site emergency plan; for Sizewell this is **Suffolk County Council**, working with the Suffolk Resilience Forum (SRF) and operators. Source: Suffolk County Council Sizewell B page – https://www.suffolk.gov.uk/community-and-safety/emergency-planning/sizewell-b – accessed 16/11/2026; REPPIR 2019 – https://legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2019/703 – accessed 16/11/2026.
- **Published plan.** SRF "Radiation Emergency Plan" (public version), Issue 4.2 – https://suffolkprepared.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/PUBLIC-SRF-Radiation-Emergency-Plan-Issue-4.2-1.pdf – accessed 16/11/2026. SRF radiation advice page – https://suffolkprepared.co.uk/get-prepared/risk-advice/radiation/ – accessed 16/11/2026.
- **Zones.**
  - **DEPZ:** Suffolk Prepared radiation page (per search summary) describes the DEPZ as roughly **3–4 km** from the station and including **Leiston**. EDF Sizewell B Consequences Report v2, Jan 2020 – https://www.edfenergy.com/sites/default/files/szb_consequences_report_v2_jan_2020.pdf – accessed 16/11/2026: recommended minimum DEPZ "no smaller than 1350m" from the centre point; the council then sets the actual boundary (which follows geographic features, so it is larger than the minimum). Older figure of 2.4 km (ONR 2012 letter) is historical.
  - **Stable iodine pre-issue zone:** Suffolk County Council Sizewell B page: "Residents and businesses within 1.35km of Sizewell B are provided with stable iodine tablets in advance."
  - **Outline Planning Zone (OPZ):** Suffolk County Council Sizewell B page and EDF Consequences Report: OPZ of **about 30 km**. SRF OPZ leaflet (Oct 2020) "During any Sizewell radiation emergency" – https://suffolkprepared.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/OPZ-leaflet-Oct-20.pdf – accessed 16/11/2026: covers people beyond the DEPZ within the 30 km OPZ and a **15 km Extended Emergency Planning Zone** (the latter term reported in the snippet; not verified on the leaflet itself).
- **Stable iodine tablets.** Suffolk County Council / SRF: tablets pre-issued within 1.35 km; DEPZ residents receive information about tablets "where appropriate"; tablets "should only be taken when instructed during a radiation emergency"; further stocks distributed if needed. Sizewell C DCO emergency plan (public, Rev 2) – https://www.aldeburghtowncouncil.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/DCO_Requirement_6_Emergency_Plan_Public_Rev_2_Clean.pdf – accessed 16/11/2026: stable iodine "would not provide protection against" other radionuclides; mentions an Emergency Holding Area at Sizewell Sports & Social Club. The household-dose and potassium iodide vs potassium iodate formulation for the public were **not verified**.
- **How warnings are given.** Suffolk Prepared radiation page (per search summary): DEPZ residents follow directions given by **telephone warning**; people outside the DEPZ should **listen to local radio** and follow guidance. An EADT news article (undated in results) – https://www.eadt.co.uk/news/residents-get-new-nuclear-warning-system-7445428 – reported an automated system able to send about 1,500 calls a minute (**news source; currency not verified**). The national **Emergency Alerts** system (cell broadcast to 4G/5G phones in range, loud alarm even on silent) – https://www.gov.uk/alerts – accessed 16/11/2026 – can be used by government and emergency services; I found no Sizewell-specific statement about its use.
- **National radiation advice.** GOV.UK/UKHSA "What to do in a radiation emergency" (last updated 10 September 2024) – https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/radiation-emergencies-information-for-the-public/what-to-do-in-a-radiation-emergency – accessed 16/11/2026: "In most situations, if you are in the affected area, you will be advised to **go in, stay in and tune in**"; "You may need to stay indoors for **one to two days**"; do not go to collect children from school if told to stay indoors; advice comes via TV, radio, internet, social media or police. Related Prepare page – https://prepare.campaign.gov.uk/be-informed-about-hazards/hazardous-materials-incident – accessed 16/11/2026: close doors and windows, turn off fans/ventilation. UKHSA easy-read decontamination leaflet – https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/65670ab3d6ad75001302fc6d/How_to_remove_radioactive_material_from_your_skin_and_clothes__decontamination__-_EASY_READ-2.pdf – accessed 16/11/2026.

### Confidence and caveats

- **Definitive:** Suffolk County Council owns the off-site plan; OPZ about 30 km; stable iodine pre-issued within 1.35 km; take tablets only when told; go in, stay in, tune in.
- **Unclear:** exact DEPZ boundary (the "3–4 km" figure is a summary of the SRF page; the boundary is a mapped area, not a circle). Check the SCC DEPZ map. The "15 km extended zone" wording needs checking on the leaflet. Whether the telephone warning system is still the primary DEPZ method, and how people sign up, was not confirmed. Public tablet dose/formulation not found.
- **Sizewell C** (under construction) will have its own future arrangements; not researched in depth.
- The SRF OPZ leaflet is dated October 2020; newer versions may exist.

### Implications for the household plan (my interpretation)

- "Some miles" from Sizewell almost certainly places the household **outside the DEPZ but inside the 30 km OPZ**. Confirm with the SCC DEPZ map. If outside the DEPZ: no pre-issued tablets, no automatic phone warning; rely on Emergency Alerts, local radio (BBC Radio Suffolk) and Suffolk Prepared.
- Plan action: go in, stay in, tune in: dogs indoors, windows/doors shut, ventilation off, battery/wind-up radio, enough supplies for 1–2 days indoors. Do not travel to collect people unless advised.
- This overlaps with the **power-cut** risk: a radio that works without mains is essential.
- Download and keep the SRF OPZ leaflet with the plan.

## 3. Flooding, route planning and being cut off

### Findings (as reported by source)

- **Flood warnings sign-up.** GOV.UK "Get flood warnings by text, phone or email" – https://www.gov.uk/sign-up-for-flood-warnings – accessed 16/11/2026: free; warnings for flooding from rivers, the sea or groundwater; **"you cannot get flood warnings for surface water flooding"**; not available in all areas.
- **Floodline:** **0345 988 1188** (24 hours); textphone **0345 602 6340**. Can sign up/update by phone. (Multiple council pages and GOV.UK snippets; older 0845 number is outdated.)
- **Warning levels.** GOV.UK "Flood alerts and warnings: what they are and what to do" – https://check-for-flooding.service.gov.uk/what-to-do-in-a-flood – accessed 16/11/2026: flood alert = prepare; flood warning = take action; **severe flood warning = danger to life**.
- **Long-term flood risk.** GOV.UK "Check your long term flood risk" (rivers, sea, surface water, reservoirs, groundwater where data available) and "check for flooding in the next 5 days" – via https://check-for-flooding.service.gov.uk and https://www.gov.uk/check-long-term-flood-risk – accessed 16/11/2026.
- **Personal flood plan.** GOV.UK "Personal flood plan" template – https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/personal-flood-plan – accessed 16/11/2026: stages: before, getting help, flood kit, immediately before, during, after. Cambridgeshire County Council – https://www.cambridgeshire.gov.uk/flood-prepare – accessed 16/11/2026: include evacuation routes, safe places, contacts, plans for pets and vulnerable members.
- **Driving through flood water.** National Water Safety Forum flooding safety advice 2025 – https://www.nationalwatersafety.org.uk/media/1491/wse-flooding-safety-advice-2025.pdf – accessed 16/11/2026: "it takes just **30cm** of water" for a car to float. The AA – https://theaa.com/driving-advice/seasonal/driving-through-flood-water – accessed 16/11/2026: "**60cm** of standing water will float your car". Shropshire FRS – https://shropshirefire.gov.uk/flooding – accessed 16/11/2026: 30 cm can move a vehicle. Environment Agency advice (quoted second-hand): do not drive into water that is moving or **more than 10cm** deep; "30cm of flowing water can move a car and 60cm can float it". **The EA original page was not retrieved.** Walking: as little as 15 cm of fast-flowing water can knock a person off their feet (Autotrader, motoring site; commonly cited by EA as "15cm/6 inches"; not verified on GOV.UK).
- **Coastal/tidal surge.** EA press release on tidal surge (Broads) – https://www.gov.uk/government/news/tidal-surge-to-cause-potential-flooding-on-the-broads – accessed 16/11/2026: surges caused by a low-pressure system and strong winds in the North Sea. EA issues separate warning areas for Suffolk coast stretches (e.g. Southwold; Felixstowe to Clacton incl. Orwell and Stour; tidal Deben; Felixstowe Ferry/Bawdsey Quay) – reported via third-party aggregators (riverlevels.uk, floodradar.co.uk: **not official**); live status on the GOV.UK flood service.
- **Emergency Alerts** may be used for severe flooding (gov.uk/alerts; operator pages).

### Confidence and caveats

- **Definitive:** sign-up route, Floodline number, warning levels, no warnings for surface water flooding.
- **Disputed:** the 60 cm figure is variously given as standing or flowing water; the 10 cm limit is attributed to the EA but not seen on an EA page. The safest message (do not drive through flood water) is consistent.
- **Not found:** official GOV.UK wording on "what to do if cut off/stay upstairs" (the "help during a flood" page was not retrieved). One AA snippet suggested staying in a stranded car; **this conflicts with usual advice to get out and onto higher ground if water is rising** – not relied on.
- Whether the household's location is covered by a flood warning area was not checked (would need the address – deliberately not searched).

### Implications for the household plan (my interpretation)

- Sign up for warnings for every warning area covering the **access roads**, not just the house: the risk is being cut off, not flooded.
- Use the long-term flood risk map and local knowledge to mark which lanes flood and an alternative route; record "decision points" (e.g. leave before a flood warning becomes active on the access road).
- Because surface water flooding is not warned, heavy-rain Met Office warnings should also be a trigger.
- If cut off: stay put on high ground, conserve supplies (links to power and water sections), do not drive or walk through water, call 999 only for danger to life.
- Rule for drivers: **never drive into flood water**; 30 cm can float a car.

## 4. Long power cuts (rural, overhead lines)

### Findings (as reported by source)

- **105.** Oxfordshire County Council utility failure page – https://oxfordshire.gov.uk/node/7457 – accessed 16/11/2026: "call 105", free, puts you through to the local network operator (UK Power Networks for Suffolk). Not available in Northern Ireland.
- **UKPN Priority Services Register (PSR).** UKPN "Priority Services Register (PSR) extra support during a power cut" – https://www.ukpowernetworks.co.uk/extra-support – accessed 16/11/2026: "24/7 priority when calling us to report a power cut"; password scheme to verify staff. Register: **0800 169 9970**, psr@ukpowernetworks.co.uk, or online (per Bromley Council – https://www.bromley.gov.uk/emergencies/emergency-planning/8 – accessed 16/11/2026). Register with both the network operator and your energy supplier, as they provide different services (Selectra – **comparison site**).
- **PSR eligibility (general Ofgem-based criteria; not UKPN's own list).** SSEN PSR page and CPAG – https://askcpag.org.uk/?id=-260864 – accessed 16/11/2026: reliance on medical equipment (oxygen concentrators, home dialysis, stair lifts, ventilators, nebulisers), refrigerated medicines, **State Pension age**, children under five, chronic illness, disability, long-term health condition; temporary circumstances (e.g. after surgery) may also qualify. "If unsure, ask, it is free."
- **Digital landlines.** Ofcom "Upgrading landlines to digital technology" – https://www.ofcom.org.uk/phones-and-broadband/landline-phones/upgrading-landlines-to-digital-technology – accessed 16/11/2026: a phone connected to a broadband router "will not work in a power cut, as the router gets its power from the mains". Ofcom guidance (per Ofcom's 2023 page "Protecting customers during the migration to digital landlines" – https://www.ofcom.org.uk/news-centre/2023/protecting-customers-during-the-migration-to-digital-landlines – and Ofcom FOI documents): providers must offer customers who **depend on their landline** (no mobile alternative, e.g. no signal) at least one solution keeping access to emergency services for **a minimum of one hour** during a power cut, **free of charge** (e.g. a battery backup unit).
- **Switch-off timetable.** The national PSTN close-down target is **31 January 2027** (multiple trade/news sources: TechRadar, ISPreview, City AM; e.g. https://www.cityam.com/bt-delays-public-network-switchover-until-2027-despite-increasingly-fragile-old-tech/ – accessed 16/11/2026). **Not confirmed on GOV.UK/Ofcom pages.**
- **Provider practice.** BT battery backup gives about one extra hour; Vodafone (press release 14 March 2025 – https://www.vodafone.co.uk/newscentre/press-release/broadband-battery-backup/ – accessed 16/11/2026) claims "up to 4-7 hours" for its newer device. **Provider sources.**
- **Mobile masts in a power cut.** Ofcom "Mobile RAN power resilience" technical report, 10 February 2025 – https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/consultations/category-1-10-weeks/272921-resilience-guidance-and-mobile-ran-power-back-up/associated-documents/mobile-ran-power-resilience-technical-report-cfi-update.pdf – accessed 16/11/2026: per search summary, about **20% of mast sites have at least one hour** of battery backup and about **5% at least six hours**; one-hour backup at all sites estimated at £0.9bn–£1.8bn (via bratby.law summary – **law-firm source**). A law-firm summary – https://bratby.law/network-resilience-guidance-mobile-power-backup/ – reports Ofcom revised its Network and Service Resilience Guidance (June 2026) to add expectations for mobile power backup **without a fixed minimum number of hours**. **These figures are not verified against the Ofcom report text and should be treated as provisional.**
- **Generators and carbon monoxide.** HSENI (Health and Safety Executive for Northern Ireland) "Safety tips using a portable generator in an emergency" – https://www.hseni.gov.uk/news/safety-tips-using-portable-generator-emergency – accessed 16/11/2026: do not operate near open doors and windows; fans will not prevent CO build-up; CO is "completely colourless and odourless"; fit CO alarms. Ards and North Down Council – https://www.ardsandnorthdown.gov.uk/Portable-heaters-and-generators – accessed 16/11/2026: use only in well-ventilated areas; CO alarms in the home. Overseas sources (Victoria, Australia; US): never run indoors or in garages; **never backfeed the house via a socket** (danger to neighbours and line workers); don't refuel when hot. No GOV.UK/HSE (GB) page was returned.
- **Food in fridges and freezers.** Food Standards Agency "Food safety in a power cut: advice for consumers" – https://www.food.gov.uk/safety-hygiene/food-safety-in-a-power-cut-advice-for-consumers – accessed 16/11/2026: fridge should stay cold for **up to 4 hours** if kept shut; a **full freezer about 48 hours, half full about 24 hours** (estimates). Food Standards Scotland – https://www.foodstandards.gov.scot/consumers/food-safety/at-home/power-cuts – accessed 16/11/2026: if a fridge reaches **8°C** or more, eat chilled food within four hours (or discard). If unsure, throw it out.
- **Water during power cuts.** Not specifically found. Mains water usually continues for some time (company pumping stations have backup), but **I could not verify** any Anglian Water/Essex & Suffolk Water statement on this. Homes on a private borehole/pumped supply lose water when the pump loses power (DWI guidance notes "loss of power" as a supply risk – https://www.dwi.gov.uk/private-water-supplies/guidance-documents/pws-regulations/protecting-your-private-water-supply – accessed 16/11/2026).

### Confidence and caveats

- **Definitive:** 105; PSR exists and is free; digital landlines fail in a power cut without backup; Ofcom one-hour minimum free solution for landline-dependent customers; FSA 4 h/48 h/24 h figures.
- **Provisional:** mast backup percentages and the June 2026 Ofcom guidance change (secondary summaries only). PSTN date of 31 January 2027 from trade press.
- **Not found:** UKPN's own eligibility wording; a GB (HSE/GOV.UK) generator page; water company statements on power cuts.

### Implications for the household plan (my interpretation)

- Assume a long rural power cut could also take out the **home phone and broadband within about an hour** (if on a digital line with a battery unit; immediately if none) and **mobile signal within hours**, as the local mast may have little or no backup. The plan needs a no-mains communication fallback: charged power banks, car charger, battery/wind-up radio, and an agreed "if we can't be reached, we do X" rule.
- Check whether the line is already digital, ask the provider for a battery backup, and ask about longer-life units if mobile signal is poor (you may qualify as landline-dependent).
- Consider PSR registration (UKPN and supplier) if anyone qualifies; registration with the water company is separate.
- Generator: outdoors only, well away from doors/windows, CO alarm inside, never connect to a house socket (a qualified electrician must fit a changeover switch).
- Freezer: keep it full (bottles of water fill gaps and become ice blocks), keep doors shut, fridge thermometer, 8°C rule.

## 5. Loss of water supply

### Findings (as reported by source)

- **Essex & Suffolk Water** "How we manage incidents" – https://www.eswater.co.uk/managingincidents – accessed 16/11/2026: bottled water stations set up for prolonged incidents; Priority Services customers who cannot get to a station can have bottled water delivered. Free delivery to PSR customers when an outage is expected to last longer than **three hours** (one ESW page) or **six hours** (another page) – **conflicting**.
- **Anglian Water** "Alternative water supplies" – https://www.anglianwater.co.uk/help-and-advice/emergencies/alternative-water-supplies – and FAQ "I'm on the Priority Services Register and I have no water" – https://faq.anglianwater.co.uk/article/qed94997 – accessed 16/11/2026: for longer/larger incidents, bottled water delivered (sometimes by contractor Water Direct), priority to PSR customers with a medical need or who cannot reach a collection hub. Anglian PSR has 500,000+ customers (Anglian news – https://www.anglianwater.co.uk/news/500000-priority-services-register). Register: https://anglianwater.co.uk/priority.
- **Storing water.** GOV.UK Prepare "Get prepared for emergencies" – https://prepare.campaign.gov.uk/get-prepared-for-emergencies – accessed 16/11/2026: "A minimum of **2.5-3 litres** of drinking water per person per day is recommended by the World Health Organisation for survival. **10 litres** per person per day will make you more comfortable by also providing for basic cooking and hygiene needs." GOV.UK Prepare "Water supply interruptions or outages" – https://prepare.campaign.gov.uk/be-informed-about-hazards/water-supply-interruptions-or-outages – accessed 16/11/2026: keep some bottled water for essential needs. Press coverage of the launch (2025) framed the household kit as **three days'** supply.
- **Norfolk Prepared** "Loss of water supply" – https://www.norfolkprepared.gov.uk/article/63773/Loss-of-water-supply – accessed 16/11/2026: check PSR eligibility and register before an outage.
- **DWI on storing tap water:** no DWI guidance on how long to store tap water was found.

### Confidence and caveats

- **Definitive:** water companies run bottled water stations and prioritise PSR customers; Prepare figures of 2.5–3 L survival and 10 L comfortable per person per day.
- **Unclear:** ESW 3-hour vs 6-hour delivery threshold; which company supplies the household (Suffolk is split between Anglian Water and Essex & Suffolk Water – check the bill).
- **Not found:** official tap-water storage durations; any figure for **dog** water needs (not in official sources).
- If the household is cut off by flood water, a bottled water station may be unreachable; no source addressed this.

### Implications for the household plan (my interpretation)

- Store water for people **and dogs** for at least three days; because the household can be cut off, consider more. Use the 10 L/person/day comfort figure for planning, 3 L minimum for drinking.
- Note the water company name, incident line and PSR status in the plan.
- If on a private supply/borehole, a power cut = no water, so the water store matters even more.

## 6. Dogs in emergencies

### Findings (as reported by source)

- **RSPCA** "Keeping animals safe during floods" – https://rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/seasonal/floods – accessed 16/11/2026: have carriers/transport ready and food for you and your animals in case of evacuation; when leaving take pet food, bowls, bedding and medication; dogs on sturdy leads; keep dogs indoors and away from rising water; "many dogs are not natural swimmers and can panic"; evacuate early and follow official routes; never put your own life at risk to rescue an animal. RSPCA line **0300 1234 999** (hours reported inconsistently: 24 h vs 8am–6pm).
- **Rest centres.** Stroud District Council pet preparedness plan (Aug 2023) – https://www.stroud.gov.uk/media/xn5d3qor/pet-preparedness-plan-august-2023.pdf – accessed 16/11/2026: assistance animals "will, of course, be permitted"; other animals "may not be able to be accommodated" depending on the centre. Staffordshire Prepared "Pet Advice" – https://www.staffordshireprepared.gov.uk/Preparing-yourself/Pet-Advice – accessed 16/11/2026: will try to accommodate pets "as far as reasonably practicable… although this is not always possible"; each local authority has its own policy; keep a list of boarding kennels. Norfolk Prepared "Prepare your pets for emergencies" – https://norfolkprepared.gov.uk/article/67035/Prepare-your-pets-for-emergencies – accessed 16/11/2026: "not all rest centres or hotels will be able to accept your pets"; arrange a "pet buddy".
- **Pet grab bag.** Medway Council "Looking after your pets in an emergency" – https://www.medway.gov.uk/info/200220/dogs_and_pests/1444/looking_after_your_pets_in_an_emergency – accessed 16/11/2026: a grab bag light enough for anyone to carry with medication, food, vaccination certificates, stored somewhere easy to reach. Staffordshire Prepared: food and water for several days; physical copy of registration and microchip details in a waterproof container; microchipping dogs is a legal requirement; keep microchip details up to date.
- **Fire and pets:** see Gap 1 (never go back; tell firefighters).
- **Dogs Trust / Blue Cross:** no pages returned by searches; their guidance was **not verified**.
- **Suffolk-specific rest-centre pet policy:** **not found**.

### Confidence and caveats

- **Definitive:** never risk life for a pet; rest centres do not guarantee to take pets (assistance dogs excepted); pet grab bag and up-to-date microchip details recommended.
- **Not found:** Suffolk County Council/SRF policy on pets in rest centres; official advice on dogs and radiation sheltering (my assumption: bring dogs indoors with you).

### Implications for the household plan (my interpretation)

- Assume a rest centre **may not take the dogs**: arrange a pet buddy and a kennel/boarding fallback, ideally one reachable without crossing flood-prone roads.
- Dog grab bag: leads, harnesses (with handles, also useful for the ladder), 3+ days of food and water, bowls, medication, vaccination record, microchip number and vet details in a waterproof pouch, poo bags, towel, a recent photo.
- Check microchip contact details are current.
- In radiation sheltering, bring the dogs in early; in flooding, keep them on leads (risk of swimming/panic).
