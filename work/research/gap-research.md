# Gap research: authoritative guidance for the household emergency plan

**Version 0.2.** Date of research and of every "accessed" date below: 16/11/2026.

## Change log

- **v0.2 (16/11/2026).** Every finding in v0.1 rechecked against the live pages, now that GOV.UK and most official sites can be reached. Each finding now carries a status: ✅ Verified, ✏️ Corrected or ❓ Still unverified. Most important corrections:
  1. **Sizewell telephone warning.** The automated phone warning (EDF's "PETIS") covers **registered residents within 1.35 km** of Sizewell B. It does **not** cover the whole DEPZ. Beyond 1.35 km the plan pre-agrees **no immediate action**. The SRF plan also says an **Emergency Alert** (mobile cell broadcast) will be sent to the DEPZ.
  2. **Sizewell DEPZ "3–4 km incl. Leiston"** is the official leaflet's own wording, so it is now verified. The SRF plan also confirms a **15 km Extended Emergency Planning Zone (EEPZ)**, with arrangements to extend sheltering and stable iodine up to 15 km within 10–12 hours.
  3. **Stable iodine.** UKHSA timing and dosing advice has been added: one dose protects for 24 hours, and it must not be taken for the first time more than 24 hours after exposure. The Sizewell leaflet says a single dose should be enough and the tablets can be taken at any age. The SRF plan's glossary names potassium iodate **or** potassium iodide; the formulation actually issued at Sizewell is still not stated publicly.
  4. **NFCC alarms.** The "every circulation space + kitchen heat alarm + all ceiling-mounted + all interlinked" list in v0.1 is **Scotland's legal requirement**, quoted inside the NFCC statement. It is not NFCC's own recommendation. NFCC's own wording is: a smoke alarm on every floor as a minimum; additional alarms in regularly used rooms, based on risk; a heat alarm in the kitchen; interlinked "where possible".
  5. **Official window advice found.** The GOV.UK / Home Office "Fire safety in the home" guidance says that on the ground or first floor you may be able to escape through a window: "Use bedding to cushion your fall and lower yourself down carefully. Don't jump." Several fire services say to throw bedding or cushions out first. v0.1 said no official source covered throwing items out; that is now corrected.
  6. **10 cm driving figure** is the **AA's** advice, not the Environment Agency's. The **EA** figures are 30 cm of flowing water to float a car (EA press releases) and, on GOV.UK "Flash flooding", about 15 cm to knock a person over and about 60 cm to carry away a vehicle of any size.
  7. **Water storage.** The GOV.UK preparedness site (now "Be ready", beready.campaign.gov.uk) says **3 litres per person per day for 3 days**. The "2.5–3 L WHO / 10 L comfort" wording in v0.1 is no longer on the site.
  8. **Essex & Suffolk Water** delivery threshold: the current page says **six hours**, which resolves the 3 h vs 6 h conflict.
  9. **RSPCA flood page.** The current page does not contain the dog-specific lines v0.1 attributed to it ("sturdy leads", "not natural swimmers"). "Dogs using sturdy leads" and "take food, water, bowls and bedding" are in a **Blue Cross** leaflet.
  10. **Landline battery backup and the PSTN date** are now verified on GOV.UK (DSIT/DCMS): at least one hour, free, for customers who rely solely on their landline; Openreach PSTN migration by **31 January 2027**.
  11. **Escape-ladder standards.** EN 14043/14044 cover **fire-service turntable ladders**, not domestic escape ladders. The only residential escape-ladder standard found is the **US ASTM F2175-23**. No UK or EN standard was found.
- **v0.1 (16/11/2026).** First draft, written from web-search summaries only (official sites were blocked).

### Confidence and caveats

- **Definitive (official, verified):** get out, stay out, call 999; never go back for pets, tell firefighters; safe room with the door closed, gap blocked and window open; give the full address, the building type and which room anyone is trapped in; ground or first floor only: use bedding to cushion the fall, lower yourself, don't jump; higher up, wait for rescue; smoke alarm on every level, on the ceiling on halls and landings; heat alarm in the kitchen; interlinking recommended "where possible".
- **Variation between services:** Warwickshire says the first floor is "only … a last resort – when you are in immediate danger". Devon & Somerset only describes window escape for the ground floor. The others allow ground or first floor.
- **Not found:** any UK official endorsement, standard or load rating for rope or portable escape ladders; any official advice on taking a dog down a ladder. The 200 kg, 450 kg and 150 kg figures are retailer or general-ladder figures, and the 1,000 lb figure is unverified US advice.

### Implications for the household plan (my interpretation)

- Put the official sequence first: close the bedroom door, block the gap with bedding, call 999 (say "two-storey house", which room, that dogs are inside, and that you may use a window ladder), open the window and be visible.
- The ladder is a **last resort**. Official first-floor advice (bedding thrown out to cushion the fall, hang by the arms, then drop) is a recognised alternative and should be written into the plan in case the ladder fails. Because one FRS treats first-floor exits as a last resort in immediate danger only, the plan should say "use the window only if the door route is blocked and staying is no longer safe".
- Check the ladder's own rated load, length and window/sill fit. No guidance covers carrying a dog. Assume the rating is for one person at a time and decide in advance that dogs go first or are left for firefighters, never that someone goes back in. Practise only at ground level.
- A light, soft "chuck bag" thrown out with the bedding fits the official "throw bedding out" advice; keep it clear of the landing spot.
- Bedside: charged phone (charged before bed, not left charging all night), torch, window and door keys, glasses, shoes. Dog leads and harnesses near the escape window.
- Alarms: at least NFCC minimum (smoke alarm on each floor's hall or landing, heat alarm in the kitchen, interlinked). Add alarms in the living room and bedrooms, per NFCC's risk-based recommendation.
- Request a Suffolk FRS visit if eligible. Otherwise use the online home fire safety check and ask the Prevention Team (01473 260588) about the ladder and the dogs.

## 2. Sizewell: off-site emergency arrangements (REPPIR 2019) and radiation advice

### Findings

- ✅ **Who plans.** Suffolk County Council, "Sizewell B" – https://www.suffolk.gov.uk/community-and-safety/emergency-planning/sizewell-b – accessed 16/11/2026: "Suffolk County Council leads the planning for any off-site nuclear emergency at Sizewell B. These arrangements comply with the Radiation Emergency Preparedness and Public Information Regulations 2019 (REPPIR 2019) and are regularly reviewed and tested." REPPIR 2019, reg. 11 – https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2019/703/regulation/11 – accessed 16/11/2026: "the local authority must make an adequate off-site emergency plan covering that zone or zones." Reg. 8 – https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2019/703/regulation/8: "The local authority must determine the detailed emergency planning zone on the basis of the operator's recommendation … and may extend that area".
- ✏️ **Published plan.** SRF "Radiation Emergency Plan", Issue 4.2, Feb 2024 (review three-yearly). The current download link on the SRF radiation page is https://suffolkprepared.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/REDACTED-SRF-Radiation-Emergency-Plan-Issue-4.2.pdf – accessed 16/11/2026. The v0.1 link (…/2024/02/PUBLIC-SRF-Radiation-Emergency-Plan-Issue-4.2-1.pdf) still works. SRF radiation page – https://suffolkprepared.co.uk/get-prepared/risk-advice/radiation/ – accessed 16/11/2026.
- ✅ **DEPZ extent.** The SRF radiation page says: "If you live within the DEPZ, 3 – 4kms from Sizewell B Power Station you will be issued with information". The SRF OPZ leaflet (Oct 2020) – https://suffolkprepared.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/OPZ-leaflet-Oct-20.pdf – accessed 16/11/2026 – says: "the Sizewell DEPZ, an area approximately (3 to 4kms) from the site, which includes Leiston". The official DEPZ map is in the DEPZ leaflet (link "See Sizewell DEPZ map": https://suffolkprepared.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/DEPZ-zcard-web-version-Oct-20.pdf) and in the SRF plan, Appendix I. These are image maps; the boundary cannot be read from the text. The SRF plan's DEPZ summary gives: "Permanent Population 6158 (56 within 1.35kms)", "Private Properties 2750 (25 within 1.35kms)". It also says the DEPZ is split into 22½-degree sectors "that can be published in the form of post code areas". The SCC Sizewell B page does not give a distance for the DEPZ ("This covers the area around the station where detailed emergency plans are in place and residents receive specific prior information").
- ✅ **Operator's minimum DEPZ.** EDF Sizewell B Consequences Report v2, Jan 2020 – https://www.edfenergy.com/sites/default/files/szb_consequences_report_v2_jan_2020.pdf – accessed 16/11/2026: "The Detailed Emergency Planning Zone for the site be no smaller than 1350m from the centre"; OPZ: "Recommended Minimum for the site be set at 30km, as per REPPIR". (The 2.4 km ONR 2012 figure was not rechecked; it is historical.)
- ✅ **1.35 km stable iodine zone.** SCC Sizewell B page: "Residents and businesses within 1.35km of Sizewell B are provided with stable iodine tablets in advance. In a radiation emergency, people in this area may be advised to shelter immediately and take their tablets when instructed."
- ✅ **OPZ.** SCC Sizewell B page: "This wider area extends up to approximately 30km from the station. People in this area may receive information, updates or alerts during an emergency, but protective actions would only be required if advised by the authorities."
- ✅ **15 km Extended Emergency Planning Zone (previously unverified).** OPZ leaflet: "Information for people living in the 30km (18.6 mile) Outline Emergency Planning Zone and the 15km (9.3 mile) Extended Emergency Planning Zone". It also says: "It is unlikely that you will be asked to take immediate actions"; "It should be safe to drink/use fresh tap water and to consume covered food and drink that you already have within your house"; "Prior to any announcement of any food or drink restrictions, you should avoid eating uncovered food, such as fresh vegetables from the garden"; wider evacuation routes would be given "via local TV and radio, or by door to door visits". The SRF plan (§19.1–19.2 and response table) adds: "No immediate shelter action is pre-agreed beyond 1.35km … arrangements are in place to implement additional shelter up to 15kms within 10-12hours" and "arrangements are in place to provide additional tablets up to 15kms within 10-12hours". EEPZ (DEPZ to 15 km): "Permanent Population 25,000"; OPZ (15–30 km): "Permanent Population 132,000".
- ✏️ **How warnings are given – correction.** The SRF radiation page says: "Living within the Sizewell DEPZ – follow the direction given to you via telephone warning. If you do not receive a telephone warning listen to local radio"; "Living outside of the Sizewell DEPZ – listen to local radio and follow any guidance provided." The SRF plan (§3.6) shows how this works in practice:
  - **Automated telephone warning (PETIS):** "Residents within 1.35km (0.8mile) of the site … are asked on an annual basis to register for an automated telephone warning system managed by EDF (PETIS)", to warn "within 1 hour of an OSNE declaration" (off-site nuclear emergency). Recorded message: "Please stay indoors, shut all doors and windows. Take your stable iodine tablets as directed in the Sizewell leaflet. Tune into local radio and television for information." **So the phone warning is for registered people within 1.35 km, not the whole DEPZ.**
  - **Emergency Alert (cell broadcast):** "An Emergency Alert will be used to warn the public in the vicinity of Sz B that there is an imminent risk to life." "The Emergency Alert message will be transmitted to the Detailed Emergency Planning Zone (DEPZ), although the exact footprint is dependent on the position, number and range of mobile masts and the topography." The pre-approved text points to www.suffolk.gov.uk/sizewell. That page returned 404 on 16/11/2026 (it is presumably activated during an incident).
  - **Wider DEPZ and beyond:** "For the public within the wider DEPZ beyond 1.35km, it will reinforce the message that no immediate actions are required, but that families should collect children from schools and childcare facilities that will close as a precaution." For the wider DEPZ, the EEPZ and the OPZ, the Media Coordination Cell will "reinforce the message that no immediate actions are needed by people in these areas, but to stay tuned in to local TV and radio".
  - **Sirens:** the plan mentions only **on-site** warning sirens for site personnel ("Personnel will be warned of any radiation emergency by the existing on-site warning sirens"). No public off-site siren system is described.
  - **EADT article** (https://www.eadt.co.uk/news/21316644.residents-get-new-nuclear-warning-system/ – accessed 16/11/2026): the "about 1,500 calls a minute" system was being rolled out to "other British Energy sites". This is a **historical news item** from the British Energy era; the current system is PETIS as described above.
- ✅ **Stable iodine – Sizewell specifics.** DEPZ leaflet (published October 2019, web version Oct 2020): tablets "pre-issued to residents within a 1.35km (0.8 mile) area"; "only to be taken when advised to by automated telephone message, or other communications methods"; "A specific information leaflet is provided with the tablets explaining the dose to be taken. Only one dose should be necessary and the tablets can be taken by any age"; "The only people who should not take these tablets are those who are allergic to iodine and those people who have the very rare conditions of Hypocomplementaemic Vasculitis or Dermatitis Herpetiformis"; very young babies (under 1 month) and women more than six months pregnant "should take the tablets as directed in the leaflet". "Additional stocks of these tablets are available to be issued following an emergency." The SRF plan notes that "each pack holds 10 tablets or 5 adult doses" and that "To maximise effectiveness, the tablets must be administered promptly, ideally within 6 hours of exposure."

### Confidence and caveats

- **Definitive:** official advice of 3 L per person per day for drinking, for 3 days, with more for cooking, washing and pets; ESW free delivery to PSR customers unable to collect when the outage is expected to last more than six hours; PSR customers get alternative supplies arranged.
- **Unclear:** which company supplies the household (Suffolk is split between Anglian Water and Essex & Suffolk Water; check the bill); Anglian's own wording (site blocked).
- **Not found:** official tap-water storage durations; an official figure for dogs' water needs (the official site says only that more is needed for pets).
- If the household is cut off by flood water, a bottled water station may be unreachable; no source addressed this.

### Implications for the household plan (my interpretation)

- Store at least 3 L per person per day for 3 days for drinking, plus extra for cooking, hygiene and the **dogs**. Because the household can be cut off, plan for more than 3 days. The old 10 L/person/day "comfort" figure is no longer official but is still a reasonable upper planning figure (my suggestion, not source text).
- Note the water company, its incident line and PSR status in the plan. If eligible, register so that bottled water is delivered rather than collected.
- If on a private supply or borehole, a power cut probably means no water, so the store matters more.

### Confidence and caveats

- **Definitive:** never risk life for a pet; act early; don't leave pets behind if evacuating; rest centres may not take pets (assistance animals excepted); grab bag, documents in a watertight bag, up-to-date microchip and ID tag; UKHSA pet advice for radiation sheltering.
- **Partly answered:** Suffolk's evacuation guide tells the local authority to "consider requirements for pet care" at rest centres but makes no public promise to accept pets.
- **Not found:** Dogs Trust emergency guidance; an explicit Suffolk rest-centre pet policy.

### Implications for the household plan (my interpretation)

- Assume a Suffolk rest centre **may not take the dogs**. Arrange a pet buddy or friend outside the flood and Sizewell risk areas, plus a kennel fallback reachable without crossing flood-prone roads. Ring Suffolk County Council or the district council to ask its rest-centre pet policy, as Blue Cross advises.
- Dog grab bag: leads (sturdy), harnesses with handles, 3+ days of food and water, bowls, medication, vaccination record, microchip number and vet details in a watertight pouch, poo bags, towel, a recent photo.
- Check microchip details are current. A collar tag with contact details is a legal requirement for dogs (Blue Cross).
- Put a "2 dogs inside" sign near the door (PDSA), useful for firefighters if you're out.
- Radiation: bring the dogs in **before** going in; don't go out to look for one. Flooding: move the dogs upstairs early and keep them on leads if moving through any water.
