# 3C Leave instantly: house fire cards

Draft v0.1 (content only, not print layout), 16/11/2026. Scenario C: leave instantly. Designed around a fire downstairs at night, with the household asleep upstairs and the stairs possibly unusable.

> **Official UK advice comes first, and the household's ladder plan sits inside it.** In a fire: **get out, stay out, call 999** (UK-GOV-PLAN p.1; FK). **Never go back** for possessions, pets or people; tell the fire service if anyone, or any animal, is still inside (FK; PDSA). If you can't get out: get into a room with a window, close the door, block the gap under it with bedding, call 999, open the window and shout "HELP FIRE" (FK; GOV-FSH). On the ground or first floor you *may* be able to escape through a window: cushion your fall with bedding, lower yourself down carefully, **don't jump** (GOV-FSH). Higher up: don't jump, wait for rescue (FK).
>
> **No UK fire service, NFCC or GOV.UK page recommends domestic escape ladders or sets a standard for them** (GR §1). The ladder is therefore the household's own choice, used only when the stairs are not safe **and** staying in the room is no longer safe. It never delays getting out, and the chuck bag never delays anything.

**Card wording is generic.** "Person 1 / person 2", "dog 1 / dog 2", "escape room", "bag window". Fill in real names, the room, the meeting point and the address wording in the private household copy only.

**Markers** (as in `3A-stay-put-supplies.md` and `3B-leave-with-notice-cards.md`): **[1 source]** = one publisher only; **(not in sources – check)** = added from general knowledge or from the household's own plan; **SUGGESTION** = proposed in this draft, not a source claim; **(retailer claim)** = a seller's statement about its own product; **(unverified)** = marked ❓ in the gap research. Source IDs without a prefix are those in `work/evidence-matrix.md`.

## Card C1: Fire at night – escape sequence (A5)

**Before reading the steps, remember three rules:** get out, stay out, call 999 (UK-GOV-PLAN p.1; FK). Do not stop for possessions or to look for pets (SFRS; SURREY). Once out, never go back in (FK; PDSA).

### Part 1: Alarm goes off

| # | Who | Do this | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Whoever wakes first | Shout "FIRE!" and wake the other person. | DE pp.51–53, step 4 ("warn and protect people") **[1 source]** |
| 2 | Both | Phone, torch and glasses from the bedside (Card C3). Take nothing else. | FK (phone by bed); DSFRS (phone and torch in bedroom); NLAND |
| 3 | Both | Before opening the bedroom door, feel it with the back of your hand. If it is warm, keep it shut and go to Part 3. | (not in sources – check) |
| 4 | Both | Check the stairs. **If the stairs are clear and safe, go to Part 2.** If there is fire or thick smoke on the stairs, go to Part 3. | FK; GR §1 ("normal route first") |

### Part 2: Stairs are safe – get out by the stairs

| # | Who | Do this | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Both | Go straight down and out. If there is smoke, get down low and crawl. | SFRS; DSFRS |
| 6 | Both | Take the dogs only if they are already with you. **Do not stop to look for them.** | SFRS; SURREY; PDSA |
| 7 | Last out | Close doors behind you. Do not lock them. | DE p.53; NOTTS (shut the door behind you) |
| 8 | Both | Go to the meeting point (step 22). Call 999 (see the box below). | UK-GOV-PLAN p.1; FK |

### Part 3: Stairs not safe – escape room

| # | Who | Do this | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | Both | Go into the **escape room** (the agreed bedroom with the ladder). Bring a dog only if it is with you or in the escape room already. | FK; GOV-FSH; SFRS |
| 10 | Both | **Close the door.** | FK; GOV-FSH; STAFFS |
| 11 | Person 2 | Block the gap under the door with bedding or towels. | FK; GOV-FSH; STAFFS; NOTTS |
| 12 | Person 1 | Call **999** now (see the box below). | GOV-FSH ("Call 999 then open the window") |
| 13 | Person 1 | Stay on the call unless told otherwise. Keep the operator updated on where you are. **Do what the fire service tells you.** | NOTTS; UK-GOV 091213 |
| 14 | Person 2 | Open the window. Shout "HELP, FIRE". Stay at the window for fresh air and so firefighters can see you. | GOV-FSH; STAFFS; SURREY; WARKS |
| 15 | Person 2 | If the window will not open: break the glass in the bottom corner; make jagged edges safe with a towel or blanket. | GOV-FSH **[1 source]** |

### Part 4: Window escape – only if staying is no longer safe

**Decide:** stay at the window and wait for the fire service **unless** you are in immediate danger. Window escape is for the **ground or first floor only**, when the route is blocked; Warwickshire FRS says from the first floor "only … as a last resort – when you are in immediate danger" (GOV-FSH; WARKS; NORTHANTS). Above the first floor: don't jump, wait for rescue (FK; NORTHANTS). If the 999 operator tells you to wait, wait.

| # | Who | Do this | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | Person 2 | Throw bedding or cushions out of the escape window, onto the ground below, to break a fall. | DSFRS; WARKS; SURREY; NORTHANTS; GOV-FSH ("use bedding to cushion your fall") |
| 17 | Person 1 | Fix and drop the ladder out of the escape window as practised. | (not in sources – household plan; no official ladder advice, GR §1) |
| 18 | Person 1 + dog 1 | Person 1 goes down first, with dog 1 (harness or as agreed, see Questions 2–3). **One adult plus one dog on the ladder at a time.** At the bottom, move clear of the landing spot. | (not in sources – household plan; no source covers taking a dog down a ladder, GR §1) |
| 19 | Person 2 | While person 1 is on the ladder, and **only if it takes seconds**: throw the chuck bag from the bag window, away from the ladder and the landing spot. If it is not to hand, leave it. If the bag window is in another room, **do not open the escape-room door to reach it.** | Chuck bag: (not in sources – household plan). Throwing items out: DSFRS; WARKS; SURREY. Never delay for possessions: FK; SURREY. Keep the bag clear of the landing spot: GR §1 (interpretation) |
| 20 | Person 2 + dog 2 | Person 2 goes down with dog 2. If a dog cannot be got down safely, leave it in the escape room with the door shut. **Do not delay your own escape for a dog.** | PDSA (never go back for a pet; tell firefighters); FK |
| 21 | Anyone | **If the ladder fails or cannot be used:** from the ground or first floor only, lower yourself from the window ledge by your arms to arm's length, then drop onto the bedding. Don't jump. | WARKS; SURREY; NORTHANTS; GOV-FSH |

| # | Who | Do this | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| 22 | Both | Move away from the house to the **meeting point**: ______ (agree in advance). | Meeting point: UK-GOV 091213; DE p.33. Moving away from the house: (not in sources – check) |
| 23 | Both | Count everyone and every dog. | DE p.53 ("If someone is missing, the fire brigade must assume that they are still in the building") |

| Say | Sources |
|---|---|

| Then stay on the line, answer questions, and update them if you move or get out | NOTTS; DE p.68 ("Wait for questions!") |

## Questions for the household

1. **Ladder rating and length.** What is the ladder's rated load? Is it higher than the heaviest adult plus the heaviest dog plus a harness? Is it long enough for the escape window, and does it fit that sill or frame? (Only retailer figures and a US standard exist; GR §1.)
2. **How will each dog get down?** Carried in a harness or sling, lowered on a separate line, or sent down first? Can each dog be carried by the adult going with it, with one hand free? If a dog cannot be got down safely, the plan is to leave it in the closed room and tell firefighters (PDSA). Agree this now so no one hesitates on the night.
3. **Order on the ladder.** Who goes first and with which dog? Should one adult go first alone to steady the ladder from below? (No source covers this.)
4. **Where do the dogs sleep?** If they sleep downstairs, they cannot be part of the window plan. If upstairs, which room?
5. **Which room is the escape room?** It needs a window that opens fully, a phone, and ideally visibility from the road (DSFRS: a safe room "should have a window that opens, and at least one phone"). Is it the first floor? Is it directly above a downstairs window (HG says not to use the ladder there; retailer claim)? Is the ground below clear?
6. **Which window is the bag window?** Is it in the escape room? If it is in another room, the bag can only be thrown before the escape-room door is closed, or not at all.
7. **Meeting point.** Where, outside and away from the house, visible to fire engines? Choose a fallback too.
8. **A second ladder?** Is a second ladder possible for a second bedroom, in case the escape room cannot be reached? Is a ladder even appropriate for this house (for example, if any bedroom is above the first floor, official advice is to wait for rescue: FK)?
9. **Phone by both beds?** Is one phone enough, and which phone calls 999?
10. **Alarm testing:** monthly (GOV.UK) or weekly (Suffolk leaflet)?
11. **Alarm cover:** does the house meet the NFCC minimum? Are the alarms interlinked, so an alarm downstairs is heard upstairs with doors closed?
12. **Doors at night:** will the household close internal doors at night? Can the dogs cope with that?
13. **Should the household ask the local fire and rescue service** about the ladder and taking dogs down it before relying on the plan?
14. **Glass:** is there anything in the escape room that could break the glass if the window will not open, and does the window type (e.g. top-opening or restricted) allow a ladder at all?

## Unverified or unsupported points

**Unverified (❓ in the gap research):**
- Any UK or European standard for domestic or rope escape ladders: none found; absence is not proof that none exists (GR §1). EN 14043/14044 are fire-service turntable ladders and do not apply.
- US fire-service ladder advice (1,000 lb rating, standoffs, practise from a ground or first-floor window, "don't test ladders in your fire drills"): not re-verified (Seattle PDF 404, ESFI blocked). Treat as unconfirmed overseas advice.
- Safelincs FAQ on top-opening windows: not re-verified.
- BS 5839-6 alarm category for existing homes, and England's legal alarm minimum for owner-occupiers: not checked.

**Retailer claims only (not official):** 200 kg approved load, 450 kg tested, 4.5 m maximum height, 15–34 cm frame width, window must open fully, not above another first-floor window (HG); "Load tested to BS EN 131-2: 2010" (SL). BS EN 131 is a general portable-ladder standard and says nothing about escape ladders (GR §1).

**Not in any source (household plan or general knowledge):**
- Using a rope escape ladder at all; one adult plus one dog at a time; who goes first; taking dogs down a ladder.
- The chuck bag itself, throwing it from a second window, and all its packing rules (soft, no glass, weight, label, storage place). The weight figure (2–3 kg) is a SUGGESTION.
- Feeling the door with the back of the hand before opening it.
- Moving away from the house to the meeting point (meeting points are sourced, but in an evacuation context); using a neighbour's phone.
- Spare phone, padded pouch, shoes by the bed, glasses by the bed (glasses are sourced only in take-with-you lists, not for fire).
- Ladder inspection and storage routine; practice intervals; practising with dogs; practising the window fallback; heat alarm test frequency.

**Official advice that varies between sources (state the strictest on the card):**
- Window escape from the first floor: allowed by GOV-FSH, STAFFS, SURREY and NORTHANTS; "only … as a last resort – when you are in immediate danger" (WARKS); Devon & Somerset describes window escape for the ground floor only; Fire Kills says on an upper floor "don't jump – wait for rescue". Card C1 Part 4 follows the strictest reading.
- Smoke alarm testing: at least monthly (UK-GOV; GOV-FSH) versus weekly (SFRS-L).
- Phone by the bed (FK; DSFRS; NLAND) versus never charging it overnight (FSCOT): resolved as "charge before bed, keep within reach".
