# 3A Stay put and cope: supplies checklist

Draft v0.1 (content only, not print layout), 16/11/2026. Scenario A: staying at home and coping (storm, long power cut, loss of gas or water, cut off by flooding, told to shelter in place).

Built from `work/evidence-matrix.md` and the extracts in `work/evidence/`. Source IDs are those in the matrix. Pages and screenshots are as cited in the extracts (screenshots by time stamp, e.g. "091126"; SE as "p.6 (pr. 10–11)"; FR as "p1", translated by the extractor).

## How to use this list

- **P** = number of people in the household. **D** = number of dogs. Fill these in from the household plan.
- **3-day core** = the minimum to hold at all times. Every source that gives a period says at least 3 days (matrix, topic 22).
- **7-day-plus** = the store to build up to. It reflects NO's one week (NO p.2) and the longer outages several sources mention. The household may go further (DE says 10 days, DE p.10, p.34), especially if the home can be cut off by flooding.
- The quantities in the 7-day-plus column are **totals**, not amounts added on top of the 3-day core.
- "pp" = per person; "hh" = per household.

**Storage and rotation.** Bottled water: cool, dark place, until the expiry date on the bottle (DK p.2). Your own tap water: sealed and cool, "several months" (DK p.2) or "for years" in clean containers (NO p.5); this is DECISION 15. Use old water on plants, then wash and refill (DK p.2). Rain or river water is for hygiene only and may be contaminated (NO p.13).

## Safety box (from the sources only)

> **Light**
> - "Torches are safer than candles." (UK-GOV 091134; UK-GOV-PLAN p.8)
> - If candles or other heat sources are used, put them on a solid surface away from flammable materials such as curtains and carpets. (DK p.3)
> - Never leave naked flames, such as candles, unattended. (DE p.47)
> - Put out all candles and alternative heating before you go to sleep. (SE p.6 (pr. 10))
> - When sheltering in a sealed room, avoid using up oxygen with candles. (DE p.64)
>
> **Cooking and barbecues**
> - "Never use outdoor grills etc. indoors or in fully or partially enclosed spaces. There is a risk of smoke and/or carbon monoxide poisoning." (DK p.2)
> - "Do not grill food in the apartment or house – there is a risk of suffocation!" (DE p.17)
> - Check regularly that camping stoves, gas cookers and grills are in good condition. (NO p.8)
>
> **Heating**
> - Use only a gas or paraffin heater "designed for use indoors". (NO p.6)
> - Make sure any fireplace and chimney are approved and in good condition. (NO p.6)
> - Air the room regularly to let in oxygen. (SE p.6 (pr. 10))
>
> **Generators**
> - If you use a generator or battery unit, "make yourself thoroughly familiar with regulations and guidelines for safe use". (NO p.6)
>
> **Not covered by any source:** carbon monoxide alarms, and any UK guidance on generators, camping stoves or heaters. DK is the only source that names carbon monoxide.

**Awaiting verification (not part of the sourced box above):** the gap research reports that generators must be run outdoors only, away from open doors and windows; that fans do not stop carbon monoxide building up; that carbon monoxide is colourless and odourless; that CO alarms should be fitted; and that a generator must never be connected to the house through a socket (gap research section 4 – verification pending).
