## How to use this list

- **P** = number of people in the household. **D** = number of dogs. **A** = number of adults. Fill these in from the household plan.
- **3-day core** = the minimum to hold at all times. Every source that gives a period says at least 3 days (matrix, topic 22). GOV.UK's "Be ready" site says the same: "Being ready for 3 days helps you manage while support reaches those most in need" (GAP §5 ✏️, GOV.UK "Be ready").
- **7-day-plus** = the store to build up to. It reflects NO's one week (NO p.2) and SE26's "at least one week" (SE26 p.6, p.15), and the longer outages several sources mention. The household may go further (DE says 10 days, DE p.10, p.34), especially if the home can be cut off by flooding.
- The quantities in the 7-day-plus column are **totals**, not amounts added on top of the 3-day core.
- "pp" = per person; "hh" = per household.

**Storage and rotation.** Bottled water: cool, dark place, until the expiry date on the bottle (DK p.2). Your own tap water: sealed and cool, "several months" (DK p.2) or "for years" in clean containers (NO p.5); SE26 says check whether it "tastes or smells unusual once or twice a year" and change it when necessary (SE26 p.15). This is DECISION 15; the 6-monthly kit check meets SE26's interval. Use old water on plants, then wash and refill (DK p.2). Rain or river water is for hygiene only and may be contaminated (NO p.13).

**If the mains supply fails.** UKHSA notes there is "typically 2 to 3 days' worth of treated water within the mains system in any given area" (in its radiation guidance, GAP §2 ✅); no water company statement on supply during power cuts was found (GAP §4 ❓). Essex & Suffolk Water: "If we expect the water to be off for longer than six hours, we aim to deliver bottled water for free to our Priority Services customers who are unable to collect an alternative supply" (GAP §5 ✏️, eswater.co.uk; v0.1's "3 hours" was wrong). Anglian Water's wording could not be checked (GAP §5 ❓). Your water company is on your bill.

**In a power cut, eat chilled and frozen food first**, then the cupboard store (NO p.8; DE p.11). Do not refreeze food that has defrosted (DE p.11). Keep fridge and freezer doors closed (GAP §4 ✅, Suffolk Prepared). Food Standards Agency, "Food safety in a power cut" (GAP §4 ✅, FSA on GOV.UK):
- "Your fridge should stay cold for up to 4 hours. The food in your freezer should stay frozen for up to 48 hours in a full freezer (or 24 hours if it's half full), but these are estimates."
- "If the power outage is for less than 4 hours this is unlikely to impact the safety of food in chilled or frozen storage."
- If the fridge has reached 8°C or above, food that needs refrigeration should be eaten within 4 hours or thrown away after 4 hours, unless the power comes back within 4 hours.
- In cold weather (below 8°C) food can be stored outside in a sealed box.

## Safety box (from the core sources)

> **Light**
> - "Torches are safer than candles." (UK-GOV 091134; UK-GOV-PLAN p.8)
> - If candles or other heat sources are used, put them on a solid surface away from flammable materials such as curtains and carpets. (DK p.3)
> - Never leave naked flames, such as candles, unattended. (DE p.47)
> - "make sure to switch off heat sources before going to sleep." (SE26 p.16)
> - When sheltering in a sealed room, avoid using up oxygen with candles. (DE p.64)
>
> **Cooking and barbecues**
> - "Never use outdoor grills etc. indoors or in fully or partially enclosed spaces. There is a risk of smoke and/or carbon monoxide poisoning." (DK p.2)
> - "Do not grill food in the apartment or house – there is a risk of suffocation!" (DE p.17)
> - Check regularly that camping stoves, gas cookers and grills are in good condition. (NO p.8)
>
> **Heating**
> - Use only a gas or paraffin heater "designed for use indoors". (NO p.6)
> - Make sure any fireplace and chimney are approved and in good condition. (NO p.6)
> - "Open a window to let in breathable air" when using alternative heat sources. (SE26 p.16)
>
> **Generators**
> - If you use a generator or battery unit, "make yourself thoroughly familiar with regulations and guidelines for safe use". (NO p.6)
>
> **Not covered by any core source:** carbon monoxide alarms, and any UK guidance on generators, camping stoves or heaters. DK is the only core source that names carbon monoxide.

## Safety box (from the gap research, v0.2)

> **Generators and carbon monoxide** (GAP §4 ✅, HSENI, the Northern Ireland health and safety regulator; no GB page was found)
> - "Do not use a generator indoors or in partially enclosed spaces- including homes, garages, and crawl spaces."
> - "Do not operate near open doors and windows. Using fans will not prevent carbon monoxide build-up in the home."
> - "Carbon monoxide from generators is completely colourless and odourless ... It could kill your family and your pets."
> - "Install battery-powered carbon monoxide alarms inside your home."
>
> **Camping stoves and barbecues** (GAP §4 ✅, FSA): "most are designed for outdoor use only".
>
> **Still unverified (GAP §4 ❓):** never connect a generator to the house wiring through a socket. This comes only from overseas sources; ask a qualified electrician about a proper changeover switch.
