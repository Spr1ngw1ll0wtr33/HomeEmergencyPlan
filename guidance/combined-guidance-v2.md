# Combined emergency guidance

Version 0.1, 23/11/2026

This is a combined summary of the household emergency guidance from the UK (UK Government, British Red Cross, Ready Scotland), Denmark, Norway, Sweden (2026 edition), Finland, Germany and France. Where the guides agree, the advice is given once. Where they disagree, the main text gives the common ground and the disagreement is listed in **Conflicts in the guidance** at the end.

Quantities are for **7 days**. Where a guide gives figures for a different period, they have been scaled to 7 days.

---

## Getting ready (applies to every scenario)

### Know your risks
- Find out what is likely where you live: flooding, storms, power cuts, extreme weather and wildfire.
- Prepare for the risks that apply to you. Being prepared lets help go first to those who need it most.

### Staying informed
- Keep a radio that works without mains power (battery, wind-up or solar) with spare batteries. A car radio is a fallback.
- Know which national and local stations carry emergency news, and write down their frequencies.
- Emergency alerts may be sent to mobile phones in the area. Keep phones charged and alerts switched on.
- Follow official sources and trusted broadcasters. Be wary of anything that provokes a strong reaction, check it against official sources, and don't pass on rumours. Criminals use emergencies for scams.

### Contacts
- Keep a **paper list** of important numbers (family, neighbours, GP, vet, insurer, utilities), because phones may be flat or networks down. Keep it somewhere easy to find in the dark.
- Add emergency contacts and medical information to your phone (ICE or Medical ID).
- When networks are busy, only call if you need help.

### Cash and payments
- Card payments and cash machines may not work in a power cut or cyber attack.
- Keep **cash at home in mixed small notes and coins**, plus more than one bank card with known PINs.
- Use the cash now and then so it isn't forgotten, and replace it.

### Documents
- Keep **copies** of key documents together in a **waterproof bag or pouch**, ready to take: ID and passports, birth certificates, insurance details, prescriptions and the medication list, medical equipment instructions, and the vet's details and pet ID.
- Keep a digital backup (external drive, USB stick or cloud).
- Keep spare house and car keys.

### Medicines and first aid
- Keep a supply of essential medicines, with a **written list** of medicines and doses.
- Order repeat prescriptions in good time so you never run low.
- Keep a first aid kit: waterproof plasters, bandages, sterile dressings, medical tape, antiseptic, eyewash, a thermometer, tweezers, gloves and painkillers.
- Check expiry dates and throw out old medicines.
- Learn basic first aid, including how to stop severe bleeding.
- Keep spare glasses, and spare batteries for hearing aids.

### Neighbours and community
- Swap contact details with neighbours. Think about a street messaging group.
- Agree who checks on whom, especially anyone living alone or with health needs.
- Check on others once your own household is safe.
- Anyone using powered medical equipment, or with a disability or long-term health condition, can join their electricity and water companies' **Priority Services Register**.

### Your home
- Know how to turn off the **gas, electricity and water**. If the gas has been turned off, don't turn it back on yourself.
- Check your insurance covers your risks, including flooding, and keep the policy details with your documents.
- Photograph your home and contents; it helps with insurance claims.

## Scenario A: Staying at home
*Storm, power cut, loss of water or gas, or being told to stay indoors*

### Water
- Store **at least 3 litres per person per day** for drinking and cooking: **21 litres per person for 7 days**. Add water for pets.
- Use bottled water or clean food-grade containers with lids. Keep it cool and dark.
- Check stored water for taste and smell once or twice a year, and replace it if needed. Bottled water keeps to its date.
- More water is needed for washing and flushing. If a cut is expected, **fill the bath, buckets and containers**.
- If you are unsure whether water is safe, bring it to a rolling boil.
- Water in the hot water tank can be used for washing.
- Rain and river water may be contaminated.

### Food
- **7 days** of long-life food that you normally eat, stored at room temperature. Most of it should be edible cold or need little water or cooking.
- Choose filling, energy-rich food: tins (meat, fish, vegetables, beans, soup), crispbread, pasta, rice, oats, dried fruit, nuts, peanut butter, cooking oil, long-life milk and fruit juice.
- As a guide, per person for 7 days, about:

  | Food | Amount |
  |---|---|
  | Bread, cereals, potatoes, pasta, rice | 2.45 kg |
  | Vegetables and pulses | 2.8 kg |
  | Fruit and nuts | 1.75 kg |
  | Milk and milk products | 1.8 kg |
  | Fish, meat and eggs | 1.05 kg |
  | Fats and oils | 0.25 kg |
  | Daily energy | About 2,200 kcal |

- Build the store up gradually, adding one or two items each time you shop. Rotate it by using and replacing.
- In a power cut, eat chilled food first, then frozen food. Don't refreeze food that has thawed.
- Don't forget a **tin opener**.
- Allow for special diets.

### Cooking without power
- Have a way to cook or boil water without electricity, for example a camping stove or gas burner with spare fuel, and matches or a lighter.
- **Barbecues and grills must never be used indoors**: they give off carbon monoxide.
- Check stoves are in good condition before you need them.

### Keeping warm
- Homes get cold quickly when the heating stops.
- Have **warm clothing** (wool layers, thick socks, hats, gloves, scarves), **blankets, duvets, sleeping bags and sleeping mats**.
- **Gather in one room.** Close the doors, hang blankets over the windows and between rooms, and put rugs on the floor.
- An alternative heater (gas or paraffin, rated for indoor use) can be used to warm a room:
  - stand it on a solid, non-flammable surface away from anything that can burn;
  - open a window to let in fresh air;
  - **switch it off before sleeping**.

  Keep a fire extinguisher nearby.
- A fireplace or stove must be approved and the chimney sound. Keep a store of fuel.
- If the home becomes too cold, arrange to stay with family or friends, or take in neighbours who have no heat.

### Light and power
- **Torches or head torches** with plenty of spare batteries.
- **Candles and tea lights** with matches, if used: on a stable, non-flammable base, away from curtains and anything that can burn, and never left unattended. Torches are safer than candles.
- **Charged power banks** for phones and essential devices, plus an in-car phone charger. Solar chargers help in a long outage.
- A generator or battery power unit can run essential equipment, used with care.

### Communications in a power cut
- Keep phones charged and save battery.
- Mobile networks may fail. Wi-Fi calling can help while broadband is still working.
- Keep the paper contact list to hand.

### Hygiene and toilets without water
- Hand sanitiser, wet wipes, toilet paper, soap, toothpaste, sanitary products, disposable gloves, kitchen roll and rubbish bags. Disposable plates and cutlery save water.
- **If the toilet can't be flushed:**
  - put a strong bag (or two) in the toilet bowl, or use a bucket with a lid or a camping toilet;
  - cover each use with sawdust or compost litter to cut smell;
  - keeping urine and solids apart also reduces odour;
  - tie the bags and store them outside until you're told how to get rid of them.
- Keep food preparation clean and wash hands often.

### Pets
- At least **7 days** of pet food and water, and medicines.
- Also keep bedding, waste bags, leads, harnesses or carriers, and the vet's number written down.
- Keep pets calm and indoors in severe weather.

### Storms and severe weather
- Storms bring power cuts, flooding, fallen trees and travel disruption: prepare for both power cuts and flooding.
- Before a storm, secure loose items outside.
- During a storm, stay indoors, away from windows. Don't travel unless you must. Keep at least 50 m away from fallen power lines.
- In snow and ice, rural homes may be cut off: have extra warm clothing, bedding and battery lighting.
- In a heatwave: extra drinking water, keep medicines cool, and use fans and cooling towels.

### Being told to stay indoors (shelter in place)
*For example, after a chemical, nuclear or other release*
- **Go in, stay in, tune in.**
- Close all windows and doors, switch off ventilation, fans and air conditioning, and seal gaps with tape if advised.
- Go to an inner room, away from windows.
- Listen to the radio and stay indoors until told it is safe.
- After a radiation release, levels fall sharply over the first couple of days.
- **Iodine tablets** are taken **only when the authorities say so**.
- Don't use candles while the room is sealed.

### Coping
- Being prepared reduces stress.
- Anxiety and poor sleep are normal. Keep routines, eat and sleep well, stay in touch with others, and limit how much news you take in.
- Have books, games and puzzles that don't need power.

## Scenario B: Leaving with notice
*About 1–2 hours to leave, for example flood or wildfire*

### Deciding to go
- You may be told to leave by the emergency services, through alerts, or on radio and TV. Follow their instructions.
- Leave in good time, because roads and transport may be slow.
- If you're told to leave, go. Return only when told it is safe.

### What to take
Have as much as possible **packed or listed in advance**, kept easy to reach. Per person:
- **Water and food** for a few days, a tin opener and cutlery;
- **medicines**, the medication list, the first aid kit, glasses or contact lenses, and hearing or mobility aids;
- **documents** in the waterproof pouch, with **ID, bank cards and cash**;
- the **phone, charger and power bank**;
- a **radio**, a **torch** and spare batteries;
- **warm and waterproof clothing**, with spare sets and stout footwear;
- a **sleeping bag or blanket** and **toiletries**;
- **spare house and car keys**;
- a **paper map** (and compass);
- the **paper list of phone numbers** and insurance details;
- **pet supplies** (see below);
- a **whistle**, and something to pass the time.

Use a rucksack so your hands are free.

### Shutting up the house
- **Turn off gas, electricity and water** before leaving if you have time, or if told to.
- **For flooding**, move valuables, documents and electrical items upstairs, and secure anything that could float or leak, such as an oil tank.
- Close and lock windows and doors.

### Where to go and meeting up
- Decide in advance where you would go: family or friends outside the risk area.
- Agree a **meeting point** for the household, in case you are separated or can't get home. It should be easy to find, sheltered and accessible to everyone.
- Agree how you will contact each other if networks fail.

### Pets
- Food and water for several days, medicines, a lead, a harness or carrier/cage, bedding, waste bags, and the vet's number, pet ID and insurance details.
- Plan in advance how pets will travel and where they can stay.

### Flooding
- Check your flood risk and sign up for warnings. Keep the Floodline number to hand.
- **Before:** sandbags or boards, valuables upstairs, documents in waterproof bags, medicines easy to reach.
- **During:** don't stay in a cellar; switch off the power; **never walk or drive through flood water**; keep out of underground garages.
- **After:** photograph the damage for insurance, dry rooms out, have gas and electrics checked before use, and don't eat food that has been in flood water.

### The car
- Keep the fuel tank topped up (or an electric car charged).
- Keep in the car:
  - a first aid kit, torch, phone charger and radio;
  - warm clothing and blankets;
  - water and snacks;
  - high-visibility clothing;
  - jump leads;
  - an ice scraper and de-icer, and a snow shovel;
  - a paper map;
  - a fire extinguisher.

## Scenario C: House fire
*Leaving instantly*

### Preventing fire
- Keep candles, heaters and stoves on a stable, non-flammable surface, well away from curtains, furniture and anything else that can burn. Never leave them unattended.
- Switch heaters off before sleeping.
- Barbecues and camping stoves are not for indoor use.
- Don't use old or damaged extension leads.
- Have chimneys and stoves checked.

### Alarms and equipment
- Fit **smoke alarms** and **test them at least once a month**.
- Keep a **fire extinguisher** and know how to use it. Have it serviced.
- Never put water on a fat fire.

### Escape plan
- Work out the **best escape route** from your home, and a second way out. Make sure everyone in the house knows it.
- Keep escape routes clear. Internal doors can be closed but should not be locked.
- Agree a **meeting place outside**.

### If fire breaks out
- **Get out, stay out, call 999.**
- Close doors behind you to slow the fire.
- Don't go back in for anything.
- Only tackle a small fire yourself if it is safe to do so.

## Keeping things up to date
- **Every month:** test smoke alarms.
- **Every 6 months:** check the kit:
  - food, water, medicine and first aid dates;
  - batteries and power banks charged.

  Replace anything used or out of date.
- **Stored water:** check once or twice a year.
- **The plan:** review it whenever circumstances change (moving home, new medicines, health or care changes) and go through it together. Keep a printed copy where everyone knows to find it.
- Build the store up gradually rather than all at once.

## Conflicts in the guidance

| # | Topic | The conflict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **How long to prepare for** | 3 days (UK, Denmark, Finland, France); at least a week (Norway, Sweden); 10 days (Germany). This document uses 7 days. |
| 2 | **Drinking water** | 2 litres per person per day (Finland, Germany, France) or 3 litres (UK, Denmark, Sweden; Red Cross 2.5–3 L). Some count drinking only, others drinking and cooking. No guide gives a figure for washing and flushing. |
| 3 | **Cooking** | The UK, Finnish and French guides say store food that needs no cooking and give no cooking advice. Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Germany say have a camping stove or gas burner. |
| 4 | **Candles** | The UK says torches are safer than candles. All the European guides include candles, with safety rules. |
| 5 | **Alternative heaters** | The UK, Finnish and French guides say blankets and warm clothing only. Norway, Sweden and Germany include a gas or paraffin heater, with ventilation and switching it off before sleep. |
| 6 | **How much medicine to keep** | "Several days" (UK); 3 days (Denmark); 7 days extra (Norway); a month's supply (Sweden). |
| 7 | **Cash** | "Enough for your family's needs" or "a small amount" in most guides. Only Sweden gives a figure: about 1,000 Swedish kronor per adult. |
| 8 | **Grab bag** | A pre-packed bag per person (Germany, France) or a prepared list of things to take (Norway, Sweden). Water in it: a bottle each (Germany), a few days' worth (Sweden), 6 litres per person (France). |
| 9 | **Documents** | Copies only (UK, Red Cross, France) or some originals in a folder, with duplicates held by relatives (Germany). |
| 10 | **Meeting point** | Close to home (Ready Scotland) or a public place or relative's home further away (UK Government). |
| 11 | **Radio** | FM (UK, Denmark), DAB (Norway), or both (Ready Scotland, Germany). |
| 12 | **Iodine tablets** | Keep them at home for under-40s and pregnant or breastfeeding women (Denmark, Norway, Finland), or not mentioned (UK, Sweden, Germany, France). |
| 13 | **Blankets and toiletries** | Core items (UK Government) or "not essential" (one Red Cross list, which recommends them elsewhere). |
| 14 | **How long stored water keeps** | "Several months" (Denmark), "for years" (Norway), check once or twice a year (Sweden). |
| 15 | **How often to check the kit** | Every 6 months (UK) or once a year (France); others say "regularly". |
| 16 | **Smoke alarms in the kitchen** | Germany says no smoke alarm in the kitchen; the UK sources don't cover kitchens. |
| 17 | **Utilities when leaving** | The UK guides say turn off gas, electricity and water; Germany covers electricity only; the Nordic and French guides don't mention it. |
